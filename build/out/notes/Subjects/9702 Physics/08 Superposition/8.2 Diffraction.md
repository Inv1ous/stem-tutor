# 8.2 Diffraction
> [!abstract] In one breath
> Diffraction is the spreading of waves as they pass through an aperture or around the edge of an obstacle. The spreading is most obvious when the aperture width is comparable with the wavelength.

## Key ideas
A plane wave approaching a barrier travels straight until it reaches an opening. After the opening, the wavefronts spread into the region that would otherwise be in the barrier's shadow. This is **diffraction**. The same effect occurs around a single edge. It is a behaviour of waves, including water waves, sound and light.

The comparison that matters is the wavelength $\lambda$ relative to the gap width $w$. When $w$ is of the same order as $\lambda$, the emerging wavefronts can be strongly curved and the wave spreads through a large angle. When $w$ is much greater than $\lambda$, most of each wavefront continues almost straight, with noticeable bending only near the edges. A narrower gap, at unchanged wavelength, gives a larger spread. Diffraction can occur at other widths too; “comparable” describes when it is particularly easy to observe.

A barrier does not change the medium. If the water depth stays the same, the wave speed stays approximately constant. The source sets the frequency, which also remains constant across the gap. Thus $v=f\lambda$ tells us that the wavelength before and after the gap is unchanged. Changing the gap width changes the shape and direction of the wavefronts, not the spacing between successive crests.

## Method
1. In a ripple tank, use a vibrating straight bar to make approximately plane water waves.
2. Put a barrier with one gap across their path. Observe the wavefronts before and after the barrier, ideally from above with a lamp or projected image.
3. Compare the gap width with the measured spacing of successive crests, the wavelength. Adjust the gap until its width is comparable with the wavelength and look for curved wavefronts beyond it.
4. Widen the gap while keeping the water depth and vibrator frequency fixed. The spreading angle decreases. Alternatively, lower the frequency at constant depth. Since $v=f\lambda$, the wavelength rises and spreading increases through a fixed gap.

> [!example]- Worked example
> Sound of frequency $440\ \mathrm{Hz}$ travels at $330\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ towards a doorway $0.80\ \mathrm{m}$ wide. Predict whether diffraction is pronounced.
> Use $v=f\lambda$, then $\lambda=v/f$.
> Substitute: $\lambda=330/440=0.75\ \mathrm{m}$.
> This wavelength is close to the $0.80\ \mathrm{m}$ gap width, so the sound spreads appreciably through the doorway.

## Traps
- **Trap:** A wider gap gives a wider spread. **Why it's wrong:** A wider gap is larger relative to the same wavelength. **Instead:** Compare $w$ with $\lambda$; widening the gap reduces spreading.
- **Trap:** Passing through a gap changes the wavelength. **Why it's wrong:** The medium and frequency have not changed. **Instead:** Draw the outgoing crests at the same spacing, although they curve.
- **Trap:** Increasing wave amplitude increases the diffraction angle. **Why it's wrong:** Amplitude describes wave size, not its wavelength-to-gap ratio. **Instead:** Change wavelength or gap width to change the spread.

## Exam technique
For **Define**, say “spreading of waves” and name a gap or edge. For a ripple-tank **Describe** question, give the source, barrier and gap, then state what is observed beyond the gap. For **Explain** or **Predict** questions, compare wavelength with gap width and mention any constant depth or speed before using $v=f\lambda$. Never claim the gap changes the wavelength by itself.
