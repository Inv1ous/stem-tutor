# 7.5 Polarisation
> [!abstract] In one breath
> Polarisation restricts the vibrations of a transverse wave to one plane. A polarising filter transmits the component of plane-polarised electromagnetic radiation parallel to its transmission axis, so intensity follows Malus’s law.

## Key ideas
A transverse wave vibrates perpendicular to its direction of travel. Those vibrations can point in different directions in the plane perpendicular to travel. **Polarisation** is the restriction of those vibrations to one plane. A plane-polarised electromagnetic wave has its electric-field vibration in just one plane containing the direction of propagation. Light, radio waves and microwaves can be polarised because electromagnetic waves are transverse. Ordinary sound in air is longitudinal: its vibrations are parallel to the travel direction, so there is no transverse vibration plane for a filter to select. Diffraction, interference and reflection do not make the same distinction; both transverse and longitudinal waves can show them.

A polarising filter has a **transmission axis**. If plane-polarised radiation of intensity $I_0$ reaches it, the transmitted intensity $I$ is

$$
I=I_0\cos^2\theta,
$$

where $\theta$ is the angle between the incident plane of polarisation and the filter's transmission axis. At $0°$, ideal transmission is unchanged; at $90°$, it is zero. The radiation that emerges is polarised along the filter's axis. This law applies to an incident **plane-polarised** electromagnetic wave. You are not required to calculate the effect of a filter on an unpolarised wave in this syllabus outcome.

Intensity is proportional to the square of wave amplitude. Hence the amplitude ratio through one filter is $|\cos\theta|$, while the intensity ratio is $\cos^2\theta$. If an exam asks for CRO trace amplitude or wave amplitude, take the square root of the intensity ratio, or use the cosine directly.

## Method
1. Mark the initial polarisation direction and each filter's transmission axis. A quick sketch of their angles can prevent a reference-angle error.
2. For the first filter, find the angle between initial polarisation and its axis. Substitute into $I_1=I_0\cos^2\theta_1$.
3. For every later filter, use the angle between **adjacent filter axes**. The preceding filter has reset the wave's polarisation direction. Apply Malus’s law again to the preceding output intensity.
4. Give the final result with its unit and appropriate significant figures. If the question asks for amplitude, use the amplitude ratio rather than the intensity ratio.

> [!example]- Worked example
> Vertically polarised light has intensity $8.0\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$. It passes filters whose axes are $50°$ and then $20°$ to vertical. Calculate the final intensity.
> The first angle is $50°$:
> $$
> I_1=8.0\cos^250°=3.31\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}.
> $$
> The second angle is $|50°-20°|=30°$:
> $$
> I_2=3.31\cos^230°=2.48\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}\approx2.5\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}.
> $$
> Using $20°$ in the second calculation would measure from the original vertical direction rather than the wave leaving the first filter.

## Traps
- **Trap:** Treating sound as polarisable because it is a wave. **Why it's wrong:** sound in air is longitudinal. **Instead:** check whether vibration is transverse to propagation.
- **Trap:** Measuring every filter angle from the original polarisation. **Why it's wrong:** the plane of polarisation changes at each filter. **Instead:** compare consecutive axes.
- **Trap:** Giving $\cos^2\theta$ as the amplitude ratio. **Why it's wrong:** intensity is proportional to amplitude squared. **Instead:** use $|\cos\theta|$ for amplitude.

## Exam technique
For **State** or **Explain** questions, say explicitly that polarisation is associated with transverse waves and that longitudinal sound in air cannot be polarised. For **Calculate**, write Malus’s law, show the relevant angle at each filter and substitute in stages. The second filter angle is often the discriminating mark. Keep full calculator precision until the final answer. If the incident light is already plane-polarised, do not introduce an unpolarised-light halving step.
