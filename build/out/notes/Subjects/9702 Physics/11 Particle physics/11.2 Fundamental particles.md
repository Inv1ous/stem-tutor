# 11.2 Fundamental particles
> [!abstract] In one breath
> Quarks and leptons are fundamental particles. Protons and neutrons are composite hadrons, and their quark charges explain both their total charge and the change in beta decay.

## Key ideas
A **fundamental particle** cannot be broken down into anything smaller. A quark is fundamental. Its six flavours (types) are **up, down, strange, charm, top and bottom**. A meson is a particle class, not a quark flavour. You need to know the electrical charge of each flavour, but no other quark properties are required here.

Up, charm and top each have charge $+2e/3$. Down, strange and bottom each have charge $-e/3$. The matching **antiquark** has the same charge magnitude and the opposite sign: for example, an antiup quark has $-2e/3$, while an antidown quark has $+e/3$. Here $e$ is the elementary charge. Add constituent charges algebraically to obtain the charge of a composite particle.

A proton is $uud$: two up quarks and one down quark. Its charge is $+2e/3+2e/3-e/3=+e$. A neutron is $udd$: one up quark and two down quarks. Its charge is $+2e/3-e/3-e/3=0$. Neither a proton nor a neutron is fundamental, because each contains three quarks.

A **hadron** is composed of quarks. A **baryon** is a hadron consisting of three quarks, so protons and neutrons are baryons. A **meson** is a hadron consisting of one quark and one antiquark. An electron or neutrino is a fundamental particle called a **lepton**; neither is a hadron. A positron and an antineutrino are also leptons.

## Method
1. Identify each quark flavour. Group up, charm and top as $+2e/3$, and down, strange and bottom as $-e/3$.
2. Reverse the sign for any antiquark. Keep all values in units of $e$ until the end.
3. Add the fractional charges. Use the result and the number and type of constituents to check your classification.
4. For beta decay, write the starting and ending nucleon compositions before stating which single quark changes. In $\beta^-$ decay, $udd\to uud$: **down changes to up**, and an electron and electron antineutrino are emitted. In $\beta^+$ decay, $uud\to udd$: **up changes to down**, and a positron and electron neutrino are emitted. The change in nucleon charge is respectively $+e$ or $-e$.

> [!example]- Worked example
> Calculate the charge of a baryon containing an up, a down and a strange quark.
> Assign $+2e/3$ to up and $-e/3$ to both down and strange.
> $$Q=+\frac{2e}{3}-\frac{e}{3}-\frac{e}{3}=0$$
> The particle is neutral, but is still a baryon because it has three quarks.

## Traps
- **Trap:** Treat all quarks as $+e/3$. **Why it's wrong:** Quark flavours fall into two charge groups with different signs and magnitudes. **Instead:** Write each charge before adding.
- **Trap:** Call a meson a quark flavour. **Why it's wrong:** A meson is a composite hadron. **Instead:** List the six flavours separately from the hadron classes.
- **Trap:** Reverse the beta-decay quark change. **Why it's wrong:** $\beta^-$ raises nucleon charge, while $\beta^+$ lowers it. **Instead:** Start with $udd$ or $uud$ and check the charge change.

## Exam technique
For **State** or **Describe**, give the complete composition: a proton is $uud$, a neutron is $udd$, a baryon has three quarks, and a meson has one quark plus one antiquark. For **Calculate**, show the signed thirds in the charge sum. For beta decay, name both the quark that changes and what it becomes; merely naming a final nucleon can miss the requested change.
