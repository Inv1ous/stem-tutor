# 2.1 Equations of motion
> [!abstract] In one breath
> Motion is described by displacement, velocity and acceleration, and graphs turn them into gradients and areas. When the acceleration is uniform, four equations link $s,u,v,a,t$; a projectile is just two independent motions, constant velocity sideways and uniform acceleration downwards.

## Key ideas

**Distance** is the total length of the path (scalar). **Displacement** is the straight-line distance from start to finish in a stated direction (vector). **Speed** is the rate of change of distance; **velocity** is the rate of change of displacement; **acceleration** is the rate of change of velocity.

$$v=\frac{\Delta s}{\Delta t},\qquad a=\frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}$$

**Graphs.** On a displacement-time graph the gradient is the velocity. On a velocity-time graph the gradient is the acceleration and the area under the graph is the displacement (area below the axis is negative). For a curve, the gradient at an instant is the gradient of the tangent.

![[Assets/9702/9702-2.1-vt-graph.svg]]

![[Assets/9702/9702-2.1-st-graph.svg]]

**Equations of motion.** For uniform (constant) acceleration in a straight line, starting from $a=(v-u)/t$ and the average velocity $\tfrac12(u+v)$ (the trapezium under the graph):

$$v=u+at,\qquad s=\tfrac12(u+v)t,\qquad s=ut+\tfrac12at^2,\qquad v^2=u^2+2as$$

Eliminating $v$ from the first two gives the third; eliminating $t$ gives the fourth.

**Free fall.** With air resistance negligible every object falls with $a=g=9.81\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$ downwards. To measure $g$, release a steel ball from rest from several measured heights $h$, time each fall electronically, and plot $h$ against $t^2$: since $h=\tfrac12gt^2$ the gradient is $g/2$.

**Projectiles.** The horizontal and vertical motions are independent: horizontally constant velocity $v\cos\theta$, vertically uniform acceleration $g$ downwards. Together they give a parabolic path.

![[Assets/9702/9702-2.1-projectile.svg]]

## Method

1. **Sketch** the motion and choose one direction as positive; write signs on $u$, $a$ and $s$.
2. **List** $s, u, v, a, t$: mark the three you know and the one you want.
3. **Pick** the equation that contains those four and not the fifth quantity.
4. **Substitute** with units, solve (a quadratic gives two roots: reject the unphysical one), and check the sign and size.
5. **Projectile:** resolve the launch velocity, apply suvat to each direction separately and join them only through the time.

> [!example]- Worked example
> A stone is thrown vertically upwards at $12\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. Find the maximum height and the time to return to the ground ($g=9.81\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$).
>
> 1. Upwards positive: $u=+12\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, $a=-9.81\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$, and $v=0$ at the top.
>    *(Fix the signs first; mixing $+g$ and $-g$ is the classic error.)*
> 2. No time is needed, so use $v^2=u^2+2as$:
> $$0=12^2-2(9.81)s\ \Rightarrow\ s=7.3\ \mathrm{m}$$
>    *(Choose the equation with the knowns and the wanted quantity only.)*
> 3. On return $v=-12\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$:
> $$t=\frac{v-u}{a}=\frac{-12-12}{-9.81}=2.4\ \mathrm{s}$$
>    *(Up and down take equal times, so this is twice the time to the top.)*

## Traps

- **Trap:** Using average speed for average velocity, or subtracting speeds after a reversal. **Why it's wrong:** velocity is a vector, so a lap of a circular track has zero average velocity. **Instead:** use displacement, with signs.
- **Trap:** Thinking constant speed, or zero velocity at the top, means zero acceleration. **Why it's wrong:** acceleration is the rate of change of velocity; the ball at the top still accelerates at $9.81\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$. **Instead:** ask whether the velocity is changing.
- **Trap:** Adding the area below the time axis. **Why it's wrong:** it is displacement in the opposite direction. **Instead:** subtract for displacement, add for distance.
- **Trap:** Counting grid squares or dividing one value by one time for a gradient. **Why it's wrong:** a gradient is change over change, read from the axis scale. **Instead:** draw a large triangle, using labelled values.
- **Trap:** Forgetting to double the time to the top, or using $+9.81$ when up is positive. **Why it's wrong:** the ball takes equal times up and down, and the sign of $a$ must match the chosen positive direction. **Instead:** decide what the question asks, and fix the signs at the start.
- **Trap:** Believing a projectile needs a forward force, or using the launch speed as the vertical component. **Why it's wrong:** only weight (and drag) act, and the vertical component is $v\sin\theta$. **Instead:** resolve, treat each direction alone, and combine with Pythagoras.

## Exam technique

- *Define* velocity and acceleration as rates of change of **displacement** and **velocity**, not distance.
- *Determine* from a graph: show the tangent or the triangle you used and read values from the axes.
- *Show that* needs every algebraic step, for example $s=ut+\tfrac12at^2$ from $s=\tfrac12(u+v)t$ and $v=u+at$.
- *Describe* the free-fall experiment: release the ball from rest with an electromagnet (switching it off starts the timer), stop the timer when the ball opens a trapdoor or passes a light gate, measure $h$ with a metre rule, vary $h$, plot $h$ against $t^2$, and use gradient $=g/2$.
- Give answers to 2 or 3 significant figures with units, and keep $g=9.81\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$ unrounded until the end.
