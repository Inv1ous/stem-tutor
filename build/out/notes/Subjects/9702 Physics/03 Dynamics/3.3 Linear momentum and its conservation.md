# 3.3 Linear momentum and its conservation
> [!abstract] In one breath
> The total momentum of an isolated system is constant, so every collision and explosion can be solved by setting the total momentum after equal to the total momentum before. Kinetic energy has no such guarantee: it is conserved only when the collision is perfectly elastic.

## Key ideas

The **principle of conservation of momentum**: the total momentum of a system is constant, provided that no resultant external force acts on the system. A system with no resultant external force on it is called **isolated** (or closed). The condition is part of the statement and Cambridge expects it; "momentum is always conserved" is not enough.

For two objects moving along one line,

$$
m_1u_1+m_2u_2=m_1v_1+m_2v_2 .
$$

Momentum is a **vector**. Choose one direction as positive, and enter every velocity in the opposite direction as a negative number. A rebound changes the sign of a velocity, not just its size.

In **two dimensions** the principle holds independently along any two perpendicular directions. Resolve the *momentum* of each object, not its velocity: the perpendicular equation is $m_1v_1\sin\theta_1=m_2v_2\sin\theta_2$. Equivalently, the momentum vectors form a closed triangle, as below for a puck striking an identical stationary puck.

![[Assets/9702/9702-3.3-momentum-triangle.svg]]

A **perfectly elastic** collision is one in which total kinetic energy is conserved. For such a collision,

$$
u_1-u_2=v_2-v_1 ,
$$

the **relative speed of approach** equals the **relative speed of separation**. This compares the two objects with each other, and is usually far quicker than writing a quadratic energy equation.

In an **inelastic** collision kinetic energy is not conserved. Momentum still is. The missing kinetic energy becomes internal energy, sound and permanent deformation; the extreme case is a collision in which the objects move off together. Total *energy* is always conserved — only *kinetic* energy falls.

## Method

1. Draw before and after diagrams with masses and velocities labelled, and mark your positive direction.
2. Write total momentum before $=$ total momentum after, using signed velocities. In two dimensions write one equation along the original direction and one perpendicular to it.
3. Solve. If you have two unknown final velocities and the collision is elastic, get the second equation from $u_1-u_2=v_2-v_1$.
4. Only if the question asks about energy, calculate $\tfrac12mv^2$ for every object before and after, and state the difference as the energy transferred from kinetic energy.

> [!example]- Worked example
> A trolley of mass 0.80 kg moving at 3.0 m s$^{-1}$ hits a stationary 1.20 kg trolley on a frictionless track; they move off together. Find their common speed and the loss of kinetic energy.
> Taking the initial direction as positive, no resultant external force acts, so
> $$
> 0.80\times3.0=(0.80+1.20)v, \qquad v=1.2\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}.
> $$
> The mass that moves afterwards is the **sum** of the masses.
> Kinetic energy before $=\tfrac12(0.80)(3.0)^2=3.60$ J; after $=\tfrac12(2.00)(1.2)^2=1.44$ J.
> Loss $=3.60-1.44=2.16$ J, transferred to internal energy, sound and deformation.

## Traps

- **Trap:** Stating the principle as "momentum is always conserved". **Why it's wrong:** an external force, such as a wall or friction, does change a system's momentum. **Instead:** add "provided no resultant external force acts on the system".
- **Trap:** Adding the momenta of two approaching objects as magnitudes. **Why it's wrong:** momentum is a vector, so opposing velocities partly cancel. **Instead:** fix a positive direction and use signed velocities; a head-on pair gives $m_1u_1-m_2u_2$.
- **Trap:** Setting the speed of the struck object equal to the relative speed of approach. **Why it's wrong:** the separation speed is the *difference* of the final velocities, and the first object usually still moves. **Instead:** use $v_2=v_1+(u_1-u_2)$.
- **Trap:** Assuming kinetic energy is conserved because momentum is. **Why it's wrong:** only a perfectly elastic collision conserves kinetic energy. **Instead:** check the wording — "stick together", "embeds itself", "coalesce" all mean inelastic.
- **Trap:** In two dimensions, resolving velocities and omitting the masses. **Why it's wrong:** the conserved quantity is momentum, so each component carries its own mass. **Instead:** multiply by the mass first, then resolve, and draw a momentum triangle rather than a velocity triangle.

## Exam technique

*State* the principle in one sentence with both parts: what is constant, and the condition. For *calculate* and *determine*, quote the conservation equation, show the substitution with signs, then give the answer with its unit and direction — a negative answer means the object moves the other way, and examiners want that said in words. *Show that* questions need every line, including the sign convention. Where a question asks you to *explain* why momentum is conserved while kinetic energy falls, name Newton's third law: the forces on the two objects are equal and opposite and act for the same time, so the changes in momentum cancel.
