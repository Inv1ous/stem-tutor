# 11.4 The reactions of chlorine
> [!abstract] In one breath
> Chlorine reacts with aqueous sodium hydroxide to give two products containing chlorine at different oxidation numbers. In water, it produces $\ce{HOCl}$ and $\ce{ClO-}$, which kill bacteria.

## Key ideas

**Disproportionation** means oxidation and reduction of the same element in the same reaction. Chlorine in $\ce{Cl2}$ has oxidation number 0. In both reactions with sodium hydroxide, some chlorine becomes chloride at -1, so it is reduced. The rest becomes a chlorine oxyanion with a positive oxidation number, so it is oxidised.

With **cold aqueous sodium hydroxide**, the products are sodium chloride, sodium chlorate(I) and water:

$$\ce{Cl2(g) + 2NaOH(aq) -> NaCl(aq) + NaClO(aq) + H2O(l)}$$

Chlorine is -1 in $\ce{Cl-}$ and +1 in $\ce{ClO-}$. The Roman numeral in *chlorate(I)* tells you that chlorine is at +1. Thus one chlorine atom in each $\ce{Cl2}$ molecule is reduced and the other is oxidised.

With **hot aqueous sodium hydroxide**, sodium chlorate(V) replaces sodium chlorate(I):

$$\ce{3Cl2(g) + 6NaOH(aq) -> 5NaCl(aq) + NaClO3(aq) + 3H2O(l)}$$

Here chlorine becomes -1 in chloride or +5 in $\ce{ClO3-}$. The coefficients matter: six chlorine atoms enter; five become chloride and one becomes chlorate(V). The change from 0 to +5 for one atom balances five changes from 0 to -1.

For **water purification**, chlorine reacts with water:

$$\ce{Cl2(g) + H2O(l) <=> HCl(aq) + HOCl(aq)}$$

Hypochlorous acid can dissociate:

$$\ce{HOCl(aq) <=> H+(aq) + ClO-(aq)}$$

Both $\ce{HOCl}$ and $\ce{ClO-}$ are active species that kill bacteria. Chlorine is +1 in each: in neutral $\ce{HOCl}$, hydrogen contributes +1 and oxygen -2; in $\ce{ClO-}$, oxygen contributes -2 and the total charge is -1.

## Method

1. Identify whether the alkali is cold or hot. Write $\ce{NaClO}$ for cold conditions and $\ce{NaClO3}$ for hot conditions.
2. Balance chlorine atoms first, then sodium, oxygen and hydrogen. For hot alkali, the ratio is $3\ce{Cl2}:6\ce{NaOH}:5\ce{NaCl}:1\ce{NaClO3}$.
3. Assign oxidation numbers to chlorine in the reactant and each product. State both the increase and decrease when explaining disproportionation.

> [!example]- Worked example
> **Explain** why chlorine and hot aqueous sodium hydroxide undergo disproportionation.
> $$\ce{3Cl2 + 6NaOH -> 5NaCl + NaClO3 + 3H2O}$$
> Chlorine starts at 0. It decreases to -1 in $\ce{NaCl}$, so chlorine is reduced. It increases to +5 in $\ce{NaClO3}$, so chlorine is oxidised. Oxidation and reduction of chlorine occur in the same reaction.

## Traps

- **Trap:** Giving +5 for chlorine in the cold-alkali product. **Why it's wrong:** Cold alkali forms $\ce{ClO-}$, whose chlorine is +1. **Instead:** Link +5 to hot alkali and $\ce{ClO3-}$.
- **Trap:** Calling the process oxidation alone. **Why it's wrong:** Chloride also forms, so some chlorine is reduced. **Instead:** State both oxidation-number changes.
- **Trap:** Naming $\ce{Cl-}$ as the species that kills bacteria. **Why it's wrong:** The active species are $\ce{HOCl}$ and $\ce{ClO-}$. **Instead:** Write the water equilibrium and the acid dissociation.

## Exam technique

For **describe**, give the conditions, products and balanced equation. For **explain**, name the oxidation-number changes and say why these prove disproportionation. For water purification, give the equation forming $\ce{HOCl}$, its dissociation to $\ce{ClO-}$, and identify both as bacteria-killing species. In calculations with hot alkali, read the coefficients before finding a mass or amount.
