# 2.1 Relative masses of atoms and molecules
> [!abstract] In one breath
> Chemists compare tiny masses with a fixed reference: one twelfth of a carbon-12 atom. Choose the relative mass term by deciding whether you mean an isotope, an element, a molecule, or a formula unit.

## Key ideas
The **unified atomic mass unit**, symbol $u$, is **one twelfth of the mass of one carbon-12 atom**. Therefore one carbon-12 atom has a mass of exactly $12u$. This particular isotope is the reference for every relative mass in this chapter. Relative masses are ratios, so they have no units; do not attach $u$ to an $A_r$ or $M_r$ answer. An actual atom or molecule can have a mass expressed in $u$.

**Relative isotopic mass** is the mass of one atom of a particular isotope relative to one twelfth of the mass of one carbon-12 atom. It refers to a specified isotope, such as chlorine-35. It is not the mass averaged across the element's naturally occurring isotopes.

**Relative atomic mass**, $A_r$, is the **weighted mean mass of an atom of an element** relative to one twelfth of the mass of one carbon-12 atom. The word *weighted* matters: isotopes with higher abundance contribute more. For example, a chlorine $A_r$ near 35.5 does not mean an individual chlorine isotope has mass 35.5 on this scale. Use the $A_r$ values supplied by the Periodic Table in your paper.

**Relative molecular mass**, $M_r$, is the mass of a molecule relative to one twelfth of the mass of one carbon-12 atom. A molecular formula states how many atoms contribute. **Relative formula mass** is the mass of one formula unit relative to the same reference. This is the term for ionic compounds such as $\ce{NaCl}$ or $\ce{CaCO3}$, which form extended lattices instead of discrete molecules.

## Method
1. Identify the entity named in the question: an isotope, an element, a molecule, or an ionic formula unit.
2. For a molecule or formula unit, count each atom from the subscripts. A missing subscript means one.
3. Multiply each element's $A_r$ by its atom count and add. Report the result without a unit.

> [!example]- Worked example
> Calculate the relative formula mass of $\ce{CaCO3}$ using $A_r(\ce{Ca})=40.1$, $A_r(\ce{C})=12.0$ and $A_r(\ce{O})=16.0$.
> There is one Ca atom, one C atom and three O atoms per formula unit.
> $$M_r=40.1+12.0+3(16.0)=100.1$$
> Call this a relative formula mass because calcium carbonate is ionic. The numerical value is unitless.

## Traps
- **Trap:** Calling 35.5 the relative isotopic mass of chlorine-35. **Why it's wrong:** 35.5 is an average for chlorine atoms. **Instead:** Name the isotope when giving an isotopic mass.
- **Trap:** Calling the relative formula mass of $\ce{NaCl}$ its relative molecular mass. **Why it's wrong:** Ionic solids contain formula units, not separate molecules. **Instead:** Match the term to the entity.
- **Trap:** Ignoring the 2 in $\ce{O2}$. **Why it's wrong:** Both atoms contribute to its mass. **Instead:** Use $M_r=2A_r(\ce{O})$.

## Exam technique
For **Define**, include the exact carbon-12 reference, and include *weighted mean* for $A_r$. For **Calculate**, show the atom counts before adding $A_r$ values. Check whether the question asks for the mass of a molecule or a formula unit. A relative mass answer needs no unit, even though an actual mass measured in unified atomic mass units would use $u$.
