# 1.4 Ionisation energy

> [!abstract] In one breath
> Ionisation energy is the energy needed to pull electrons off gaseous atoms, and it measures how strongly the nucleus attracts the outer electron. That attraction depends on nuclear charge, distance, shielding and electron pairing, so ionisation energies reveal the shells, the configuration and the group of an element.

## Key ideas

**First ionisation energy** ($IE_1$) is the energy required to remove one electron from each atom in one mole of gaseous atoms to form one mole of gaseous $1+$ ions.

$$\ce{X(g) -> X+(g) + e-}$$

**Successive ionisation energies** remove one electron at a time from the ion left behind. The $n$th ionisation energy is for $\ce{X^{(n-1)+}(g) -> X^{n+}(g) + e-}$, for example the second for magnesium:

$$\ce{Mg+(g) -> Mg^2+(g) + e-}$$

Every species needs a state symbol (g) and only one electron is removed in each equation. Successive values always increase: the nuclear charge is unchanged but there are fewer electrons, so the ion is smaller, there is less repulsion, and the attraction on each remaining electron is greater.

**Cause of ionisation energy:** the electrostatic attraction between the positive nucleus and the negative outer electron. Energy must be supplied to overcome it, so ionisation is endothermic.

**Factors:** nuclear charge (more protons, greater attraction); atomic or ionic radius (further away, weaker attraction); shielding by inner shells and subshells (more shielding, weaker attraction); spin-pair repulsion (two electrons in one orbital repel, so one is removed more easily).

**Trends.** Across a period, $IE_1$ generally increases: more protons, the same shell and similar shielding. Down a group it decreases: the outer electron is further out and there are more inner shells, which outweighs the greater nuclear charge.

![[Assets/9701/9701-1.4-period3-ie1.svg]]

The two dips in Period 3: Al is lower than Mg because its outer electron is in a $3p$ subshell, higher in energy and shielded by $3s$; S is lower than P because $3p^4$ has a pair of electrons whose spin-pair repulsion makes one easier to remove.

**Big jumps in successive values** show a change to an inner shell, closer to the nucleus and less shielded.

## Method

**Equation for the $n$th ionisation energy**
1. Start from the species with charge $(n-1)+$, in state (g).
2. Remove one electron: the charge rises by one, and $e^-$ appears on the right.
3. Check that every species has (g).

**Deducing the outer configuration and group from data**
1. Find the biggest jump between two consecutive values.
2. The number of values before the jump is the number of outer electrons.
3. Use the period to place them in $s$ and $p$ subshells; for Groups 13 to 18, group $=10+{}$outer electrons.

> [!example]- Worked example
> The first seven ionisation energies of an element in Period 2 are 1402, 2856, 4578, 7475, 9445, 53 267 and 64 360 kJ mol$^{-1}$. Deduce its group.
>
> 1. Find the biggest jump: $53267/9445 \approx 5.6$, between the 5th and 6th values.
>    *(The next electron comes from an inner shell.)*
> 2. Five electrons come before the jump, so there are five outer electrons.
>    *(The group is set by the count before the jump, not by the size of the values.)*
> 3. Outer configuration $2s^2\,2p^3$, so the element is in Group 15.
>    *(For Groups 13 to 18 the group is 10 plus the outer electrons.)*

## Traps

- **Trap:** Defining $IE_1$ as removing an electron from one mole of atoms, or from atoms that are not gaseous. **Why it's wrong:** it must be one electron from each atom in one mole of gaseous atoms. **Instead:** include "each atom", "one mole of gaseous atoms" and "$1+$ ions".
- **Trap:** Removing two electrons, or omitting (g), in an equation. **Why it's wrong:** each ionisation removes exactly one electron from a gaseous species. **Instead:** write $\ce{X^{(n-1)+}(g) -> X^{n+}(g) + e-}$.
- **Trap:** Saying $IE$ increases down a group because nuclear charge increases. **Why it's wrong:** shielding and distance increase too and outweigh it. **Instead:** write "more shells, greater shielding, further away, weaker attraction".
- **Trap:** Explaining S below P by "half-full subshells are stable". **Why it's wrong:** the mark scheme wants spin-pair repulsion. **Instead:** say the $3p^4$ pair repel, so one electron is removed more easily.
- **Trap:** Reading the group from where the values are largest. **Why it's wrong:** the group comes from the count before the first big jump. **Instead:** count the values before the jump.

## Exam technique

- *Define* needs the full definition with the state and the amount. *State* asks for one clear equation or fact, with state symbols.
- *Explain* needs a chain: factor, then attraction, then energy. Name both the electron being removed and the reason, using the words "nuclear charge", "shielding" and "attraction".
- *Deduce* from data: quote the values on either side of the jump.
- For "why is $IE_2$ of X larger than that of Y", compare the shells and subshells of the electrons removed, not the atoms.
