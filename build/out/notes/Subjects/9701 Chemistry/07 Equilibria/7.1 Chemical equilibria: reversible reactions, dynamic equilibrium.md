# 7.1 Chemical equilibria
> [!abstract] In one breath
> A reversible reaction proceeds in both directions. In a closed system it can reach dynamic equilibrium: the forward and reverse rates are equal, so reactant and product concentrations remain constant, even while molecules continue reacting.

## Key ideas
A **reversible reaction** has both forward and reverse reactions. A **closed system** prevents reactants or products escaping; without it, a stable dynamic equilibrium cannot be established. Equal rates mean constant concentrations, not necessarily equal concentrations.

**Le Chatelier's principle** states: if a change is made to a system at dynamic equilibrium, the position of equilibrium moves to minimise this change. For a temperature rise, favour the endothermic direction; for a temperature fall, favour the exothermic direction. Adding a reactant usually shifts the position towards products as the added reactant is used. Increasing pressure by reducing volume favours the side with fewer gaseous molecules. If both sides have the same number of gas molecules, pressure does not move the position. Adding an inert gas at constant volume does not change reacting gas partial pressures. A catalyst speeds both directions and leaves the position unchanged.

For $aA+bB\rightleftharpoons cC+dD$, the **equilibrium constant in concentrations** is $K_c=[C]^c[D]^d/([A]^a[B]^b)$. Use equilibrium concentrations, raise each to its stoichiometric coefficient, and omit pure solids and pure liquids. A **mole fraction** is the moles of one component divided by total moles. Its **partial pressure** is $p_i=x_i p_{\mathrm{total}}$. For gases, $K_p=p_C^cp_D^d/(p_A^ap_B^b)$ using equilibrium partial pressures. The relationship between $K_p$ and $K_c$ is not required here.

## Method
1. Write the balanced reversible equation and the appropriate $K_c$ or $K_p$ expression.
2. For equilibrium quantities, make an initial, change, equilibrium table. Apply coefficient ratios to each change, then use the given equilibrium datum.
3. Convert equilibrium moles to concentrations by dividing by volume, or to mole fractions and partial pressures using total pressure. Substitute only equilibrium values into the constant expression.
4. Simplify units from the net powers of concentration or pressure. No quadratic solution is required in this syllabus section.

> [!example]- Worked example
> For $H_2(g)+I_2(g)\rightleftharpoons2HI(g)$, start with 0.20 mol $H_2$ and 0.15 mol $I_2$; there is 0.26 mol $HI$ at equilibrium. Find $K_c$.
> The reaction extent is $0.26/2=0.13$ mol, so equilibrium amounts are 0.07 mol $H_2$, 0.02 mol $I_2$, and 0.26 mol $HI$.
> $$K_c=\frac{[HI]^2}{[H_2][I_2]}=\frac{0.26^2}{0.07(0.02)}=48.3$$
> The volume cancels because there are two gaseous molecules on each side.

## Traps
- **Trap:** Equilibrium means reaction has stopped. **Why it's wrong:** Both directions still occur. **Instead:** State equal forward and reverse rates and constant concentrations.
- **Trap:** Substitute starting moles into $K_p$. **Why it's wrong:** Its terms are equilibrium partial pressures. **Instead:** Find equilibrium moles, total moles, mole fractions, then partial pressures.
- **Trap:** A catalyst or pressure changes $K_c$ or $K_p$. **Why it's wrong:** At fixed temperature, neither changes the constant. **Instead:** Only temperature changes its value.

## Exam technique
For **Deduce**, show the balanced equation before the expression and powers. For **Calculate**, show the equilibrium table or pressure conversion and retain units where the powers demand them. Explain industrial choices as compromises among equilibrium yield, reaction rate and cost. The **Haber process** uses $N_2+3H_2\rightleftharpoons2NH_3$, about 450 °C, about 200 atm and an iron catalyst: lower temperature and higher pressure favour ammonia, but rate and compression cost matter. The **Contact process** uses $2SO_2+O_2\rightleftharpoons2SO_3$, about 450 °C, near atmospheric pressure and a $V_2O_5$ catalyst: high pressure would improve yield but is not economical when yield is already high.
