# 14.1 Alkanes
> [!abstract] In one breath
> Alkanes are saturated hydrocarbons whose strong, relatively non-polar C–H bonds make them generally unreactive, especially towards polar reagents. Heat can crack large hydrocarbons into useful smaller molecules; combustion and UV-initiated halogen substitution are the main reactions to know.

## Key ideas

**Making alkanes.** Hydrogenation adds $\ce{H2(g)}$ across an alkene's C=C bond with a Pt or Ni catalyst and heat. For example, $\ce{C2H4(g) + H2(g) -> C2H6(g)}$. Cracking a longer-chain alkane by heating it with $\ce{Al2O3}$ can also make a shorter alkane. Both atoms and bonds must balance in a proposed cracking equation.

**Combustion.** In enough oxygen, complete combustion gives carbon dioxide and water: $\ce{2C2H6(g) + 7O2(g) -> 4CO2(g) + 6H2O(g)}$. With limited oxygen, incomplete combustion can produce toxic carbon monoxide or solid carbon (soot), as well as water. For example, $\ce{2C2H6(g) + 5O2(g) -> 4CO(g) + 6H2O(g)}$. The carbon monoxide equation uses five oxygen molecules, so check oxygen atoms as well as carbon and hydrogen.

**Halogen substitution.** Ethane reacts with $\ce{Cl2}$ or $\ce{Br2}$ in ultraviolet light. A hydrogen is replaced by halogen: $\ce{C2H6 + Cl2 -> C2H5Cl + HCl}$. Further substitution is possible, so excess halogen can give a mixture. This is free-radical substitution, not an addition reaction.

**Why alkanes are unreactive.** The C–H bonds are strong: breaking them costs considerable energy. They are also relatively non-polar, leaving no strongly charged site for a polar reagent to attack. Ultraviolet light changes the situation for a halogen by splitting its molecule into reactive radicals.

**Crude-oil fractions and exhaust.** Heavy fractions have long molecules. Cracking supplies lower-$M_r$ alkanes, useful as fuels, and alkenes, useful as chemical feedstocks. Engine combustion can release carbon monoxide, oxides of nitrogen and unburnt hydrocarbons. CO is poisonous because it reduces oxygen transport in blood; nitrogen oxides and unburnt hydrocarbons help form photochemical smog. Catalytic converters oxidise CO and hydrocarbons to $\ce{CO2}$ and $\ce{H2O}$ and reduce nitrogen oxides to $\ce{N2}$: $\ce{2CO + 2NO -> 2CO2 + N2}$. This removes pollutants but still makes $\ce{CO2}$.

## Method

1. **Initiation:** UV light causes homolytic fission: $\ce{Cl2 -> 2Cl^{\bullet}}$. Each chlorine atom takes one bonding electron.
2. **Propagation:** $\ce{Cl^{\bullet} + C2H6 -> HCl + C2H5^{\bullet}}$, then $\ce{C2H5^{\bullet} + Cl2 -> C2H5Cl + Cl^{\bullet}}$. A radical is regenerated, so the chain continues.
3. **Termination:** Two radicals join and no radical remains, for example $\ce{C2H5^{\bullet} + Cl^{\bullet} -> C2H5Cl}$ or $\ce{2C2H5^{\bullet} -> C4H10}$.

> [!example]- Worked example
> Suggest products when one molecule of decane cracks into octane and one other molecule.
> Carbon balance leaves two C atoms; hydrogen balance leaves four H atoms. The second product is ethene.
> $$\ce{C10H22 -> C8H18 + C2H4}$$
> Octane is the lower-$M_r$ alkane; ethene is the alkene. Count atoms on both sides before accepting the equation.

## Traps

- **Trap:** Calling radical combination a propagation step. **Why it's wrong:** No radical is regenerated. **Instead:** Label it termination.
- **Trap:** Assuming limited oxygen still gives only $\ce{CO2}$. **Why it's wrong:** Carbon monoxide or soot can form. **Instead:** State the oxygen supply and balance the chosen products.
- **Trap:** Saying a converter prevents all greenhouse-gas release. **Why it's wrong:** Oxidation produces $\ce{CO2}$. **Instead:** Name the pollutant removed and the product formed.

## Exam technique

For **Describe**, give reagents, conditions and products. For **Explain** alkane unreactivity, mention both C–H bond strength and relative lack of polarity. For a mechanism, write complete initiation, propagation and termination equations with radical dots. For **Suggest** a cracking equation, balance C and H separately; a valid product pair is worth more than a named process alone.
