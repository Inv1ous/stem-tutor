# 10.1 Group 2 metals and compounds
> [!abstract] In one breath
> Group 2 metals become more reactive from magnesium to barium. Their nitrates and carbonates become more thermally stable; hydroxides become more soluble, while sulfates become less soluble.

## Key ideas
The Group 2 elements magnesium, calcium, strontium and barium form $M^{2+}$ ions. Their outer electrons are lost more easily down the group because distance and shielding increase. Their reactions with water become faster: magnesium reacts only very slowly with cold water; calcium, strontium and barium react increasingly vigorously. Oxygen produces a white oxide, for example $2Mg(s)+O_2(g)\rightarrow2MgO(s)$. Water gives a hydroxide and hydrogen, for example $Ca(s)+2H_2O(l)\rightarrow Ca(OH)_2(aq)+H_2(g)$. Magnesium reacts with steam more readily than cold water, forming magnesium oxide and hydrogen. With dilute hydrochloric acid, $Mg(s)+2HCl(aq)\rightarrow MgCl_2(aq)+H_2(g)$; dilute sulfuric acid similarly gives the sulfate and hydrogen. An insoluble sulfate coating can slow a reaction.

Group 2 oxides are basic. An oxide plus water forms a hydroxide: $BaO(s)+H_2O(l)\rightarrow Ba(OH)_2(aq)$. White barium oxide disappears into a colourless alkaline solution; there are no gas bubbles. Magnesium oxide and magnesium hydroxide are only slightly soluble. An oxide or hydroxide plus dilute hydrochloric or sulfuric acid gives a salt and water, such as $MgO(s)+H_2SO_4(aq)\rightarrow MgSO_4(aq)+H_2O(l)$ and $Ca(OH)_2(aq)+2HCl(aq)\rightarrow CaCl_2(aq)+2H_2O(l)$. Carbonates do not appreciably react with water alone. With dilute acid they produce salt, water and carbon dioxide: $MgCO_3(s)+2HCl(aq)\rightarrow MgCl_2(aq)+CO_2(g)+H_2O(l)$.

On heating, Group 2 carbonates decompose: $CaCO_3(s)\rightarrow CaO(s)+CO_2(g)$. Nitrates form oxide, brown nitrogen dioxide and oxygen: $2Ca(NO_3)_2(s)\rightarrow2CaO(s)+4NO_2(g)+O_2(g)$. Both nitrate and carbonate thermal stabilities increase down the group. A larger $M^{2+}$ ion has lower charge density and polarises the anion less, so decomposition takes more heat.

The group 2 hydroxide solubility trend is upward from magnesium to barium; the group 2 sulfate solubility trend is downward. Thus $Ba(OH)_2$ readily forms an alkaline solution, whereas $BaSO_4$ is nearly insoluble. These opposite trends predict precipitates and the pH of saturated hydroxide solutions. Melting points of the metals do not follow a simple smooth trend, so use supplied data for precise physical-property predictions. For example, approximate melting points are Mg 650 degrees C, Ca 842 degrees C, Sr 777 degrees C and Ba 727 degrees C. At 700 degrees C, magnesium has melted while calcium, strontium and barium remain solid, assuming the same pressure and no chemical reaction. Compare the temperature with each supplied melting point rather than assuming a smooth trend down the group.

## Method
1. Identify whether the starting material is metal, oxide, hydroxide, carbonate or nitrate.
2. Choose the reaction pattern: metal plus acid gives hydrogen; oxide or hydroxide neutralises acid; carbonate plus acid gives carbon dioxide and water; heat gives the decomposition products above.
3. Balance metal atoms, then the polyatomic ions, then hydrogen and oxygen. Check state symbols and whether a sulfate precipitates.

> [!example]- Worked example
> Predict and balance the products when magnesium carbonate reacts with dilute hydrochloric acid.
> Carbonate plus acid gives salt, carbon dioxide and water. Magnesium needs two chloride ions.
> $$MgCO_3(s)+2HCl(aq)\rightarrow MgCl_2(aq)+CO_2(g)+H_2O(l)$$
> Check: one Mg, one C, three O, two H and two Cl on each side.

## Traps
- **Trap:** Oxide plus water effervesces. **Why it's wrong:** no gaseous product forms. **Instead:** describe hydroxide formation and any dissolving.
- **Trap:** Carbonate plus acid gives only salt and carbon dioxide. **Why it's wrong:** water is also formed. **Instead:** include all three products.
- **Trap:** Nitrate heating yields nitrogen monoxide. **Why it's wrong:** the Group 2 pattern produces nitrogen dioxide and oxygen. **Instead:** balance the $NO_2$ and $O_2$ together.
- **Trap:** All Group 2 salts become more soluble down the group. **Why it's wrong:** sulfates show the reverse of hydroxides. **Instead:** state each trend separately.

## Exam technique
For **Describe**, give both observation and products. For **Predict**, name the trend and then apply it to the two elements. For **Give** an equation, balance it and include state symbols. If asked for a gas volume, first find moles from the balanced equation, then use the stated molar volume.
