# 3.2 Ionic bonding
> [!abstract] In one breath
> Ionic bonding is the electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions: positive cations and negative anions. Sodium chloride, magnesium oxide and calcium fluoride each contain a giant lattice of such ions.

## Key ideas

**Ionic bonding** means the **electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions**. A positively charged ion is a *cation*; a negatively charged ion is an *anion*. This definition describes the force holding ions together. Electron transfer is a way ions may form, but is not itself the ionic bond.

Metal atoms commonly lose electrons, leaving cations. Non-metal atoms commonly gain electrons, forming anions. The resulting positive and negative charges attract. In a solid ionic compound, each ion has oppositely charged neighbours in a repeating, three-dimensional **giant ionic lattice**. A chemical formula gives the simplest whole-number ratio of ions; it does not imply separate molecules.

In **sodium chloride**, sodium loses one electron to form $\ce{Na+}$ and chlorine gains one electron to form $\ce{Cl-}$. The charges balance in a 1:1 ratio, so the formula is $\ce{NaCl}$. The solid contains a giant lattice of $\ce{Na+}$ and $\ce{Cl-}$ ions, with electrostatic attractions extending throughout it.

In **magnesium oxide**, magnesium loses two electrons to form $\ce{Mg^{2+}}$; oxygen gains two to form $\ce{O^{2-}}$. One of each ion balances the charge, giving $\ce{MgO}$. Its giant lattice is held by electrostatic attraction between $\ce{Mg^{2+}}$ and $\ce{O^{2-}}$ ions.

In **calcium fluoride**, calcium forms $\ce{Ca^{2+}}$ and each fluorine atom forms $\ce{F-}$. Two fluoride ions balance one calcium ion, giving $\ce{CaF2}$. The formula is the 1:2 ion ratio throughout a giant ionic lattice, not one isolated calcium ion attached to two fluoride ions.

## Method

1. Identify which atom loses electrons and which gains them. Metals form positive ions; non-metals form negative ions in these examples.
2. Write each ion with its charge. Check the charges against the electron changes: loss of two electrons gives 2+, while gain of one gives 1−.
3. Choose the smallest whole-number numbers of ions whose total charge is zero. Then write the formula and describe the electrostatic attraction throughout the lattice.

> [!example]- Worked example
> **Describe ionic bonding in calcium fluoride.** Calcium loses two electrons, forming $\ce{Ca^{2+}}$. Each fluorine atom gains one electron, forming $\ce{F-}$. Two fluoride ions balance one calcium ion: $+2+2(-1)=0$. The formula is $\ce{CaF2}$. Oppositely charged ions attract electrostatically throughout a giant ionic lattice.

## Traps

- **Trap:** Calling electron transfer the ionic bond. **Why it's wrong:** Transfer creates ions; the bond is their electrostatic attraction. **Instead:** Name the cations, anions and force between them.
- **Trap:** Writing $\ce{CaF}$ because calcium and fluorine are two elements. **Why it's wrong:** $\ce{Ca^{2+}}$ and $\ce{F-}$ do not balance in a 1:1 ratio. **Instead:** Use one calcium ion and two fluoride ions.
- **Trap:** Calling solid $\ce{NaCl}$ a collection of molecules. **Why it's wrong:** The ions extend through a giant lattice. **Instead:** Treat $\ce{NaCl}$ as a formula unit showing the 1:1 ratio.

## Exam technique

For **Define**, state “electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions”, and identify cations and anions if asked. For **Describe**, give the correct ion charges, their ratio and the giant ionic lattice. For **Deduce**, use electron loss or gain to infer ion charge, then balance total charge. A smaller ionic radius than atomic radius is evidence of electron loss; a larger one supports electron gain.
