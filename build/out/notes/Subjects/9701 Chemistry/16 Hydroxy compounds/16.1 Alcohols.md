# 16.1 Alcohols
> [!abstract] In one breath
> The reactions of an alcohol depend on its O–H group and on the carbon that carries it. Count that carbon's neighbours before predicting oxidation, and look for $\ce{CH3CH(OH)-}$ before predicting an iodoform result.

## Key ideas

**Preparing alcohols.** Add steam to an alkene using a $\ce{H3PO4}$ catalyst: ethene gives ethanol by electrophilic addition. Cold, dilute, acidified $\ce{KMnO4}$ adds two OH groups across an alkene double bond to make a diol. Heat a halogenoalkane with aqueous $\ce{NaOH}$ for substitution. Reduce an aldehyde to a primary alcohol or a ketone to a secondary alcohol with $\ce{NaBH4}$ or $\ce{LiAlH4}$. A carboxylic acid needs $\ce{LiAlH4}$ to give a primary alcohol. Heating an ester with dilute acid gives a carboxylic acid and an alcohol; dilute alkali gives a carboxylate ion and an alcohol.

**Classify each OH group separately.** The carbon bearing OH is attached to one carbon in a primary alcohol, two in a secondary alcohol, and three in a tertiary alcohol. Propane-1,2-diol therefore contains one primary and one secondary OH group. Ethanol is primary because its OH carbon is attached to one carbon. Methanol has no carbon neighbour but is usually grouped with primary alcohols for oxidation behaviour.

**Reactions.** Complete combustion produces $\ce{CO2}$ and $\ce{H2O}$, for example $\ce{C2H5OH + 3O2 -> 2CO2 + 3H2O}$. Sodium metal replaces the O–H proton and releases hydrogen: $\ce{2C2H5OH + 2Na -> 2C2H5ONa + H2}$. Replace OH with halogen using $\ce{HX(g)}$, or $\ce{KCl}$ and concentrated $\ce{H2SO4}$ or $\ce{H3PO4}$, or $\ce{PCl3}$ with heat, $\ce{PCl5}$, or $\ce{SOCl2}$. Dehydrate an alcohol to an alkene over heated $\ce{Al2O3}$ or with a concentrated acid. Ethanol and ethanoic acid form ethyl ethanoate and water with concentrated $\ce{H2SO4}$ catalyst.

**Oxidation.** Warm acidified $\ce{K2Cr2O7}$ changes from orange to green when it oxidises a primary or secondary alcohol; acidified $\ce{KMnO4}$ also works. Distil a primary alcohol's aldehyde away promptly. Heat under reflux with excess oxidant to reach the carboxylic acid. A secondary alcohol forms a ketone and resists further oxidation under these conditions. A tertiary alcohol has no hydrogen on its OH-bearing carbon, so it does not undergo this oxidation.

**Iodoform and acidity.** Alkaline aqueous iodine gives a yellow precipitate of tri-iodomethane, $\ce{CHI3}$, from an alcohol with $\ce{CH3CH(OH)-}$. The rest of $\ce{CH3CH(OH)R}$ becomes $\ce{RCO2^-}$; ethanol is also positive. Oxygen makes the O–H bond polar, so alcohols can donate a proton. Ethanol is less acidic than water: its ethyl group donates electron density by the inductive effect and destabilises the ethoxide conjugate base relative to hydroxide.

## Method

1. Locate every OH-bearing carbon and count its carbon neighbours.
2. For oxidation, choose distillation or reflux before naming the product. Check for the orange-to-green change.
3. For alkaline iodine, search specifically for $\ce{CH3CH(OH)-}$; then name yellow $\ce{CHI3}$ and the remaining carboxylate.

> [!example]- Worked example
> Deduce the iodoform products from butan-2-ol.
> Identify $\ce{CH3CH(OH)-}$ in $\ce{CH3CH(OH)CH2CH3}$.
> The methyl side forms yellow $\ce{CHI3}$; the remaining three-carbon part forms propanoate, $\ce{CH3CH2CO2^-}$.

## Traps

- **Trap:** A tertiary alcohol oxidises to a ketone. **Why it's wrong:** Its OH-bearing carbon lacks a hydrogen. **Instead:** Check that carbon before choosing a product.
- **Trap:** Every secondary alcohol gives the iodoform test. **Why it's wrong:** Only those with $\ce{CH3CH(OH)-}$ qualify. **Instead:** Inspect the adjacent methyl group.
- **Trap:** An ethyl group makes ethanol more acidic than water. **Why it's wrong:** It destabilises the ethoxide ion. **Instead:** Compare conjugate-base stability.

## Exam technique

For *describe*, name both reagent and condition: “acidified” and “heat under reflux” may be separate credit. For *deduce*, give the structural feature that supports your product or test result. Distinguish the alcohol group from other groups in multifunctional molecules.
