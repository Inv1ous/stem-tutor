# 13.4 Isomerism: structural isomerism and stereoisomerism
> [!abstract] In one breath
> Isomers share a molecular formula. First decide whether their atom connections differ; then test structures with the same connections for distinct arrangements in space.

## Key ideas

**Structural isomers** have the same molecular formula but different structural formulae. In **chain isomerism**, the carbon skeleton differs: butane and 2-methylpropane are examples. In **positional isomerism**, the same functional group or multiple bond moves to another position on the same skeleton, as in propan-1-ol and propan-2-ol. In **functional group isomerism**, the functional groups differ, as in ethanol and methoxymethane, both $\ce{C2H6O}$. Be precise: a shared molecular formula alone does not tell you which type of isomerism is present.

**Stereoisomers** have the same structural formula but different arrangements of atoms in space. The syllabus divides these into **geometrical (cis/trans) isomerism** and **optical isomerism**. E/Z names are acceptable but are not required here.

For an alkene, sideways p-orbital overlap creates a $\pi$ bond. Rotating about $\ce{C=C}$ would break this overlap, so rotation is restricted. A cis/trans pair exists only when **each** double-bond carbon has two different attached groups. In but-2-ene, the two methyl groups are on the same side in the cis form and opposite sides in the trans form. But-1-ene has a terminal $\ce{CH2=}$ carbon with two identical hydrogen atoms, so it has no cis/trans pair. The same two-group test applies to each double bond in a molecule with several alkenes.

A **chiral centre** in these compounds is a tetrahedral carbon attached to four different groups. Its two optical isomers, or **enantiomers**, are non-superimposable mirror images. Carbon 2 of butan-2-ol is attached to $\ce{H}$, $\ce{OH}$, $\ce{CH3}$ and $\ce{CH2CH3}$, so it is chiral. Carbon 2 of propan-2-ol has two methyl groups and is not. A compound can have more than one chiral centre.

## Method

1. From a molecular formula, list distinct carbon skeletons, possible functional groups and positions. Check each formula's atom count and carbon valencies. Reverse the numbering or turn a symmetric skeleton around to remove duplicates.
2. For every $\ce{C=C}$ bond, compare both substituents on each double-bond carbon. Where both carbons pass, add cis/trans arrangements. In a cyclic compound, substituents on different ring carbons may be on the same or opposite face of the ring.
3. Inspect tetrahedral carbons for four different complete groups. In rings, trace both paths around the ring before deciding whether they are identical. Add optical isomers where appropriate, then compare complete structures for symmetry duplicates.

> [!example]- Worked example
> Deduce all acyclic alkene isomers of $\ce{C4H8}$, including stereoisomers.
> List the three structural isomers: but-1-ene, but-2-ene and 2-methylpropene.
> But-1-ene and 2-methylpropene each have two identical groups at one alkene carbon. But-2-ene has two different groups on each alkene carbon, so it gives cis-but-2-ene and trans-but-2-ene.
> The distinct compounds are therefore but-1-ene, cis-but-2-ene, trans-but-2-ene and 2-methylpropene: **four**.

## Traps

- **Trap:** Calling any two compounds with the same molecular formula stereoisomers. **Why it's wrong:** Their atom connections may differ. **Instead:** Compare structural formulae first.
- **Trap:** Counting every double bond as two geometrical forms. **Why it's wrong:** A double-bond carbon may have two identical groups. **Instead:** Test both carbons of each $\ce{C=C}$.
- **Trap:** Assuming four bonds make a carbon chiral. **Why it's wrong:** Two attached groups may be identical, even if their ring paths need tracing. **Instead:** Compare all four complete groups.
- **Trap:** Adding independent stereochemical choices or counting mirror-equivalent cases twice. **Why it's wrong:** Independent choices multiply, and symmetry can collapse apparent combinations. **Instead:** Enumerate the actual distinct structures.

## Exam technique

For **describe** or **define**, include both what remains the same and what differs. For **identify**, mark the relevant carbon or bond and state the groups that prove the test. For **deduce** and **determine**, show a systematic list before giving a total. If a question says *structural isomerism only*, do not count cis/trans or optical pairs separately; if it asks for all isomers, check both structural and stereochemical alternatives.
