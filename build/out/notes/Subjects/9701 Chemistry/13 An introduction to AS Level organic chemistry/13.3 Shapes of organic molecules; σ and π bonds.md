# 13.3 Shapes of organic molecules; σ and π bonds
> [!abstract] In one breath
> Carbon connectivity tells you whether a molecule is straight-chained, branched or cyclic. The bonding at each atom determines its local shape, bond angles, and the arrangement of σ and π bonds; a flat drawing alone cannot establish whether a molecule is planar.

## Key ideas
A **straight-chain** molecule has an unbranched carbon chain, even when its structural formula is drawn as a zigzag. A **branched** molecule has a side carbon chain attached to the main chain. A **cyclic** molecule contains a closed ring of atoms. For example, butane is straight-chained, 2-methylpropane is branched, and cyclohexane is cyclic. These words describe connectivity. A ring drawn as a hexagon on paper need not be flat in three dimensions.

Hybridisation describes the mixing of atomic orbitals on an atom to form orbitals used in its σ bonding. Classify the atoms one at a time. An $sp^3$ carbon forms four σ-bond directions, giving **tetrahedral** local geometry and bond angles close to $109.5^\circ$. An $sp^2$ carbon forms three σ-bond directions in a **trigonal planar** arrangement, with angles close to $120^\circ$; one unhybridised p orbital remains. An $sp$ carbon forms two σ-bond directions in a **linear** arrangement, with a $180^\circ$ angle; two unhybridised p orbitals remain. These are approximate angles in molecules where substituents are not identical.

A **σ bond** forms by end-on overlap along the line joining two nuclei. A **π bond** forms by sideways overlap of parallel unhybridised p orbitals, placing electron density above and below the σ-bond framework. A single bond contains one σ bond. A double bond contains one σ and one π bond. A triple bond contains one σ and two π bonds. In ethane, each carbon is $sp^3$ and all C–C and C–H bonds are σ. In ethene, each carbon is $sp^2$: the C=C comprises one σ and one π bond. In ethyne, each carbon is $sp$: the C≡C comprises one σ and two π bonds. For C–H σ bonds, a carbon hybrid orbital overlaps end-on with a hydrogen $1s$ orbital.

**Planar** means that the atoms under discussion lie in the same plane. All six atoms in ethene are planar: each carbon and its attached atoms have trigonal planar geometry, and the two carbon p orbitals overlap sideways. A saturated cyclohexane ring has tetrahedral $sp^3$ carbon centres and normally adopts a puckered, non-planar conformation. Local trigonal planarity at one carbon does not guarantee that every atom of a larger molecule lies in one plane.

## Method
1. Trace the carbon skeleton and decide whether it is an open unbranched chain, an open branched chain, or a ring.
2. At each atom of interest, count σ-bond directions: four suggests $sp^3$, three $sp^2$, and two $sp$. Name the corresponding local shape and approximate bond angle.
3. Count one σ bond per connected pair of atoms. Add one π for each double bond and two π for each triple bond.
4. For a question about planarity, consider the positions of **all** specified atoms in three dimensions, especially around $sp^3$ centres.

> [!example]- Worked example
> In propene, $\ce{CH3CH=CH2}$, the methyl carbon has four σ bonds, so it is $sp^3$, tetrahedral, and near $109.5^\circ$. Each alkene carbon has three σ-bond directions, so it is $sp^2$, trigonal planar, and near $120^\circ$. The C=C bond contains one σ and one π bond; its π bond comes from sideways overlap of unhybridised p orbitals.

## Traps
- **Trap:** Calling a zigzag chain branched. **Why it's wrong:** A bend on paper is not a side chain. **Instead:** Follow the carbon connections.
- **Trap:** Counting a double bond as two σ bonds. **Why it's wrong:** The second component is π overlap. **Instead:** Count one σ per connected pair, then add π bonds.
- **Trap:** Calling every ring planar. **Why it's wrong:** Saturated $sp^3$ ring atoms often pucker out of the drawing plane. **Instead:** Test the three-dimensional geometry.

## Exam technique
For **Describe**, give the shape and angle together: tetrahedral $109.5^\circ$, trigonal planar $120^\circ$, or linear $180^\circ$. For **Explain**, link the number of σ-bond directions and remaining p orbitals to the hybridisation and the resulting bonds. When a structure mixes single and multiple bonds, label each carbon separately before counting. In a bond-counting answer, include the σ component of every double or triple bond.
