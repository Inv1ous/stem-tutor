# 1 Mathematical models in mechanics
> [!abstract] In one breath
> A mechanical model keeps the properties needed to answer a question and simplifies the rest. Read each adjective literally: it tells you which effect may be ignored and which forces or motions remain possible.

## Key ideas

A **particle** has mass, but its dimensions are negligible for the question. Treat a car as a particle when finding its motion along a road; its shape is irrelevant to that calculation. A **lamina** is a flat sheet whose thickness is negligible compared with its other dimensions. A **rigid body** keeps its shape and size under the forces being considered. It can still move, rotate and have weight.

A **rod** is a thin straight body. A **light** rod has negligible mass, so its own weight can be ignored, but it can still transmit a force. In a **uniform** rod, mass is evenly distributed along its length; in a uniform gravitational field its centre of mass is at the midpoint. A **non-uniform** rod has uneven mass distribution, so you cannot assume that midpoint result. Light, uniform and rigid describe different properties. A rod may be uniform and rigid without being light.

A **light string** has negligible mass. An **inextensible string** keeps a constant length when taut, although it can move. If two particles are joined by a taut inextensible string over a fixed pulley, their displacement magnitudes along the string are equal. The string can still have tension; in the usual ideal model it pulls but does not push.

A **smooth surface** is frictionless, but can exert a normal reaction. A **rough surface** may exert friction tangential to the contact, depending on whether slipping tends to occur. Rough does not imply that the friction force is always non-zero. A **light smooth pulley** has negligible mass and no friction at the string contact. For a light string over it, the tension is the same on either side. Its rotation changes the string's direction without changing that tension in the ideal model.

A **bead** is a small object threaded on a **wire** and constrained to move along the wire. A **peg** is a fixed small support around which a string can pass; unlike a pulley, it does not rotate as a wheel. Friction at a wire or peg can only be neglected when smoothness is stated or justified.

## Method

1. Name the actual object and the model used for it: for example, a cyclist as a particle or a bar as a rigid rod.
2. Translate each modelling word into a consequence. Light removes the object's own mass; smooth removes friction; inextensible fixes length. Do not remove other forces.
3. Use the consequence in the equation or explanation. For instance, equal displacements in equal times give equal speed magnitudes for particles linked by a taut inextensible string.
4. Check the model's limits: size matters for rotation, mass distribution matters for moments, and friction may matter at rough contact.

> [!example]- Worked example
> Two particles are joined by a light inextensible string passing over a fixed light smooth pulley. One particle moves down by $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$. Explain the other particle's motion and compare the tensions.
> The taut string's total length is constant, so the segment on the other side must shorten by $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$. The other particle moves up by $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$.
> The string is light and the pulley is smooth, so its tension has the same magnitude on both sides. Neither assumption says that the tension is zero.

## Traps

- **Trap:** A particle has no mass. **Why it's wrong:** Only its dimensions are neglected. **Instead:** Keep its mass in force and momentum calculations.
- **Trap:** Smooth contact supplies no force. **Why it's wrong:** Smooth means no friction. **Instead:** Keep the normal reaction, or the string tension at a smooth pulley.
- **Trap:** An inextensible string cannot move. **Why it's wrong:** Its length stays constant while its parts can move. **Instead:** Relate the motions of the connected objects.
- **Trap:** Uniform means light. **Why it's wrong:** Evenly distributed mass can be substantial. **Instead:** Decide separately whether mass is negligible and whether it is evenly distributed.

## Exam technique

For **State**, give the assumption precisely, such as “negligible mass” or “no friction.” For **Explain**, link that assumption to a concrete consequence: “the string is inextensible, so the two particles have equal displacement magnitudes along it.” Do not claim equal tension around a peg unless its contact is specified as smooth. If a rod is non-uniform, do not place its weight at the midpoint without further information.
