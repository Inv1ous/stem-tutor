# 6 Moments
> [!abstract] In one breath
> A moment measures how strongly a force tends to turn a body about a chosen point. A body in equilibrium must have both zero resultant force and zero resultant moment.

## Key ideas
The **moment** of a force about a point is the force multiplied by the perpendicular distance from the point to the force's line of action. If the force is $F$ and that distance is $d$, the magnitude is $Fd$, in $\mathrm{N\,m}$. State whether the turning effect is clockwise or anticlockwise. A force whose line of action passes through the pivot has zero moment about it.

For equilibrium, the resultant force is zero and the sum of clockwise moments equals the sum of anticlockwise moments about the same point. These conditions apply to the coplanar parallel forces in this chapter. Equal and opposite forces acting along different lines can have zero resultant force but still form a couple that turns the body.

A uniform rod has its centre of mass at its midpoint, so its weight acts vertically down there. A light rod has negligible weight. A support exerts a reaction on the body; draw the forces on the body itself. The following schematic shows the opposing force directions: it does not show where they act or their moment arms.

## Method
1. Draw the body, its dimensions and every force, including the body's weight when it is not light. Mark the point where each acts.
2. Choose a moment point, often a support with an unknown reaction. A force through it then contributes no moment.
3. Find each perpendicular distance to a force's line of action. Decide its turning direction and equate the two sums of moments.
4. Resolve the parallel forces: for vertical forces, total upward force equals total downward force. Solve for any remaining unknown, then check both balances.

> [!example]- Worked example
> A light horizontal beam $AB$ is $4\,\mathrm{m}$ long, supported at both ends. A $60\,\mathrm{N}$ load acts downwards $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$. Find the upward reactions.
> Let them be $R_A$ and $R_B$. Taking moments about $A$, $4R_B=60(1.5)$, so $R_B=22.5\,\mathrm{N}$.
> Vertically, $R_A+R_B=60$, giving $R_A=37.5\,\mathrm{N}$. Both the force and moment balances now hold.

## Traps
- **Trap:** Use the full rod length as every moment arm. **Why it's wrong:** Distance is measured from the chosen pivot to each line of action. **Instead:** Label each perpendicular distance separately.
- **Trap:** Stop after balancing forces. **Why it's wrong:** A zero resultant can still leave a turning effect. **Instead:** Check moments too.
- **Trap:** Put a uniform rod's weight at the pivot automatically. **Why it's wrong:** Its centre may lie elsewhere. **Instead:** Mark its midpoint first.

## Exam technique
Show the pivot and the terms in the moment equation, including their distances. Keep units consistent. When using mass, convert to weight with $g=9.8\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$ and give the resulting numerical answer to 2 or 3 significant figures. A negative reaction indicates that the assumed direction needs interpretation in the model.
