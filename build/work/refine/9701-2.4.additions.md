## New lesson-note lines

> [!example]- Worked example: final gas after combustion
> Calculate the final dry gas volume at r.t.p. when $0.0400\ \mathrm{mol}$ of propane burns completely in $7.20\ \mathrm{dm^3}$ of oxygen measured at r.t.p. Cool the products to r.t.p.; assume all water condenses and no carbon dioxide is lost.
>
> $\ce{C3H8(g) + 5O2(g) -> 3CO2(g) + 4H2O(l)}$ (states after cooling to r.t.p.). Balance carbon and hydrogen first, then oxygen; the coefficients determine the amounts consumed and formed.
>
> Oxygen consumed: $n=5\times0.0400=0.200\ \mathrm{mol}$, so $V=0.200\times24.0=4.80\ \mathrm{dm^3}$. The starting $7.20\ \mathrm{dm^3}$ exceeds the oxygen needed, so all propane burns and oxygen remains.
>
> Oxygen remaining: $7.20-4.80=2.40\ \mathrm{dm^3}$. Final gas includes unreacted oxygen as well as gaseous products.
>
> Carbon dioxide formed: $n=3\times0.0400=0.120\ \mathrm{mol}$, giving $V=0.120\times24.0=2.88\ \mathrm{dm^3}$. Three moles of carbon dioxide form per mole of propane.
>
> Exclude condensed liquid water; final dry gas volume $=2.40+2.88=5.28\ \mathrm{dm^3}$ (3 s.f.). Only oxygen and carbon dioxide contribute; their volumes can be added at the same temperature and pressure.
> [!example]- Your turn: faded gas-volume balance

> Use the equation above: oxygen consumed → oxygen remaining → carbon dioxide formed → final dry gas volume.
