## New lesson-note lines

| Giant ionic | Simple molecular | Giant covalent | Giant metallic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Repeating particles:** positive and negative ions | **Repeating particles:** discrete molecules | **Repeating particles:** covalently bonded atoms | **Repeating particles:** positive metal ions surrounded by delocalised electrons |
| **Attraction across the solid:** electrostatic attraction between opposite ions | **Attraction across the solid:** intermolecular forces between molecules | **Attraction across the solid:** strong covalent bonds in a network; graphite also has weak forces between layers | **Attraction across the solid:** electrostatic attraction between positive ions and delocalised electrons |
| **Mobile charge carriers:** none in solid; ions in melt or aqueous solution | **Mobile charge carriers:** none in the pure solid or melt for the examples here | **Mobile charge carriers:** none in diamond or silicon(IV) oxide; delocalised electrons along graphite layers | **Mobile charge carriers:** delocalised electrons in solid and liquid |
A regular molecular crystal lattice still contains separate molecules: regular arrangement does not imply a continuous covalent network connecting those molecules.
When representing an ionic lattice or formula unit, label ions with their correct charges and show their ratio: sodium chloride has $\ce{Na+}$ and $\ce{Cl-}$ in a $1:1$ ratio. Do not draw an isolated covalent molecule in place of these ions.
