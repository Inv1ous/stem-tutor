## New lesson-note lines

The converter's catalyst is **heterogeneous**: it is a solid surface in a different phase from the gaseous reactants that react on it, and in $\ce{2NO + 2CO -> N2 + 2CO2}$ the oxidation number of nitrogen falls from $+2$ in NO to $0$ in $\ce{N2}$.
![[Assets/9701/9701-12-1-fig-catalytic-converter.png]]
*Figure: Cross-section of a catalytic converter: exhaust gas carrying NO and CO passes over the hot honeycomb catalyst and leaves as $\ce{N2}$ and $\ce{CO2}$ (source: 9701 c12.1, p.7)*
Notice that the catalyst is a solid honeycomb the exhaust gases flow through: the large surface area is what lets a heterogeneous catalyst treat a fast-moving gas stream.
