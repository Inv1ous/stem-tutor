## New lesson-note lines

![[Assets/9702/9702-7.2-transverse-direction.svg]]
This snapshot represents a transverse rope wave travelling right. Rope particles oscillate vertically, perpendicular to energy transfer; the arrow marked $A$ measures amplitude, not wave travel. For a longitudinal wave travelling right, particles instead oscillate left and right, parallel to energy transfer.
![[Assets/9702/9702-7.2-longitudinal-displacement.svg]]
This longitudinal snapshot uses positive displacement for motion to the right and negative displacement for motion to the left. Graph height is horizontal particle displacement, not upward motion. Around the labelled compression centre, particles on the left are displaced right and those on the right are displaced left, reducing their separation. Around the rarefaction centre the displacements separate neighbours. The centres occur at the steepest negative and positive slopes respectively.
The same sinusoidal displacement–time trace can describe either wave type: its shape alone does not give the oscillation direction relative to energy transfer.
*Sources for these visual explanations: learner library, SaveMyExams/9702 a-level-physics-cie-25/9702 c7.2.pdf, pp.2–6; the displacement-gradient relation is also illustrated by the University of Manchester [continuous longitudinal sound wave](https://personalpages.manchester.ac.uk/staff/richard.baker/WebDemonstrations/SoundTransmission/sine-wave_transmission.html).*
