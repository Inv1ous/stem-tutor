## New lesson-note lines

![[Assets/9702/9702-2.1-signed-areas.svg]]
The shaded triangles represent signed displacement: above the axis, $+\tfrac12(4)(8)=+16\ \mathrm{m}$; below, $-\tfrac12(2)(4)=-4\ \mathrm{m}$. Net displacement is $16-4=+12\ \mathrm{m}$, whereas total distance is $16+4=20\ \mathrm{m}$.
**Acceleration-time graphs.** Height gives acceleration; a horizontal line means constant acceleration. Signed area over an interval gives the change in velocity, not the final velocity: add it to the initial velocity. For example, $+2.0\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$ for $3.0\ \mathrm{s}$ gives $\Delta v=(2.0)(3.0)=+6.0\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. An area below the time axis gives a negative change in velocity.
*Graph reference: sources/library/SaveMyExams/9702 a-level-physics-cie-25/9702 c2.1.pdf, p.8 (paraphrased).*
With **uniform acceleration in a straight line**, $a=(v-u)/t$ gives $at=v-u$, hence $v=u+at$. Substitute into the trapezium-area expression:
$$s=\tfrac12(u+v)t=\tfrac12(u+u+at)t=ut+\tfrac12at^2$$
For $a\ne0$, rearrange to $t=(v-u)/a$ and substitute instead:
$$s=\tfrac12(u+v)\frac{v-u}{a}=\frac{v^2-u^2}{2a}$$
$$2as=v^2-u^2\quad\Rightarrow\quad v^2=u^2+2as$$
For $a=0$, velocity is constant, so $v=u$ and $s=ut$; the final equations still hold.
*Exam scope checked against [Cambridge 9702 syllabus, 2025–2027](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/664565-2025-2027-syllabus.pdf), p.17, outcomes 2.1.2, 2.1.3 and 2.1.6.*
