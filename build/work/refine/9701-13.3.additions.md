![[Assets/9701/9701-13-3-fig-sigma-overlap.png]]
*Figure: Sigma bonds from end-on overlap of s and p orbitals (source: 9701 c13.3, p.7)*
Notice that each σ bond, whichever orbitals formed it, ends up with its electron density symmetrical about the line joining the two nuclei.
![[Assets/9701/9701-13-3-fig-pi-overlap.png]]
*Figure: Pi bonds from sideways overlap of p orbitals in $sp^2$ and $sp$ hybridised carbons (source: 9701 c13.3, p.9)*
Notice that the p orbitals stay parallel and overlap sideways, so each π bond is one bond with two lobes, one above and one below the σ framework; the $sp$ carbon pair carries two such π bonds in perpendicular planes.
![[Assets/9701/9701-13-3-fig-ethene-coplanar.png]]
*Figure: Ethene has two trigonal planar centres that share one plane (source: 9701 c13.3, p.12)*
Notice that the two trigonal planar triangles drawn at the carbons lie in the same plane, which is why all six atoms of ethene, not just three at a time, are coplanar.
