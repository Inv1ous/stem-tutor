## New lesson-note lines

![[Assets/9702/9702-4.3-pressure-depth.svg]]
The straight line has slope $\rho g$ and intercept $p_{\mathrm{atm}}$, the pressure at the surface. Its rise between $h_1$ and $h_2$ is $\Delta p=\rho g(h_2-h_1)$; the surface pressure cancels when the two total pressures are subtracted.
For a fully immersed vertical prism of horizontal face area $A$ and height $\Delta h$, the side forces cancel and $p_b-p_t=\rho_{\mathrm{fluid}}g\Delta h$. Therefore $U=(p_b-p_t)A=\rho_{\mathrm{fluid}}gA\Delta h=\rho_{\mathrm{fluid}}gV_{\mathrm{displaced}}$. Equal displaced volumes in the same uniform fluid have equal upthrust even at different depths. For the same rigid prism moved deeper, both individual face forces increase by equal amounts, leaving their difference unchanged.
*Sources for this derivation: learner library, sources/library/SaveMyExams/9702 a-level-physics-cie-25/9702 c4.3.pdf, pp.8–10, 13–16; the force-balance connection is also explained in [OpenStax, University Physics §14.4](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/14-4-archimedes-principle-and-buoyancy).*
