## New lesson-note lines

![[Assets/9702/9702-7.5-transverse-wave.svg]]
This snapshot shows a transverse displacement wave travelling to the right, with vibration up and down, perpendicular to propagation. The profile represents one selected plane of vibration; this two-dimensional plot does not show every possible polarisation direction. For light, the corresponding oscillation is of the electric field, rather than displacement of material particles.
*Library reference: sources/library/SaveMyExams/9702 a-level-physics-cie-25/9702 c7.5.pdf, pp.2–3 (transverse vibration and polarisation).*
> [!example]- Why a middle filter restores transmission
> Two ideal filters have crossed transmission axes at $0^\circ$ and $90^\circ$. Let $I_0$ be the intensity of the plane-polarised light leaving the first filter. With no middle filter, the final intensity is $I_0\cos^2 90^\circ=0$.
> Insert a middle filter with its axis at $45^\circ$. It transmits $I_1=I_0\cos^2 45^\circ=I_0/2$ and sets the polarisation to $45^\circ$.
> The final axis is now $45^\circ$ from the incoming polarisation, so $I_2=I_1\cos^2 45^\circ=I_0/4$. Each filter sets the incident polarisation for the next filter.
> Here $I_0$ refers to an already-polarised beam; no unpolarised-light calculation is needed.
> Source: bundle examiner comment, CAIE 9702 Nov 2023 P12 Q24; the middle-filter mechanism is also illustrated by [UCSB Physics: Polarizers](https://web.physics.ucsb.edu/~lecturedemonstrations/Composer/Pages/84.36.html). Malus's law: sources/library/SaveMyExams/9702 a-level-physics-cie-25/9702 c7.5.pdf, p.7.
