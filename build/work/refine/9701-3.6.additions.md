## New lesson-note lines

> [!example]- Worked example: ethanol and methoxymethane
> Explain why ethanol, $\ce{CH3CH2OH}$, has a higher boiling point than methoxymethane, $\ce{CH3OCH3}$.
> 1. **Structure:** Ethanol has an O–H group that can donate a hydrogen bond to an oxygen lone pair on another ethanol molecule. Methoxymethane has oxygen lone pairs but no O–H or N–H donor, so its own molecules cannot hydrogen-bond to one another.
> 2. **Attractions:** Both substances have London dispersion forces and permanent dipole attractions. Ethanol additionally forms hydrogen bonds between its own molecules.
> 3. **Energy and boiling:** The stronger intermolecular attraction in ethanol means more energy is needed to separate its molecules, so its boiling point is higher.
> 4. **Check:** Boiling overcomes intermolecular attractions in both substances; covalent bonds within their molecules remain intact.
