## New lesson-note lines

### Why the denominator changes
Assume a stationary observer in a uniform, still medium, a constant source frequency and direct motion with $0<v_s<v$. In one emission period $T=1/f_s$, an earlier wavefront travels $vT$ while the source travels $v_sT$. Consecutive wavefront spacing is therefore $\lambda_{ahead}=(v-v_s)T$ ahead and $\lambda_{behind}=(v+v_s)T$ behind. Using $f_o=v/\lambda$ gives $f_o=f_s v/(v-v_s)$ for approach and $f_o=f_s v/(v+v_s)$ for recession.
### Sketching a passing source
![[Assets/9702/9702-7.3-frequency-time.svg]]
For constant source speed and frequency in the idealised direct-line model, notice the horizontal level above $f_s$ before passing and the horizontal level below $f_s$ afterwards. The dashed vertical marker locates the passing time $t_p$; the two levels switch abruptly there. Source motion changes wavefront spacing, while sound speed stays fixed. A real path offset from the observer gives a smooth transition instead; this sketch assumes direct approach and recession.
*Sources for these additions: `sources/library/SaveMyExams/9702 a-level-physics-cie-25/9702 c7.3.pdf`, pp.2–4 (wavefront spacing and sign rule); [UNSW Physclips: The Doppler Effect](https://animations.physics.unsw.edu.au/jw/doppler.htm), moving-source derivation and passing-path discussion; ER 9702 w23 P21 Q5 (sketch features).*
