## New lesson-note lines

> [!example]- From molecular to net ionic equation
> Mixing aqueous calcium chloride and sodium carbonate precipitates calcium carbonate.
> Molecular: $\ce{CaCl2(aq) + Na2CO3(aq) -> CaCO3(s) + 2NaCl(aq)}$.
> Full ionic: $\ce{Ca^2+(aq) + 2Cl-(aq) + 2Na+(aq) + CO3^2-(aq) -> CaCO3(s) + 2Na+(aq) + 2Cl-(aq)}$.
> Cancel $\ce{2Na+(aq)}$ and $\ce{2Cl-(aq)}$ on both sides; these ions are unchanged spectators.
> Net ionic: $\ce{Ca^2+(aq) + CO3^2-(aq) -> CaCO3(s)}$.
> Both sides contain one Ca, one C and three O atoms. Total charge is $+2-2=0$ on the left and zero on the right. Keep the solid intact.
> [!example]- Crucible masses and water of crystallisation
> An empty crucible weighs $20.000\,\mathrm{g}$, with $\ce{CuSO4.xH2O}$ it weighs $24.992\,\mathrm{g}$, and after complete dehydration it weighs $23.192\,\mathrm{g}$. Only water is lost. Find $x$ using formula masses 159.6 for $\ce{CuSO4}$ and 18.0 for water.
> Water lost $=24.992-23.192=1.800\,\mathrm{g}$. The crucible and dry salt remain in both loaded readings.
> Dry salt $=23.192-20.000=3.192\,\mathrm{g}$. The residue reading includes the empty crucible.
> $n(\ce{H2O})=1.800/18.0=0.100\,\mathrm{mol}$; $n(\ce{CuSO4})=3.192/159.6=0.0200\,\mathrm{mol}$. Formula ratios compare amounts in moles, not masses.
> $x=0.100/0.0200=5$, so the formula is $\ce{CuSO4.5H2O}$. Divide water moles by salt moles to find waters per formula unit.
> Check $3.192+1.800=4.992\,\mathrm{g}=24.992-20.000\,\mathrm{g}$. Dry salt plus water must recover the original hydrate mass.
