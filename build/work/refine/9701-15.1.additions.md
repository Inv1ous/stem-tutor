## New lesson-note lines

A **nucleophile donates a pair of electrons to form a covalent bond**. With bromoethane and ethanolic potassium cyanide, the reacting nucleophile is $\ce{CN^-}$, not $\ce{KCN}$. Its carbon end bonds to the carbon formerly bearing Br, giving $\ce{CH3CH2CN}$: **propanenitrile**. Count the nitrile carbon in the three-carbon parent chain; “cyanide” names the reagent ion, while “nitrile” is the product suffix.
> [!example]- Worked comparison: electron-pair arrows
> Compare bromoethane and 2-bromo-2-methylpropane with aqueous hydroxide.
> 1. Classify bromoethane as primary and 2-bromo-2-methylpropane as tertiary by counting carbon neighbours of the carbon bonded to Br. There are one and three carbon neighbours respectively; this predicts the preferred substitution pathway.
> 2. For bromoethane, draw $\ce{OH^-}$ with an oxygen lone pair. In one $S_N2$ step, draw a full-headed curly arrow from that lone pair to the carbon bonded to Br, approaching opposite Br, and another from the C–Br bond to Br. Show neutral $\ce{CH3CH2OH}$ and $\ce{Br^-}$ as products. The two arrows move electron pairs simultaneously. The reaction passes through a transition state, with no carbocation intermediate; the relatively uncrowded primary carbon permits backside attack.
> 3. For 2-bromo-2-methylpropane, first draw a curly arrow from the C–Br bond to Br. Show the separate tertiary carbocation $\ce{(CH3)3C+}$, with positive charge on the central carbon, and $\ce{Br^-}$. This slow first step of $S_N1$ is heterolytic bond breaking: both bonding electrons go to bromine.
> 4. Next draw a curly arrow from an oxygen lone pair of $\ce{OH^-}$ to the positively charged central carbon. Form neutral $\ce{(CH3)3COH}$, 2-methylpropan-2-ol. Bond formation follows carbocation formation in a second step. The three alkyl groups donate electron density by positive inductive effects, stabilising the carbocation; they also crowd the reaction carbon and hinder backside $S_N2$ attack.
>
> **Faded practice:** 1-Chloropropane is primary. Complete the mechanism name, both arrow origins and destinations, product structures and charges, and whether an intermediate forms. Explain the preference.
> [!success]- Faded practice answer
> $S_N2$: an oxygen lone pair of $\ce{OH^-}$ attacks the carbon bearing Cl while a second curly arrow runs from the C–Cl bond to Cl. The products are neutral $\ce{CH3CH2CH2OH}$ (propan-1-ol) and $\ce{Cl^-}$, with no carbocation intermediate. The primary carbon is relatively accessible to backside attack; a primary carbocation is relatively unstable.
