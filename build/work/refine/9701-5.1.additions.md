## New lesson-note lines

![[Assets/9701/9701-5.1-endothermic.svg]]
In the endothermic profile, products lie above reactants and $\Delta H>0$; in the exothermic profile they lie below and $\Delta H<0$. Both forward activation energies run from reactants to the peak.
> [!example]- Worked example: calorimetry with an excess reagent
> Mix $50.0\ \mathrm{cm^3}$ of $1.00\ \mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}}$ hydrochloric acid with $30.0\ \mathrm{cm^3}$ of $2.00\ \mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}}$ sodium hydroxide. Both start at $20.0\,^{\circ}\mathrm C$ and the final temperature is $25.0\,^{\circ}\mathrm C$. Assume additive volumes, density $1.00\ \mathrm{g\,cm^{-3}}$, $c=4.18\ \mathrm{J\,g^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$, no heat loss and negligible cup heat capacity.
> The whole solution warms: $m=(50.0+30.0)(1.00)=80.0\ \mathrm g$ and $\Delta T=25.0-20.0=5.0\ \mathrm K$.
> Hence $q_{\mathrm{solution}}=80.0(4.18)(5.0)=1672\ \mathrm J$.
> For $\ce{HCl(aq) + NaOH(aq) -> NaCl(aq) + H2O(l)}$, acid moles are $1.00(0.0500)=0.0500\ \mathrm{mol}$ and alkali moles are $2.00(0.0300)=0.0600\ \mathrm{mol}$. HCl is limiting, so $0.0500\ \mathrm{mol}$ of water forms.
> Neutralisation uses **one mole of water** as its basis: $\Delta H_{neut}=-1672/(1000\times0.0500)=-33.44\approx-33\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$ (2 s.f.). The reaction loses the energy the solution gains; excess alkali contributes to heated mass but not to water formed.
