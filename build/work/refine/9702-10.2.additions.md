## New lesson-note lines

![[Assets/9702/9702-10.2-series-loop.svg]]
The ideal ammeter has negligible resistance and reads the common current $I$ through $R_1$ and $R_2$. With negligible cell internal resistance, the cell's e.m.f. is $E=IR_1+IR_2$: the two resistor drops add around this single closed loop.
> [!example]- Worked example: opposing cells
> In one series loop, two ideal cells of e.m.f.s $12.0\,\mathrm{V}$ and $8.0\,\mathrm{V}$ oppose each other, with resistances $1.0\,\Omega$ and $0.5\,\Omega$. There is no other resistance.
> Choose both the current arrow $I\;\circlearrowright$ and the loop arrow $\circlearrowright$ clockwise. Arrange the polarities so this traversal crosses the $12.0\,\mathrm{V}$ cell from $-\to+$ (a rise) and the $8.0\,\mathrm{V}$ cell from $+\to-$ (a fall). Across each resistor, following $I$ gives a drop.
> Kirchhoff's second law gives $12.0-8.0=I(1.0+0.5)$, with voltages in volts and resistances in ohms. Hence $I=4.0/1.5=2.666\ldots\,\mathrm{A}=2.7\,\mathrm{A}$ to two significant figures. The positive result confirms the chosen arrow.
> Check using the unrounded current: the resistor drops are $8/3\,\mathrm{V}$ and $4/3\,\mathrm{V}$, so the signed loop balance is $12.0-8.0-8/3-4/3=0$. Energy gained per coulomb equals energy transferred per coulomb.
