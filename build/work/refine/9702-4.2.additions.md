## New lesson-note lines

### Three forces on one body
![[Assets/9702/9702-4.2-three-forces.svg]]
The dot represents a small body: tension $T$ pulls up and left, a horizontal push $F$ acts rightwards, and weight $W$ acts downwards. Here the tension is $30^\circ$ to the upward vertical. Copy the arrows without rotating them: draw $F$ rightwards, then $W$ downwards from its head, then $T$ up and left back to the start. This closed triangle shows force balance. All three lines of action also pass through the dot, so their moments about it are zero.
> [!example]- Worked example: from arrows to components
> For the body above, let $W=24\,\mathrm{N}$ and let the tension make $30^\circ$ with the vertical. Calculate the tension and horizontal push needed for equilibrium.
> Draw $F$ rightwards, $W$ downwards, then $T$ up-left to close the triangle. The internal angle between the vertical side and the sloping side is $30^\circ$; the angle between the actual downward weight and upward tension at the body is $150^\circ$.
> Vertical balance gives $T\cos30^\circ=24$, so $T=24/\cos30^\circ=27.7\,\mathrm{N}$ (3 s.f.). The vertical component uses cosine because the angle is measured from the vertical.
> Horizontal balance gives $F=T\sin30^\circ=24\tan30^\circ=13.9\,\mathrm{N}$ (3 s.f.). Thus the triangle's horizontal and vertical sides cancel the tension's components, and the final arrow returns to the starting point.
> The force lines meet at the body, giving zero moment about that meeting point. Together with zero resultant force this establishes equilibrium; for separated force lines on a rigid beam, calculate the moments separately.
*Source for the force-and-moment method: learner library, `sources/library/SaveMyExams/9702 a-level-physics-cie-25/9702 c4.2.pdf`, pp.5–6. The worked values and practice questions are original. For why zero force must accompany zero moment about a chosen point, see [OpenStax, University Physics 1, §12.1](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/12-1-conditions-for-static-equilibrium), supplementing the library's numerical example.*
