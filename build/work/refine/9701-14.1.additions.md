## New lesson-note lines

> [!example]- Worked example: cracking a different feedstock
> One molecule of $\ce{C12H26}$ cracks into heptane and one alkene molecule.
> 1. Heptane is $\ce{C7H16}$. Carbon remaining: $12-7=5$.
> 2. Hydrogen remaining: $26-16=10$, so the alkene is $\ce{C5H10}$.
> 3. Equation: $\ce{C12H26 -> C7H16 + C5H10}$. Check C: $12=7+5$ and H: $26=16+10$.
> 4. Heptane is useful as a fuel; the pentene product can undergo addition polymerisation to make a polymer.
> [!example]- Your turn: complete the faded example
> One molecule of $\ce{C15H32}$ cracks into $\ce{C9H20}$ and one alkene molecule. Carbon remaining: $15-9=6$.
> Complete the hydrogen subtraction, write the equation, check both elements, and state a use of the alkene.
> [!success]- Faded example answer
> Hydrogen remaining: $32-20=12$. The equation is $\ce{C15H32 -> C9H20 + C6H12}$.
> Check C: $15=9+6$ and H: $32=20+12$. The hexene product can be a monomer for addition polymerisation.
