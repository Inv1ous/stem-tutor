## New lesson-note lines

**Picture ethene:** each carbon uses three $sp^2$ orbitals for end-on $\sigma$ overlap: two with hydrogen 1s orbitals and one with the other carbon. This framework lies in one plane. Each carbon retains one unhybridised p orbital perpendicular to the plane; these parallel p orbitals overlap sideways above and below it to form one $\pi$ bond. Rotation about the carbon–carbon bond would destroy this sideways overlap, so rotation is restricted. In a triple bond, two unhybridised p orbitals on each atom overlap sideways in two mutually perpendicular planes, forming two $\pi$ bonds alongside one $\sigma$ bond.
**Full hybridisation rule:** include lone pairs as well as $\sigma$ bonds when counting electron regions around the atom: 2 regions imply sp, 3 imply $sp^2$, and 4 imply $sp^3$. A multiple bond counts as one region; its $\pi$ bonds add no extra regions. In ammonia, nitrogen has three $\sigma$ bonds and one lone pair: four regions, hence $sp^3$. Each ethene carbon has three $\sigma$ bonds and no lone pairs: three regions, hence $sp^2$.
**Scope of the comparison:** bond energy indicates the relative ease of breaking the particular bond being compared under comparable conditions. Overall reactivity also depends on the reaction pathway and the bonds formed. Read the nitrogen/oxygen example as a comparison of breaking $\ce{N#N}$ and $\ce{O=O}$, and the halogen example as breaking the halogen–halogen bond to form atoms; neither alone ranks every reaction of those molecules.
   **Include lone pairs:** use the total number of $\sigma$ bonds plus lone pairs, not the $\sigma$ count alone: 2 regions give sp, 3 give $sp^2$, 4 give $sp^3$.
For a reactivity comparison, name the bond being broken and compare its bond energy under comparable conditions. Qualify the conclusion: the reaction pathway and the bonds formed also affect overall reactivity; bond energy alone does not determine the reaction rate.
