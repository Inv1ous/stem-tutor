## New lesson-note lines

> [!example]- Worked example: total work across two regions
> A specimen is stretched slowly. The force–extension graph is straight from the origin to $240\,\mathrm{N}$ at $8.0\,\mathrm{mm}$, then curves to $300\,\mathrm{N}$ at $12.0\,\mathrm{mm}$. The elastic limit is crossed during the later region. Calculate the approximate total work input, treating that curve as a straight line between its endpoints.
> Convert first: $x_1=0.0080\,\mathrm{m}$ and $x_2=0.0120\,\mathrm{m}$, so the later region has width $0.0040\,\mathrm{m}$.
> The first area is a triangle: $W_1=\tfrac12F_1x_1=\tfrac12(240)(0.0080)=0.96\,\mathrm{J}$.
> Approximate the later area by a trapezium: $W_2\approx\tfrac12(F_1+F_2)(x_2-x_1)=\tfrac12(240+300)(0.0040)=1.08\,\mathrm{J}$.
> Add both areas: $W=W_1+W_2\approx2.04\,\mathrm{J}$, or $2.0\,\mathrm{J}$ to two significant figures. Keep unrounded values until the final sum.
> This is **total work input**. Because the elastic limit was crossed, plastic deformation occurs and the whole sum cannot be labelled recoverable elastic potential energy. The loading graph alone does not give the recoverable amount.
*Method reference: learner's library, `sources/library/SaveMyExams/9702 a-level-physics-cie-25/9702 c6.2.pdf`, pp.5–6; original numerical example. Official scope: [Cambridge Physics 9702 syllabus, §6.2, p.20](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/664565-2025-2027-syllabus.pdf), distinguishing work from graph area and elastic energy within the proportional limit.*
