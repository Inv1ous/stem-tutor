## New lesson-note lines

For a midpoint between the two force lines, each lever arm is $d/2$. The moments have the same sense, so $\tau=F(d/2)+F(d/2)=Fd$. Moving the reference point changes the signed moments by equal opposite amounts: outside the pair, at distance $x$ from the nearer line, their sum is $F(x+d)-Fx=Fd$. Unequal opposite forces leave a resultant equal to their difference, so they are not a couple.
### Inclined sign: which distance?
In side view, a uniform rectangular sign of length $L$ slopes downwards to the right from its upper hinge, at angle $\theta$ to the vertical. Its weight may be taken to act at its midpoint.
| Feature | Geometry in this side view |
| --- | --- |
| Radial distance from hinge to centre | $r=L/2$, measured along the sign |
| Weight's line of action | Vertical line through the centre |
| Perpendicular lever arm | Horizontal distance from hinge to that vertical line: $d_\perp=(L/2)\sin\theta$ |
> [!example]- Worked example: inclined sign
> A uniform sign has length $0.60\,\mathrm{m}$, mass $5.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and angle $40^\circ$ to the vertical, sloping downwards to the right.
> Its centre is $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ along the sign from the hinge. The horizontal lever arm is $d_\perp=0.30\sin40^\circ=0.193\,\mathrm{m}$.
> $M=mgd_\perp=(5.0)(9.81)(0.30\sin40^\circ)=9.5\,\mathrm{N\,m}$, clockwise in this view.
