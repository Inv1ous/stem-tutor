## New lesson-note lines

> [!example]- Worked example: a collision in two dimensions
> A $0.30\ \mathrm{kg}$ puck A moves at $4.0\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ and strikes stationary puck B of mass $0.60\ \mathrm{kg}$ on a frictionless horizontal table. Afterwards A moves at $2.0\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ at $60^\circ$ above its incident direction. Calculate B's speed and direction.
> Take positive $x$ along the incident motion and positive $y$ above it in the plane of the table. Write B's speed as $v$ and its angle below positive $x$ as $\theta$. No resultant external force acts on the pair.
> Conserve each momentum component, with a mass multiplying every velocity component:
> $$
> p_{x,\mathrm{before}}=p_{x,\mathrm{after}}:\quad 0.30(4.0)+0.60(0)=0.30(2.0\cos60^\circ)+0.60v\cos\theta.
> $$
> $$
> p_{y,\mathrm{before}}=p_{y,\mathrm{after}}:\quad 0.30(0)+0.60(0)=0.30(2.0\sin60^\circ)-0.60v\sin\theta.
> $$
> These angles are measured from $x$: cosine gives the adjacent $x$ component, sine the perpendicular $y$ component. If an angle is measured from $y$, cosine gives the $y$ component instead. The outgoing $y$ momenta have opposite signs.
> Solving gives $v\cos\theta=1.50$ and $v\sin\theta=\sqrt{3}/2$, in $\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. Hence $v=\sqrt{1.50^2+(\sqrt{3}/2)^2}=1.73\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ and $\theta=\tan^{-1}[(\sqrt{3}/2)/1.50]=30^\circ$ below the incident direction.
> Check using unrounded values: $p_{y,\mathrm{after}}=0.30(2.0\sin60^\circ)-0.60(\sqrt{3}/2)=0$; the perpendicular momenta cancel.
> [!example]- Worked example: elastic head-on collision
> On a frictionless horizontal track, a $0.40\ \mathrm{kg}$ trolley moving right at $4.0\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ collides elastically with a $0.60\ \mathrm{kg}$ trolley moving left at $1.0\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. Calculate both final velocities, taking right as positive.
> Momentum: $0.40(4.0)+0.60(-1.0)=0.40v_1+0.60v_2$.
> Elasticity supplies the second equation: $u_1-u_2=v_2-v_1$, so $4.0-(-1.0)=v_2-v_1$ and $v_2=v_1+5.0$.
> Substitute: $1.0=0.40v_1+0.60(v_1+5.0)$, giving $v_1=-2.0\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ (left) and $v_2=+3.0\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ (right).
> Separation speed uses **both** final velocities: $3.0-(-2.0)=5.0\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, equal to the approach speed.
> Check: $E_{k,\mathrm{before}}=\tfrac12(0.40)(4.0)^2+\tfrac12(0.60)(-1.0)^2=3.5\ \mathrm{J}$; $E_{k,\mathrm{after}}=\tfrac12(0.40)(-2.0)^2+\tfrac12(0.60)(3.0)^2=3.5\ \mathrm{J}$.
*Method sources for these new examples: learner library, `sources/library/SaveMyExams/9702 a-level-physics-cie-25/9702 c3.3.pdf`, pp.6–8 (components and elasticity); [OpenStax, University Physics Volume 1, §9.5](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/9-5-collisions-in-multiple-dimensions) (separate conservation equations for two-dimensional collisions).
