## New lesson-note lines

### Forces on a trolley
The box represents a trolley on a horizontal surface. Only forces acting on the trolley are shown.
![[Assets/9702/9702-3.1-trolley-free-body.svg]]
**Horizontal resultant: right. Acceleration: right.** The driving arrow is longer than the resistance arrow, so $F_{\mathrm{net}}=F_{\mathrm{drive}}-F_{\mathrm{resistance}}>0$ when right is positive. The upward normal reaction and downward weight balance, giving zero vertical acceleration. The resultant is the sum of these forces, not an extra force to add to the diagram.
During braking with no driving force, resistance gives a leftward resultant and acceleration. A trolley can still move right while slowing down: velocity describes its motion, while acceleration describes the rate of change of velocity.
*Sources for this addition: learner’s library, SaveMyExams, `sources/library/SaveMyExams/9702 a-level-physics-cie-25/9702 c3.1.pdf`, pp.16–17 (balanced forces and Newton’s second law); [OpenStax, University Physics §3.3](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/3-3-average-and-instantaneous-acceleration) (acceleration opposite to velocity when slowing down).*
