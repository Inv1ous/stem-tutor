## New lesson-note lines

**Ionic radius across a period.** Compare ions with the same electron arrangement first: $\ce{Mg^2+}$ is smaller than $\ce{Na+}$ because both have 10 electrons in two shells, but magnesium has more protons. The stable Period 3 anions $\ce{P^3-}$ and $\ce{S^2-}$ each have 18 electrons in three shells; within this pair, the greater nuclear charge makes $\ce{S^2-}$ smaller. Moving from Period 3 cations to anions therefore gives a sharp increase in ionic radius because the anions have an additional occupied shell, so there is no single steady decrease across the whole period. Down a group, comparable ions grow larger as another occupied shell is added; for example, $\ce{Br-}$ is larger than $\ce{Cl-}$ despite bromine's greater nuclear charge.
