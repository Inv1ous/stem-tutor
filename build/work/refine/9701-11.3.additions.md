## New lesson-note lines

### Reading a mixture of halide ions
Exam questions often give a solution containing two halide ions rather than one. Excess silver nitrate precipitates **every** halide present at once, so the colour of the solid you see is a blend and can be misleading: a white-and-yellow mixture can look cream. Judge the composition from the ammonia steps instead, taken in order.
- Add **dilute** aqueous ammonia: whatever dissolves is $\ce{AgCl}$, so chloride was present.
- Add **concentrated** aqueous ammonia to what is left: whatever dissolves now is $\ce{AgBr}$, so bromide was present.
- Whatever residue still remains is $\ce{AgI}$, so iodide was present. If nothing remains, there was no iodide.
So for a chloride-and-bromide mixture, excess concentrated ammonia leaves no precipitate at all, whereas any iodide in the mixture always leaves a yellow residue.
