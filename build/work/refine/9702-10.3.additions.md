## New lesson-note lines

![[Assets/9702/9702-10.3-ldr-divider.svg]]
The ideal ammeter shows the common series current and has negligible resistance. For an unloaded output between the two LDR terminals, $V_{LDR}=V_{in}R_{LDR}/(R+R_{LDR})$. Brighter light lowers $R_{LDR}$, so this output falls; an output between the fixed resistor terminals rises instead, with a constant supply p.d.
### Potentiometer wiring check
1. Connect the driver across the whole uniform wire PQ, with P positive, to establish a steady potential gradient along its full length.
2. Connect the unknown cell positive terminal to P and its negative terminal through the galvanometer to the sliding contact J. This places the detector in the comparison branch, where zero current is the balance test.
3. Check that the unknown e.m.f. opposes the p.d. along PJ in the comparison loop. At balance they are equal; reversing only the unknown cell makes them add instead.
4. Ensure the actual p.d. across PQ is at least the unknown e.m.f.; use a greater p.d. for a balance point inside the wire. Otherwise no contact position can provide enough opposing p.d.
- **Trap:** Choosing a potentiometer circuit by its shape alone. **Why it is wrong:** Reversed cell polarity prevents balance, and a galvanometer in the driver branch measures the wrong current. **Instead:** Trace the comparison loop and check that the two p.d.s oppose; zero comparison current does not mean zero driver current.
