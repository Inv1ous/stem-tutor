## New lesson-note lines

![[Assets/9702/9702-11-1-fig-scattering-complete.png]]
*Figure: Alpha-particle source, thin gold foil and movable detector in the scattering experiment (source: 9702 c11.1, p.2)*
Notice that moving the detector compares counts at different angles: most particles continue straight, while very few undergo large deflections.
> [!example]- Worked example: approximate nuclear mass
> Calculate an approximate mass of an iron-56 nucleus. With each nucleon having mass approximately $1u$, use $m\approx Au=56u$.
> Convert to kilograms: $m\approx56\times1.66\times10^{-27}=9.296\times10^{-26}\,\mathrm{kg}$, or $9.30\times10^{-26}\,\mathrm{kg}$ to three significant figures.
> This is approximate: individual nucleon masses are not exactly $1u$, and nuclear binding reduces the mass relative to the separated nucleons.
> [!example]- Try it: faded mass calculation
> For an aluminium-27 nucleus, use $m\approx27u$. Complete $m\approx27\times\ldots\,\mathrm{kg}$ and give three significant figures.
> [!answer]- Check your mass calculation
> $m\approx27\times1.66\times10^{-27}=4.482\times10^{-26}\,\mathrm{kg}\approx4.48\times10^{-26}\,\mathrm{kg}$.
The mass-conversion method follows the learner's Save My Exams notes, `9702 c11.1.pdf`, pp.6–7. The approximation is explained by [OpenStax, Nuclear Binding Energy](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-3/pages/10-2-nuclear-binding-energy).
