## New lesson-note lines

![[Assets/9701/9701-4.1-pv-temperature.svg]]
For a fixed amount of ideal gas, $pV$ is directly proportional to absolute temperature: the gradient is $nR$ and the line passes through the origin. The origin represents $pV=0$ at $T=0$ in the mathematical ideal-gas model; it is an extrapolation, not an attainable real-gas measurement.
> [!example]- Worked example: gas moles to reagent moles
> Aluminium reacts completely with excess hydrochloric acid: $\ce{2Al(s) + 6HCl(aq) -> 2AlCl3(aq) + 3H2(g)}$. The dry hydrogen collected without loss occupies $249\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ at $1.00\times10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$ and $300\,\mathrm{K}$. Assume ideal behaviour. Calculate the amount of aluminium that reacted.
> Convert $V=249\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^3}$, then calculate $n(\ce{H2})=pV/(RT)=(1.00\times10^5\times249\times10^{-6})/(8.31\times300)=0.00998797\,\mathrm{mol}$.
> The coefficients give $n(\ce{Al}):n(\ce{H2})=2:3$, so $n(\ce{Al})=\frac{2}{3}\times0.00998797=6.66\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{mol}$ (three significant figures).
> The gas equation gives hydrogen moles; the coefficient ratio converts them to the requested aluminium amount.
