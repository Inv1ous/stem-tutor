## New lesson-note lines

**Signs and quadrants:** Choose positive directions first. With right as positive $x$ and up as positive $y$, $F_x=F\cos\theta$ and $F_y=F\sin\theta$ give signed components when $\theta$ is measured anticlockwise from positive $x$. If an acute angle is given from the leftward horizontal, use it for component magnitudes, then assign signs from the axes. The principal value of $\tan^{-1}(F_y/F_x)$ needs a quadrant check using both signs; always name the final direction relative to a stated axis.
For example, $10.0\ \mathrm{N}$ at $30.0^\circ$ above the leftward horizontal has $F_x=-10.0\cos30.0^\circ=-8.66\ \mathrm{N}$ and $F_y=+10.0\sin30.0^\circ=+5.00\ \mathrm{N}$. The inverse tangent gives $-30.0^\circ$, but negative $x$ and positive $y$ put the force up-left: its direction is $150^\circ$ anticlockwise from positive $x$, or $30.0^\circ$ above the leftward horizontal.
