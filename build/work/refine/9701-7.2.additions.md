## New lesson-note lines

### Comparing titration curves
![[Assets/9701/9701-7.2-titration-curves.svg]]
The curves show $25.0\ \mathrm{cm^3}$ of $0.100\ \mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}}$ hydrochloric acid (solid) or ethanoic acid (dashed), with sodium hydroxide of the same concentration added at 298 K. The weak acid starts at higher pH because it is only partially dissociated. Its gently rising buffer region contains ethanoic acid and ethanoate ions; there is no corresponding buffer region for the strong acid. Both require the same volume at equivalence because they initially contain equal amounts of monoprotic acid. Equivalence is at pH 7 for hydrochloric acid but above 7 for ethanoic acid because ethanoate ions accept protons from water, producing hydroxide ions. After equivalence, excess sodium hydroxide controls both curves.
