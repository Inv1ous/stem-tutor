## New lesson-note lines

**CO electron check:** Count $4+6=10$ valence electrons. Use dots for C and crosses for O: first place two ordinary dot–cross shared pairs, then one O-supplied cross–cross shared pair. The remaining electrons form one dot–dot lone pair on C and one cross–cross lone pair on O. Thus six electrons are shared and four are in lone pairs; each atom has an octet. The symbols record electron origin in this conventional construction, and the coordinate arrow points from O to C, $\ce{O -> C}$.
> [!example]- Mg2Si electron check
> Draw two separate $[\ce{Mg}]^{2+}$ ions and one $[\ce{Si}]^{4-}$ ion. Each Mg transfers two electrons, giving four transferred electrons in total. Around Si draw eight outer electrons as four pairs: four dots originally from Si and four crosses transferred from the two Mg atoms. Do not place shared pairs between the ions; omit the inner shells and show no electrons on the Mg ions in this convention. Check the charge: $2(+2)-4=0$. Only outer-shell electrons and correct brackets and charges are required.
