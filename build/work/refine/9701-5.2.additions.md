## New lesson-note lines

> [!example]- Worked example: methanol via combustion
> Find the standard formation enthalpy of liquid methanol using combustion enthalpies of $\ce{C(s)}$, $\ce{H2(g)}$ and $\ce{CH3OH(l)}$: $-394$, $-286$ and $-726\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$, respectively.
> The formation equation is $\ce{C(s, graphite) + 2H2(g) + 1/2O2(g) -> CH3OH(l)}$.
> Start both routes at $\ce{C(s) + 2H2(g) + 2O2(g)}$ and end at $\ce{CO2(g) + 2H2O(l)}$.
> **Route 1:** burn the elements: $\ce{C(s) + O2(g) -> CO2(g)}$ and $\ce{2H2(g) + O2(g) -> 2H2O(l)}$. The signed total is $-394+2(-286)=-966\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$.
> **Route 2:** form one mole of methanol, leaving $\ce{3/2O2(g)}$ unused, then burn it: $\ce{CH3OH(l) + 3/2O2(g) -> CO2(g) + 2H2O(l)}$. The total is $\Delta H_f^\ominus-726$.
> Equate routes: $\Delta H_f^\ominus+(-726)=-966$, so $\Delta H_f^\ominus=-966-(-726)=-240\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$.
> Check: $-240+(-726)=-966$. Both routes make two moles of liquid water; the hydrogen combustion term must be doubled.
