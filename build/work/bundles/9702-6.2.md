## KCs

### 9702-6.2.1 — Elastic and plastic deformation (factual)
Statement: understand and use the terms elastic deformation, plastic deformation and elastic limit
Prerequisites: 9702-6.1.3 Hooke's law
Glossary: elastic deformation, plastic deformation, elastic limit

### 9702-6.2.2 — Work done from a force-extension graph (conceptual)
Statement: understand that the area under the force–extension graph represents the work done
Prerequisites: 9702-6.1.2 Load, extension and limit of proportionality; 9702-5.1.1 Work done by a force
Glossary: force-extension graph, work done, area under graph

### 9702-6.2.3 — Elastic potential energy from a graph (procedural)
Statement: determine the elastic potential energy of a material deformed within its limit of proportionality from the area under the force–extension graph
Prerequisites: 9702-6.2.2 Work done from a force-extension graph
Glossary: elastic potential energy, area under force-extension graph, limit of proportionality

### 9702-6.2.4 — Elastic potential energy formulas (procedural)
Statement: recall and use $E_P = \frac{1}{2}Fx = \frac{1}{2}kx^2$ for a material deformed within its limit of proportionality
Prerequisites: 9702-6.2.3 Elastic potential energy from a graph; 9702-6.1.4 Spring constant, k = F/x
Glossary: elastic potential energy, ep = half fx, ep = half kx squared

- **ER 9702 s22 P12 Q20** (matched: elastic limit, limit of proportionality): Candidates need to understand and use the terms elastic limit and limit of proportionality. Many candidates use the terms interchangeably, but they have distinct meanings: the elastic limit is the maximum force (or stress) before the onset of plastic behaviour, whereas the limit of proportionality is the end of the linear region of a force–extension (or stress–strain) graph. In many cases, the limit of proportionality and the elastic limit lie very close together (and may even coincide), but a wire can exhibit elastic behaviour even though the limit of proportionality has been exceeded. So, while D may be true in a particular instance, only A is correct in all circumstances.
