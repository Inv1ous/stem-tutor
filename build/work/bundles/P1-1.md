## KCs

### P1-1.1 — Laws of indices (procedural)
Statement: Laws of indices for all rational exponents.
Guidance: $a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n}$, $a^m \div a^n = a^{m-n}$, $(a^m)^n = a^{mn}$. The equivalence of $a^{\frac{1}{n}}$ and $\sqrt[n]{a}$, and of $a^{\frac{m}{n}}$ and $(\sqrt[n]{a})^m$, should be known.
Prerequisites: none
Glossary: laws of indices, rational exponents, index laws, fractional indices, surd equivalence

### P1-1.2 — Manipulating surds (procedural)
Statement: Use and manipulation of surds.
Guidance: Students should be able to rationalise denominators.
Prerequisites: P1-1.1 Laws of indices
Glossary: surd, rationalise the denominator, irrational number, simplifying surds, surd form

### P1-1.3 — Quadratic functions and graphs (procedural)
Statement: Quadratic functions and their graphs.
Prerequisites: none
Glossary: quadratic function, parabola, turning point, vertex, quadratic graph

### P1-1.4 — The discriminant (procedural)
Statement: The discriminant of a quadratic function.
Guidance: Need to know and to use $b^2-4ac>0$, $b^2-4ac=0$ and $b^2-4ac<0$.
Prerequisites: P1-1.3 Quadratic functions and graphs
Glossary: discriminant, real roots, repeated root, no real roots, nature of roots

### P1-1.5 — Solving quadratic equations (procedural)
Statement: Completing the square. Solution of quadratic equations.
Guidance: Solution of quadratic equations by factorisation, use of the formula, use of a calculator and completing the square: $ax^2+bx+c = a\left(x+\dfrac{b}{2a}\right)^2 + c - \dfrac{b^2}{4a}$.
Prerequisites: P1-1.3 Quadratic functions and graphs; P1-1.4 The discriminant
Glossary: completing the square, quadratic formula, factorising quadratics, solving quadratic equations, vertex form

### P1-1.6 — Simultaneous equations by substitution (procedural)
Statement: Solve simultaneous equations; analytical solution by substitution.
Prerequisites: P1-1.5 Solving quadratic equations
Glossary: simultaneous equations, substitution method, linear and quadratic simultaneous equations, elimination

### P1-1.7 — Interpreting inequalities graphically (conceptual)
Statement: Interpret linear and quadratic inequalities graphically.
Guidance: For example, $ax+b>cx+d$, $px^2+qx+r \geq 0$, $px^2+qx+r<ax+b$. Interpreting the third inequality as the range of $x$ for which the curve $y=px^2+qx+r$ is below the line with equation $y=ax+b$. Including inequalities with brackets and fractions; these are reducible to linear or quadratic inequalities, e.g. $\dfrac{a}{x}<b$ becomes $ax<bx^2$, $x \neq 0$.
Prerequisites: P1-1.3 Quadratic functions and graphs
Glossary: linear inequality, quadratic inequality, graphical interpretation, intersection of graphs, inequality regions

### P1-1.8 — Graphing inequality regions (procedural)
Statement: Represent linear and quadratic inequalities graphically.
Guidance: Represent linear and quadratic inequalities such as $y>x+r$ and $y>ax^2+bx+c$ graphically. Shading and use of dotted and solid line convention is required.
Prerequisites: P1-1.7 Interpreting inequalities graphically
Glossary: shading regions, dotted line convention, solid line convention, inequality regions, graphing inequalities

### P1-1.9 — Solving linear and quadratic inequalities (procedural)
Statement: Solutions of linear and quadratic inequalities.
Guidance: For example, solving $ax+b>cx+d$, $px^2+qx+r \geq 0$, $px^2+qx+r<ax+b$.
Prerequisites: P1-1.4 The discriminant; P1-1.7 Interpreting inequalities graphically
Glossary: solving inequalities, quadratic inequality, linear inequality, critical values, sign diagram

### P1-1.10 — Manipulating and factorising polynomials (procedural)
Statement: Algebraic manipulation of polynomials, including expanding brackets and collecting like terms, factorisation.
Guidance: Students should be able to expand brackets and collect like terms. Factorisation of polynomials of degree $n$, $n \leq 3$, e.g. $x^3+4x^2+3x$. The notation $f(x)$ may be used.
Prerequisites: none
Glossary: expanding brackets, collecting like terms, factorising polynomials, function notation f(x), polynomial

### P1-1.11 — Sketching curves and using intersections (procedural)
Statement: Graphs of functions; sketching curves defined by simple equations. Geometrical interpretation of algebraic solution of equations. Use of intersection points of graphs of functions to solve equations.
Guidance: Functions to include simple cubic functions and the reciprocal functions $y=\dfrac{k}{x}$ and $y=\dfrac{k}{x^2}$ with $x \neq 0$. Knowledge of the term asymptote is expected. Also, trigonometric graphs.
Prerequisites: P1-1.3 Quadratic functions and graphs; P1-1.10 Manipulating and factorising polynomials
Glossary: sketching curves, asymptote, reciprocal function, cubic function, intersection points, solving equations graphically

### P1-1.12 — Graph transformations (procedural)
Statement: Knowledge of the effect of simple transformations on the graph of $y=f(x)$ as represented by $y=af(x)$, $y=f(x)+a$, $y=f(x+a)$, $y=f(ax)$.
Guidance: Students should be able to apply one of these transformations to any of the above functions (quadratics, cubics, reciprocals, sine, cosine, and tangent) and sketch the resulting graphs. Given the graph of any function $y=f(x)$, students should be able to sketch the graph resulting from one of these transformations.
Prerequisites: P1-1.11 Sketching curves and using intersections
Glossary: graph transformations, translation, stretch, y = af(x), reflection

## Command words

- **Show that**: Prove a given result; every step must be shown (answer given).
- **Hence**: Use the previous result to reach the answer.
- **Hence or otherwise**: Using the previous result is expected, but any valid method earns the marks.
- **Find**: Work out; method marks need working.
- **Calculate**: Work out from given facts; show working.
- **Prove**: Give a complete logical argument.
- **Sketch**: Draw the shape with key features labelled (intercepts, asymptotes, turning points), not to scale.
- **Exact**: Give the answer in surd, fraction, pi or ln form, not a decimal.
- **Verify**: Show that given values satisfy the statement.
- **State**: Give the answer without working.
- **Explain**: Give reasons in context.
- **Interpret**: Explain the meaning of a result in the context of the question.
