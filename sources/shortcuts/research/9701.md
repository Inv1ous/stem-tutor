## 0. Habits that win marks everywhere

- **Carry a named quantity through the chain** `9/10`. When a calculation has several stages, label each intermediate as a species and location, such as $n(\mathrm{HCl\ in\ titre})$ or $n(\mathrm{CO_3^{2-}\ in\ flask})$, before substituting numbers. At the end, check that the requested answer refers to the same substance and sample size, rather than an intermediate $M_r$ or an aliquot amount. [src: examiner report 9701 s23 pp20,26]
- **Use an answer bound before calculating** `8/10`. Before a long calculation, write a chemical bound: percentage yield $\leq100\%$, a component's partial pressure $<P_{\mathrm{total}}$, or reacted moles $\leq$ initial moles. A result outside that bound usually locates a reversed ratio, wrong sample volume or unit conversion before you redo every line. [src: derived]

## 1. Moles, formulae and equations (topic 2)

- **Keep the aliquot fraction visible** `9/10`. If a stock solution of volume $V_s$ supplies an aliquot $V_a$, write $n_{\mathrm{stock}}=n_{\mathrm{aliquot}}(V_s/V_a)$ on its own line; this prevents a factor-of-ten error when the reaction ratio also differs from $1:1$. Check that $V_s/V_a>1$ and that the total stock amount exceeds the aliquot amount. [src: examiner report 9701 s23 p20]
- **Back-titration subtraction line** `9/10`. When an excess reagent is added and its remainder is titrated, write $n_{\mathrm{reacted}}=n_{\mathrm{added}}-n_{\mathrm{remaining}}$ before applying any coefficient or aliquot factor. Check that the remainder is less than the initial amount and that the calculated amount of analyte is positive. [src: examiner report 9701 June 2024 pp28-29]
- **Mass-loss ledger for heating** `8/10`. For a decomposition experiment, write three separate differences: $m_{\mathrm{sample}}=m_{\mathrm{vessel+sample}}-m_{\mathrm{vessel}}$, $m_{\mathrm{residue}}=m_{\mathrm{vessel+residue}}-m_{\mathrm{vessel}}$, and $m_{\mathrm{lost}}=m_{\mathrm{sample}}-m_{\mathrm{residue}}$. Use $m_{\mathrm{lost}}$ for moles of gas only when the stated reaction and conditions show that the lost mass is that gas; check $0<m_{\mathrm{lost}}<m_{\mathrm{sample}}$. [src: examiner report 9701 s23 pp19,26]
- **Balance unfamiliar equations in a fixed order** `8/10`. When a product is supplied but the full equation is not, balance the least common element first, then metals or other non-H/O atoms, then oxygen with $\mathrm{H_2O}$ if aqueous conditions permit, and hydrogen last. Recount every element and charge after the last coefficient; the examiner used this sequence for $\mathrm{Al_4C_3}$ reacting with alkali. [src: examiner report 9701 w23 p1]
- **Neutralisation first, equilibrium second** `8/10`. When strong acid or base is mixed with a weak acid, weak base or its salt, calculate $n(\mathrm{H^+})-n(\mathrm{OH^-})$ and the surviving amounts before choosing a pH equation. Check whether both conjugate partners remain (buffer), one weak species remains, or strong reagent is in excess. [src: derived]

## 2. Atomic structure and bonding (topics 1, 3)

- **Isotopologue masses by pair sums** `7/10`. For a diatomic molecule made from isotopes, list every unordered isotope pair, including mixed pairs, then add the two mass numbers and merge pairs with the same total. This catches low-abundance middle peaks such as $^{16}\mathrm O{}^{17}\mathrm O$ and $^{17}\mathrm O{}^{18}\mathrm O$; check the smallest and largest possible totals first. [src: examiner report 9701 w23 p4]
- **Compare similar molecules by holding one force fixed** `7/10`. If two small molecules have equal or nearly equal electron counts and comparable shapes, treat their London forces as roughly similar, then compare permanent dipoles to decide a boiling-point ordering. Check that the species really are comparable in size and shape before using this shortcut. [src: examiner report 9701 s23 p2]

## 3. States of matter

- **Dry-gas pressure over water** `7/10`. If a gas is collected over water and water-vapour pressure is given, use $p_{\mathrm{dry}}=p_{\mathrm{total}}-p_{\mathrm{H_2O}}$ in $pV=nRT$. Check that $p_{\mathrm{dry}}<p_{\mathrm{total}}$; using total pressure would overestimate the gas moles. [src: derived]

## 4. Energetics (topics 5 and A Level 23)

- **Read $\Delta S$ from a Gibbs plot** `7/10`. For a straight plot of $\Delta G$ against $T$ under conditions where $\Delta H$ and $\Delta S$ are approximately constant, $\Delta G=\Delta H-T\Delta S$ gives gradient $=-\Delta S$ and intercept $=\Delta H$. Check the axis units before reporting $\Delta S$, especially if $\Delta G$ is in $\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$. [src: derived]

## 5. Electrochemistry (topic 6 and A Level 24)

- **Potential is intensive** `8/10`. When balancing half-equations, multiply electron and species coefficients but never multiply the tabulated $E^\circ$ values; calculate $E^\circ_{\mathrm{cell}}$ from the two unchanged reduction potentials. A doubled overall equation must give the same $E^\circ_{\mathrm{cell}}$, which is a quick check on the working. [src: derived]
- **Check disproportionation with two reduction potentials** `7/10`. If the same intermediate oxidation state can be reduced and oxidised, pair its reduction half-equation with the reverse of its oxidation partner and calculate $E^\circ_{\mathrm{cell}}=E^\circ_{\mathrm{reduction}}-E^\circ_{\mathrm{reversed\ pair}}$. A positive value predicts thermodynamic feasibility under the stated standard conditions; check that the balanced sum really consumes the intermediate on both sides. [src: derived]

## 6. Equilibria (topic 7 and A Level 25)

- **Cancel a common total pressure** `9/10`. For a gas equilibrium with $\Delta n_{\mathrm{gas}}=0$, substitute $p_i=x_iP$ into $K_p$ and cancel $P$ before inserting numbers. The result depends only on equilibrium mole fractions, so changing total pressure alone cannot change $K_p$ or the equilibrium composition of an ideal-gas mixture at fixed temperature. [src: derived]
- **Inert gas: name the constraint** `8/10`. Adding inert gas at constant volume and temperature leaves each reacting gas's $p_i=n_iRT/V$ unchanged, so there is no immediate equilibrium shift; at constant total pressure, volume grows and reacting partial pressures fall. For the constant-pressure case, use the change in gas moles to predict the direction, and check whether the question specifies volume or pressure. [src: examiner report 9701 w23 p3]
- **Use stoichiometric symmetry before algebra** `8/10`. If an equilibrium forms $\mathrm H_2$ and $\mathrm I_2$ in a $1:1$ ratio from only $\mathrm{HI}$, set their equilibrium amounts equal immediately and obtain the remaining $p_{\mathrm{HI}}$ by subtraction from total pressure. Check that the two product partial pressures match before calculating $K_p$. [src: examiner report 9701 s23 p4]
- **Common-ion solubility check** `8/10`. For $\mathrm{MX(s)}\rightleftharpoons\mathrm{M^+(aq)}+\mathrm{X^-(aq)}$ in a solution already containing $c$ of $\mathrm X^-$, estimate $s\approx K_{sp}/c$ only if $s\ll c$. Test that inequality with the computed $s$; otherwise solve $s(c+s)=K_{sp}$. [src: derived]
- **Exact weak-acid root when the approximation fails** `7/10`. For a monoprotic acid initially at concentration $c$ with negligible water contribution, put $x=[\mathrm H^+]$ and solve $x^2+K_ax-K_ac=0$, giving $x=(\sqrt{K_a^2+4K_ac}-K_a)/2$. Use it when the approximate result $\sqrt{K_ac}$ is not small relative to $c$; check $0<x<c$. [src: derived]
- **Dilution direction check for weak acids** `7/10`. A dilution by factor $d$ raises a weak monoprotic acid's pH by approximately $\tfrac12\log_{10}d$ only while $[\mathrm H^+]\ll c$ and water ionisation is negligible. Thus a tenfold dilution gives about $0.5$ pH unit, providing a quick check against treating the acid as fully dissociated. [src: derived]

## 7. Kinetics (topic 8 and A Level 26)

- **Use rate ratios to remove $k$** `8/10`. When two experiments change several reactant concentrations at once, divide their rate laws so that $k$ cancels, then insert already known orders to solve the remaining order. Check by predicting one measured rate from a different row of the table. [src: derived]
- **Decimal-log Arrhenius slope** `8/10`. If the plotted axis is explicitly $\log_{10}k$ against $1/T$, the gradient is $-E_a/(2.303R)$; the familiar $-E_a/R$ applies to $\ln k$. Check the actual axis label and any equation supplied in the question before converting the gradient to $E_a$. [src: derived]
- **Logarithmic first-order interval** `7/10`. If first-order behaviour is established and two concentrations are known at times $t_1,t_2$, use $k=\ln(c_1/c_2)/(t_2-t_1)$ without needing a half-life or a plotted tangent. Check that $c_2<c_1$ for a disappearing reactant and that $k$ is positive with units of inverse time. [src: derived]
- **Rate units inherit the clock** `7/10`. Derive $k$ units from the rate unit printed in the question, including its actual time unit: a first-order rate in $\mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}\,min^{-1}}$ gives $k$ in $\mathrm{min^{-1}}$. Check that the time unit in your final $k$ matches the time unit used in the data or graph. [src: examiner report 9701 June 2024 paper 42]

## 8. Periodicity, Group 2, Group 17, nitrogen and sulfur (topics 9–12)

- **Use the comparison that cancels a common cause** `7/10`. In a periodicity explanation involving two species, first identify what is held nearly fixed (shell count, electron count or structure), then name the changing quantity (nuclear charge, shielding or bond strength). This keeps the explanation tied to the actual comparison; check that the chosen fixed property really is shared by both species. [src: derived]

## 9. Organic chemistry (AS 13–15, A Level 16–22)

- **Inventory every functional group before choosing a reagent** `9/10`. For a molecule with several groups, mark each site that the proposed reagent could attack and write all resulting changes before matching a product or test. For example, an oxidant may convert an aldehyde and a secondary alcohol in the same molecule; check that no unreacted group in your product should also have changed. [src: examiner report 9701 w23 p4]
- **Count ester choices by the carbon split** `8/10`. For an acyclic ester with $N$ total carbons, enumerate acid-side and alcohol-side carbon counts as $(1,N-1),(2,N-2),\ldots,(N-1,1)$, then list branch isomers within each split. Check that the carbonyl carbon stays on the acid side and that each drawn structure has exactly $N$ carbons. [src: examiner report 9701 w23 p4]
- **Collapse symmetric stereoisomers** `8/10`. After assigning E/Z at two double bonds or configurations at equivalent sites, rotate or reflect the whole molecule before counting a second structure as new. If a symmetric molecule makes $(E,Z)$ identical to $(Z,E)$, count that pair once; check the equivalence on drawn structures rather than labels alone. [src: examiner report 9701 w23 p4]
- **Check an ester or amide backwards by its bond** `8/10`. For a proposed condensation product, split the $\mathrm{C(=O){-}O}$ or $\mathrm{C(=O){-}N}$ bond and restore $\mathrm{OH}$ to the acyl fragment and H to the O/N fragment to identify possible reactants. Then recombine them and check that the product's carbon skeleton and number of links match the question. [src: derived]
- **Oxidative cleavage by carbon fate** `7/10`. For hot, concentrated acidified $\mathrm{KMnO_4}$ cleavage of an alkene, mark each alkene carbon: a carbon bearing H can become a carboxylic acid, while a carbon with no H can become a ketone; a terminal $\mathrm{CH_2}$ can be oxidised further to $\mathrm{CO_2}$. Cut each $\mathrm{C{=}C}$ only once and check the carbon count across all products. [src: derived]
- **Addition stoichiometry counts multiple bonds** `7/10`. For complete addition of bromine to isolated carbon–carbon double bonds, one mole of $\mathrm{Br_2}$ reacts per mole of $\mathrm{C{=}C}$, so $n(\mathrm{Br_2})/n(\mathrm{molecule})$ can reveal the number of double bonds. Check that the reaction specified is addition and that other groups do not also consume bromine under those conditions. [src: examiner report 9701 s23 p2]

## 10. Analysis (topic 22)

- **Use isotope spacing to infer charge** `7/10`. In an isotope cluster, adjacent isotopic mass differences appear $1/z$ units apart on the $m/z$ axis for ion charge $z$; a 2-unit mass difference therefore appears 1 unit apart for a $2+$ ion. Check the proposed charge against the ion formula and the full cluster before assigning a peak. [src: derived]

## 11. Inorganic tests and transition elements

- **Balance complex-ion equations as a ligand ledger** `8/10`. In ligand substitution, write the complete coordination spheres first, then place displaced ligands on the other side and balance any incoming ligands, water and charge. Check separately that metal atoms, ligand atoms and total charge match; a colour change alone does not determine coefficients. [src: derived]
- **Acidify before testing a hydrolysed halide** `8/10`. If a halogenoalkane was heated with excess aqueous $\mathrm{NaOH}$, acidify the sample with dilute $\mathrm{HNO_3}$ before adding $\mathrm{AgNO_3}$, otherwise residual $\mathrm{OH^-}$ can interfere. Confirm the halide from precipitate colour and ammonia solubility, then check that the organic hydrolysis product no longer contains halogen. [src: examiner report 9701 s23 p27]

## 12. Practical-paper shortcuts

- **Put units in table headings once** `9/10`. Before collecting readings, make a column for each raw measurement and put its unit in the heading, for example $V/\mathrm{cm^3}$ or $t/\mathrm s$; keep all entries in a column at instrument-appropriate precision. Check that calculated columns, such as titre, have their own heading and are not confused with raw readings. [src: examiner report 9701 s23 pp19,22; mark scheme 9701 s24 paper 33 p6]
- **Follow the stated instrument resolution** `8/10`. Record a thermometer marked in $1\,^{\circ}\mathrm C$ steps to the nearest $0.5\,^{\circ}\mathrm C$ when that is the allowed interpolation, and a coarse measuring cylinder to its actual readable division rather than invented decimal places. Check the apparatus scale before deciding precision; extra zeros do not make a measurement more accurate. [src: examiner report 9701 s23 pp25,28]
- **Make the graph use its space** `8/10`. Choose linear axis scales that spread the measured points over most of the grid, and draw a curve when the plotted trend curves rather than forcing a straight line. Check any requested intersection by projecting it carefully to both labelled scales and reporting the precision supported by the graph. [src: examiner report 9701 s23 p25]
- **Read a gradient from the fitted line** `8/10`. Choose two well-separated points on the line of best fit, not necessarily original data points, and calculate $\Delta y/\Delta x$ with both axis scale factors. Check the gradient's units as $[y]/[x]$ and its sign against the plotted slope. [src: examiner report 9701 June 2024 paper 5]
- **Trace systematic error through a formula** `8/10`. For an evaluation question, change the measured quantity first, then propagate that direction through each equation before stating whether the final answer rises or falls. For example, spilled residue makes measured mass loss too large, but the effect on a calculated $A_r$ must be determined from the actual subsequent formula. [src: examiner report 9701 s23 p19]
- **Name the observable event exactly** `8/10`. In qualitative analysis, distinguish a precipitate formed when solutions are mixed from a solid reagent that remains undissolved, and reserve “decolourised” for a solution becoming colourless. Check that the written observation describes a change seen in the tube, rather than merely naming the reagent's initial colour. [src: examiner report 9701 s23 p27]
- **Use the question's allowed test set** `7/10`. When asked to distinguish two unknowns with one test, choose a reagent that gives different observations and state both expected outcomes, then stop. Check any instruction forbidding further tests; an extra speculative test can contradict the identification. [src: examiner report 9701 s23 pp20,27]
