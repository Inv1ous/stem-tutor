## 0. Techniques that work across every topic

- **Build the results table from the intended graph** `9/10`. In a practical question, decide the $x$- and $y$-axis quantities before taking readings, then put both raw measurements and every transformed quantity needed for the graph in one table. This prevents a last-minute recalculation and makes the unit in each heading explicit, for example $I/\mathrm{mA}$ rather than just $\mathrm{mA}$. [src: Cambridge 9702 syllabus 2025–27 p45]
- **A false origin does not give an intercept** `9/10`. If the graph axis starts above zero, take a convenient point on the fitted line and calculate $c=y-mx$; the point where the line meets the drawn axis is not $c$. Substitute the result back into $y=mx+c$ at another point to catch a scale or sign slip. [src: Cambridge 9702 syllabus 2025–27 p46]
- **Compare a result with its allowed uncertainty** `9/10`. When two experimental values of a proposed constant are compared, calculate their percentage difference using the denominator requested in the question, then compare it with the stated percentage uncertainty. Finish with an explicit conclusion about whether the data support the relationship; a bare claim that the values are "close" cannot establish agreement. [src: Cambridge 9702 syllabus 2025–27 p47; 9702 s24 ms 34 p7]
- **Keep graph powers of ten in the gradient** `9/10`. For an axis labelled $y/(10^{-3}\,\mathrm m)$, a rise of $4.0$ on the paper represents $4.0\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm m$ in the physical gradient. Write the two coordinate differences with their axis factors before dividing, then check the resulting gradient unit as $[y]/[x]$. [src: examiner report 9702 June 2024 p60]
- **Subtraction follows decimal places** `9/10`. For a measured extension $L-L_0$ or temperature rise $T_2-T_1$, retain decimal places justified by the two readings; the result can have fewer significant figures than either original measurement. Use that difference, rather than the raw lengths or temperatures, when justifying precision in a quantity calculated from it. [src: 9702 s24 ms 33 p7; derived]
- **Make uncertainty match the measurement** `8/10`. When an edge or event is hard to judge, estimate uncertainty from that judgement as well as instrument resolution; a protractor's smallest division need not be the uncertainty of a fuzzy angle. Check that an uncertainty in time or length is plausible relative to the observed scatter and the measurement method. [src: examiner report 9702 June 2024 pp29, 38]
- **Recover intercept uncertainty from two lines** `8/10`. On a Paper 5 graph, calculate $c_{\rm best}=y-m_{\rm best}x$ and $c_{\rm worst}=y-m_{\rm worst}x$ using points on the respective drawn lines. Then use $|c_{\rm best}-c_{\rm worst}|$ as the absolute intercept uncertainty; gradient percentage uncertainty alone does not give intercept uncertainty. [src: Cambridge 9702 syllabus 2025–27 p52; examiner report 9702 June 2024 p60]
- **Take logarithms of numbers with declared units** `8/10`. In a table or graph write $\lg(L/\mathrm{cm})$ or $\ln(t/\mathrm s)$, so the logarithm's argument is dimensionless; the plotted logarithm itself has no unit. Use $10^c$ to undo an $\lg$ intercept and $e^c$ to undo an $\ln$ intercept, checking the chosen base before calculating a constant. [src: Cambridge 9702 syllabus 2025–27 p52; examiner report 9702 June 2024 p64]
- **Transform log-intercept uncertainty before quoting a constant** `8/10`. If a graph gives $c=\lg k$, calculate $k=10^c$ and compare it with $10^{c\pm\Delta c}$; for $c=\ln k$, use $e^{c\pm\Delta c}$. The percentage uncertainty in $k$ is not the percentage uncertainty in $c$, and the upper and lower changes need not be equal. [src: examiner report 9702 June 2015 p53; derived]
- **Fit the line, not the end readings** `8/10`. For a graph with scatter, draw the trend with points balanced on both sides and mark an anomalous point if it must be ignored. Read two easy grid intersections on the line, separated by more than half its length, for the gradient; they do not need to be measured data points. [src: Cambridge 9702 syllabus 2025–27 p46; examiner report 9702 June 2024 p60]
- **State how the graph tests the model** `8/10`. After naming axes, state the predicted line shape and intercept: for $y=kx$, the relationship is supported by a straight line through the origin, while $y=kx+c$ permits a nonzero intercept. State how the requested constant comes from the gradient or intercept; writing only $y=mx+c$ leaves the physical test unstated. [src: examiner report 9702 June 2024 p63]
- **Turn a limitation into a paired improvement** `8/10`. Name the measured quantity and why it is uncertain, then propose a change that removes that cause: an uncertain release position can use a fixed stop, and a hard-to-see instant can use video frames or a sensor. Check that the proposed apparatus measures the required quantity directly or gives a stated route to calculate it. [src: Cambridge 9702 syllabus 2025–27 p47; 9702 s24 ms 34 p8]
- **Two values test agreement, not a trend** `7/10`. If a practical question asks whether a formula holds, two values of its constant may be compared with a stated tolerance, but they cannot establish the shape of the whole relationship. Suggest several distinct independent-variable settings and a graph when evaluating the range of validity. [src: 9702 s24 ms 34 p8]
- **State what a light gate actually times** `7/10`. In a Paper 5 velocity plan, give the interrupting flag's length $l$ and its blocking time $\Delta t$, then calculate $v\approx l/\Delta t$ for a sufficiently short flag. Check that $l$ is short enough that speed does not change appreciably while it passes the gate. [src: examiner report 9702 November 2012 p45; derived]

## 1. Kinematics, dynamics and forces (AS 2–4)

- **Set a signed-motion ledger** `9/10`. Before using suvat or momentum, choose a positive direction and write each velocity and acceleration with a sign; a thrown object's velocity changes sign at the top even though its speed is positive. Substitute the signed values into one equation and check that the answer's direction agrees with the sketch. [src: derived]
- **Split motion at a turning point** `8/10`. If an object reverses direction, solve for the turning time using $v=0$ and treat the two legs separately when asked for total distance. Displacement can be calculated in one signed step, whereas distance is the sum of the magnitudes of the legs. [src: derived]
- **Use the absolute graph areas for distance** `8/10`. On a velocity–time graph that crosses the time axis, add areas with signs for displacement but add their absolute values for distance travelled. Distance must be at least the magnitude of displacement, giving an immediate check. [src: derived]

## 2. Energy, work and power (AS 5)

- **Use input power to bypass changing acceleration** `8/10`. When a motor delivers constant useful power $P$ for time $t$, its energy output is $Pt$ even while the driving force $P/v$ and acceleration change. Equate $Pt$ to the stated gains in kinetic and potential energy plus losses, checking that all terms are energies in joules. [src: derived]

## 3. Deformation of solids (AS 6)

- **Correct diameter before squaring it** `7/10`. If a micrometer has zero reading $z$, use corrected diameter $d=d_{\rm read}-z$ before calculating a wire's area $A=\pi d^2/4$; correcting the area afterward is invalid. Check several positions and perpendicular orientations if the wire may not be uniform or round, because the squared diameter magnifies any error. [src: derived]

## 4. Waves and superposition (AS 7–8)

- **Check refraction with an invariant ratio** `8/10`. Across a boundary, $f$ is unchanged, so $v_1/v_2=\lambda_1/\lambda_2=\sin\theta_1/\sin\theta_2$ for angles measured from the normal. If the ray bends towards the normal, both speed and wavelength must fall; this catches an inverted refractive-index ratio. [src: derived]
- **Gate total internal reflection by both conditions** `7/10`. A critical angle exists only for incidence from higher to lower refractive index, with $\sin c=n_2/n_1$ when $n_1>n_2$. Before using an inverse sine, check the direction of travel and then whether the incident angle measured from the normal exceeds $c$; equality gives a refracted ray along the boundary. [src: derived]

## 5. Electricity and DC circuits (AS 9–10)

- **Use node voltages for crossed branches** `8/10`. Label one node $0\,\mathrm V$ and calculate each other node's potential from known cell rises and resistor drops; the p.d. across a component is then the difference of its end-node values. This is especially useful when a bridge or multiple loops make a memorised potential-divider formula ambiguous; the signed p.d.s around any closed loop must sum to zero. [src: derived]
- **Find a nonlinear operating point by intersection** `7/10`. For a lamp or diode with an $I$–$V$ curve in series with a source of emf $E$ and fixed resistance $R$, draw or solve the load line $V=E-IR$. The physical current and component p.d. occur where that line intersects the device curve; check that $0\leq V\leq E$ for a passive device in this simple circuit. [src: derived]

## 7. A Level: circular motion and gravitation (12–13)

- **Use centre-to-centre radius in orbit calculations** `9/10`. For an object at height $h$ above a spherical body's surface, put $r=R+h$ into gravity, orbital speed and period formulae; $h$ alone is not the orbital radius. Check that the calculated $r$ is larger than $R$ and that the near-surface limit gives $r\to R$. [src: derived]
- **Check the sign of gravitational transfer** `8/10`. With zero potential at infinity, $U=-GMm/r$ becomes less negative as an object moves outward, so an external agent must supply positive energy $\Delta U=GMm(1/r_1-1/r_2)$ for $r_2>r_1$. This sign check prevents treating the negative potential as a negative energy cost. [src: derived]

## 8. A Level: gases and thermal physics (14–15)

- **Check a $p$–$V$ cycle by its direction** `8/10`. Over a closed cycle, $\Delta U=0$; clockwise traversal gives positive net work done by the gas equal to the enclosed area, and anticlockwise traversal gives negative net work by the gas. Heat added minus heat rejected must have the same sign and magnitude as that net work. [src: derived]

## 9. A Level: oscillations (16)

- **Make a quarter-cycle state table** `7/10`. For SHM starting at $x=+A$, mark states at $t=0,T/4,T/2,3T/4$: displacement $+A,0,-A,0$ and velocity $0,-v_{\max},0,+v_{\max}$. The signs expose a wrong phase or acceleration direction without differentiating the whole expression. [src: derived]

## 10. A Level: electric fields and capacitance (17–18)

- **Ask whether the capacitor stays connected** `9/10`. When plate spacing or dielectric changes, a capacitor connected to an ideal battery keeps $V$ fixed, whereas an isolated charged capacitor keeps $Q$ fixed. Use $U=\tfrac12CV^2$ in the first case and $U=Q^2/(2C)$ in the second; checking which quantity is constrained prevents a reversed energy prediction. [src: derived]
- **Separate electric field from potential at symmetry points** `8/10`. At the midpoint between two equal like point charges, their field vectors cancel but their scalar potentials add; between equal unlike charges, the potentials cancel while the fields reinforce. Sketch arrows for $\mathbf E$ and add signed numbers for $V$ before calculating either. [src: derived]

## 11. A Level: magnetism, induction, AC (19–21)

- **Magnetic force changes direction, not speed** `8/10`. For a charge moving in a magnetic field alone, $\mathbf F=q\mathbf v\times\mathbf B$ is perpendicular to the instantaneous velocity, so its work and change in kinetic energy are zero. A computed increase in speed requires an electric field or another force; only the path direction can change under the magnetic force. [src: derived]
- **Integrate an induced-emf pulse** `8/10`. For a fixed coil with flux linkage changing between two states, the signed area under an emf–time graph equals $-\Delta(N\Phi)$, even if the pulse height varies. The area of a return pulse must undo the first area if the flux linkage ends at its initial value. [src: derived]

## 12. A Level: quantum, nuclear, medical, astronomy (22–25)

- **Subtract two photoelectric experiments** `8/10`. For the same emitting surface, subtract $eV_s=hf-\Phi$ at two frequencies to eliminate the unknown work function: $e(V_{s,2}-V_{s,1})=h(f_2-f_1)$. Check that higher frequency gives the larger stopping-potential magnitude, provided both frequencies exceed threshold. [src: derived]
- **Cancel electrons in atomic-mass calculations** `8/10`. If a nuclear reaction has the same total proton number on both sides, neutral atomic masses may be used throughout because the total electron masses cancel. If positrons or bare nuclei enter the bookkeeping, first check the particle convention and account for electron masses consistently before taking the small mass difference. [src: derived]
- **Use thickness differences to remove incident intensity** `8/10`. For one material with constant attenuation coefficient, measurements at $x_1$ and $x_2$ satisfy $\mu=\ln(I_1/I_2)/(x_2-x_1)$, so the unknown initial intensity cancels. Check that $I_2<I_1$ when $x_2>x_1$ and the resulting $\mu$ is positive. [src: derived]
- **Combine brightness and temperature before solving for stellar radius** `7/10`. From $F=L/(4\pi d^2)$ and $L=4\pi r^2\sigma T^4$, obtain $r/d=\sqrt{F/(\sigma T^4)}$ directly. This gives the angular-radius scale without calculating luminosity first; check that a hotter star with the same received flux has a smaller $r/d$. [src: derived]
