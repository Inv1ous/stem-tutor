## 1. Kinematics in a straight line (suvat and graphs)

- **Pick the right suvat in two seconds** `10/10`. Write the five letters $s,u,v,a,t$; tick the three you know and the one you want; pick the equation that **lacks the fifth**.

| Missing | Use |
|---|---|
| $s$ | $v=u+at$ |
| $v$ | $s=ut+\tfrac12at^2$ |
| $a$ | $s=\tfrac12(u+v)t$ |
| $t$ | $v^2=u^2+2as$ |
| $u$ | $s=vt-\tfrac12at^2$ |

- **Average velocity trick** `9/10`. For constant acceleration, average velocity is $\tfrac12(u+v)$, so $s=\tfrac12(u+v)t$. A particle slows from $20$ to $0$ in $5\ \mathrm s$: $s=\tfrac12(20)(5)=50\ \mathrm m$, no $a$ needed.
- **Vertical motion is symmetric** `9/10`. Thrown up at $u$: time to top $=\dfrac ug$, max height $=\dfrac{u^2}{2g}$, **same speed at the same height going up and down**, total flight time (back to start) $=\dfrac{2u}g$. Landing speed from height $h$ (dropped) $=\sqrt{2gh}$.
- **Choose "up" as positive and keep $a=-g$** `9/10`. A single equation covers the whole flight. A quadratic in $t$ gives two times: the up-pass and the down-pass.
- **Successive equal intervals from rest** `8/10`. Distances covered in successive equal times are in the ratio $1:3:5:7:\dots$ And $s\propto t^2$.
- **Distance in the $n$th second** `7/10`: $s_n=u+a\left(n-\tfrac12\right)$ (velocity at the **midpoint** of that second). Difference of distances in consecutive equal intervals $T$: $\Delta s=aT^2$ — find $a$ from two distances with no $u$.
- **Accelerate then decelerate over a fixed distance** `8/10`. From rest to rest in distance $s$, accelerating at $a$ then decelerating at $b$: top speed $V^2=\dfrac{2s\,ab}{a+b}$, total time $T=\sqrt{\dfrac{2s(a+b)}{ab}}$. Check with $a=b$: $V^2=sa$, $T=2\sqrt{s/a}$ ✓.
- **v–t graph: area $=$ displacement, gradient $=$ acceleration** `10/10`. Break the area into triangles and rectangles; a trapezium is $\tfrac12(a+b)h$. **Total distance** counts areas below the axis as positive.
- **Trapezium shortcut for a three-stage journey** `9/10`. Times $t_1,t_2,t_3$ with top speed $V$: distance $=\tfrac12V(T+t_2)$ where $T$ is total time and $t_2$ the constant-speed time (area of a trapezium: $\tfrac12(\text{parallel sides})\times V$).
- **Average speed** `7/10` $=\dfrac{\text{total distance}}{\text{total time}}$, **not** the mean of speeds. Two equal distances at $v_1,v_2$: $\dfrac{2v_1v_2}{v_1+v_2}$ (harmonic mean).
- **Meeting and chase** `9/10`. Write both displacements from a common origin and time-zero, then equate: $s_1(t)=s_2(t)$. If one starts later by $T$, use $(t-T)$. Meeting while moving in opposite directions: **sum of distances** = initial gap; same direction: **difference** = initial gap.
- **Relative velocity** `8/10`. For two bodies moving along a line, relative speed $=|v_1-v_2|$ (same direction) or $v_1+v_2$ (towards each other). Time to catch up $=\dfrac{\text{gap}}{\text{relative speed}}$ — no equations of motion needed.
- **Stopping distance** `7/10`: $\dfrac{u^2}{2a}$; doubling speed quadruples it. Reaction time adds $u\times t_r$.
- **Dropped and thrown at the same time** `6/10`. Relative acceleration is zero, so the gap between them changes at constant rate $u_1-u_2$.
- **Check units and signs** `6/10`. A positive $s$ with negative $v$ at the end means "gone up then fallen back past its start": fine. A negative time is rejected.

### With calculus
- **$v=\dfrac{ds}{dt}$, $a=\dfrac{dv}{dt}$; integrate back with a constant** `9/10`. Use the starting condition ($t=0$, known $s$ or $v$) immediately to fix the constant.
- **Maximum or minimum speed** `8/10` when $a=0$ (but check the endpoints).
- **Displacement vs distance** `9/10`: if the particle turns round, split at $v=0$ and add the magnitudes.
- **Particle at rest** `8/10` means $v=0$; **instantaneous** at a given time means substitute; **returns to start** means $s=0$ again.
- **Power rule in time:** $\int t^n=\dfrac{t^{n+1}}{n+1}$; **definite** integral $\int_{t_1}^{t_2}v\,dt$ gives displacement.

## 2. Vectors and 2D motion

- **Position of a body with constant velocity:** $\mathbf r=\mathbf r_0+\mathbf vt$ `10/10`. Two bodies meet when $\mathbf r_A=\mathbf r_B$ **at the same $t$**: equate the $\mathbf i$ and $\mathbf j$ parts and check both give the same $t$. If the times differ, they only cross paths.
- **Closest approach** `9/10`. Relative position $\mathbf d(t)=\mathbf d_0+\mathbf vt$ where $\mathbf v$ is the relative velocity. Minimise $|\mathbf d|^2$: $t=-\dfrac{\mathbf d_0\cdot\mathbf v}{|\mathbf v|^2}$, and the least distance is $\dfrac{|d_{0x}v_y-d_{0y}v_x|}{|\mathbf v|}$. Quicker than differentiating a quadratic.
- **Speed and bearing** `9/10`. Speed $=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$ for $a\mathbf i+b\mathbf j$. Direction angle from east $\theta=\tan^{-1}\dfrac ba$ (adjust quadrant). **Bearing $=90^\circ-\theta$** (mod $360^\circ$), north is $000^\circ$.
- **Unit vector** `7/10`: $\hat{\mathbf a}=\dfrac{\mathbf a}{|\mathbf a|}$. Vector of given magnitude $k$ in the direction of $\mathbf a$: $k\hat{\mathbf a}$.
- **Parallel vectors** `8/10`: $\mathbf a=\lambda\mathbf b$, so $\dfrac{a_x}{b_x}=\dfrac{a_y}{b_y}$. **"Moving parallel to $\mathbf i+2\mathbf j$"**: components in ratio $1:2$.
- **Distance between two position vectors** `7/10`: $|\mathbf a-\mathbf b|$. Midpoint: $\dfrac{\mathbf a+\mathbf b}2$.
- **Pythagorean angle triples** `7/10`: $3\!:\!4\!:\!5$, $5\!:\!12\!:\!13$, $8\!:\!15\!:\!17$, $7\!:\!24\!:\!25$ let you replace $\cos\theta,\sin\theta$ by fractions without a calculator. "$\tan\theta=\tfrac34$" means $\sin\theta=\tfrac35$, $\cos\theta=\tfrac45$.
- **Resolve into components** `10/10`: force $F$ at angle $\theta$ to a direction has component $F\cos\theta$ along it and $F\sin\theta$ perpendicular. **Pick the angle to the axis you resolve along**, and sketch to check whether it's $\cos$ or $\sin$.
- **Resultant of two forces at angle $\theta$** `7/10` (beyond spec): $R=\sqrt{F_1^2+F_2^2+2F_1F_2\cos\theta}$, and its angle with $F_1$ is $\tan^{-1}\dfrac{F_2\sin\theta}{F_1+F_2\cos\theta}$. Right angles: just Pythagoras.
- **Constant velocity change** `6/10`: $\mathbf v=\mathbf u+\mathbf at$ works component by component, and so do all suvat equations (not $v^2=u^2+2as$ in vector form unless you use dot products).
- **Newton's second law in vectors** `8/10`: $\mathbf F=m\mathbf a$, so $\mathbf a=\dfrac{\mathbf F}m$ and velocity after $t$ is $\mathbf u+\dfrac{\mathbf F}mt$.
- **Lami's theorem (beyond spec)** `6/10`: three forces in equilibrium: $\dfrac{F_1}{\sin\alpha}=\dfrac{F_2}{\sin\beta}=\dfrac{F_3}{\sin\gamma}$ where $\alpha$ is the angle between $F_2$ and $F_3$ (opposite $F_1$). One line for a three-force triangle.
- **Triangle of forces** `6/10`: three forces in equilibrium form a closed triangle: use sine/cosine rules.

## 3. Dynamics, connected particles and friction

- **Write $F=ma$ along the motion and equilibrium across it** `10/10`. Resolve **in the direction of acceleration** (forces helping $-$ forces opposing $=ma$), and **perpendicular** with no acceleration (they balance).
- **Connected particles: treat the whole system first** `10/10`. For two particles joined by a string moving together, $F_{\text{net}}=(m_1+m_2)a$ gives $a$ immediately; then use **one** particle to find the tension. Internal tensions cancel.
- **Pulley over a smooth peg (hanging masses $m_1<m_2$)** `10/10`:
$$a=\frac{(m_2-m_1)g}{m_1+m_2},\qquad T=\frac{2m_1m_2g}{m_1+m_2}.$$
Check: $m_1=m_2\Rightarrow a=0$, $T=mg$ ✓.
- **Particle on a smooth table pulled by a hanging mass** `9/10`: $a=\dfrac{m_2g}{m_1+m_2}$, $T=\dfrac{m_1m_2g}{m_1+m_2}$. With friction $\mu$ on the table: $a=\dfrac{m_2g-\mu m_1g}{m_1+m_2}$.
- **Rough slope** `10/10`. Resolve perpendicular: $R=mg\cos\theta$. Down the slope $a=g(\sin\theta-\mu\cos\theta)$. **Equilibrium on the point of slipping:** $\tan\theta=\mu$ (angle of friction). The coefficient $\mu=\tan\theta$ is a one-line "find $\mu$" question.
- **Smooth slope** `9/10`: $a=g\sin\theta$ regardless of the mass. Speed at the bottom of a smooth slope of height $h$ is $\sqrt{2gh}$ whatever the slope angle.
- **Friction: $F\le\mu R$, equality only in limiting equilibrium or while sliding** `10/10`. Static and not about to slip: $F$ equals whatever balances the other forces. **Always find $R$ first**, and remember a vertical component of the applied force changes $R$: pulling up at angle $\alpha$: $R=mg-P\sin\alpha$; pushing down: $R=mg+P\sin\alpha$.
- **Best angle to pull a block (beyond spec)** `6/10`. Minimum pulling force to move a block of weight $W$ on rough ground is at $\tan\alpha=\mu$: $P_{\min}=\dfrac{\mu W}{\sqrt{1+\mu^2}}$.
- **Lifts (apparent weight)** `8/10`. Lift accelerating **upwards** at $a$: reaction $R=m(g+a)$; downwards: $R=m(g-a)$. Free fall: $R=0$ ("weightless").
- **String goes slack** `8/10`. When a hanging mass hits the floor, tension disappears: the other particle then decelerates (rough table) or moves at constant velocity (smooth). Find the speed at the moment of impact with suvat, then restart $F=ma$.
- **Tension in a string over a pulley is the same on both sides** `7/10` (light string, smooth pulley). A **light** rod or string has no mass, so the two end forces are equal.
- **Two-pulley/inclined plus hanging mass** `8/10`: write $F=ma$ **for each particle with a consistent positive direction along the string**, then add the equations to eliminate $T$.
- **Constant force from a velocity change** `7/10`: $F=\dfrac{m(v-u)}t$ (impulse–momentum). **Impulse $=Ft=mv-mu$** (vector; sign the directions).
- **Collisions (momentum conserved)** `8/10`: $m_1u_1+m_2u_2=m_1v_1+m_2v_2$. Coalescing: $v=\dfrac{m_1u_1+m_2u_2}{m_1+m_2}$. **Direction of an unknown velocity: assume positive and let the sign speak.**
- **Restitution (beyond spec)** `5/10`: speed of separation $=e\times$ speed of approach.
- **Work–energy (beyond spec)** `7/10`: $\tfrac12mv^2-\tfrac12mu^2=$ net work. Great when the path is not straight and time doesn't matter: $v^2=u^2+2gh$ for a free fall of height $h$ regardless of the path on a smooth surface.
- **Power (beyond spec)** `5/10`: $P=Fv$; at maximum speed driving force $=$ resistance.

## 4. Statics and moments

- **Moments around the point that kills unknowns** `10/10`. Take moments about the pivot where the unknown force acts, so its moment is zero. Moment $=$ force $\times$ **perpendicular** distance.
- **Equilibrium: three equations** `10/10`. Forces vertically, forces horizontally, moments about a point; **use the one that isolates the unknown**.
- **Tilting condition** `9/10`. A beam is about to tilt about a support when the **reaction at the other support is zero**. Put $R=0$ and take moments about the pivot.
- **Uniform beam:** weight acts at the **midpoint** `9/10`. A non-uniform one: weight at the centre of mass (given distance).
- **Ladder problems** `7/10`. Moments about the foot (kills friction and normal reaction there), vertical balance for friction with the ground: $F=\mu R$ at limiting equilibrium; the wall reaction is horizontal if the wall is smooth.
- **Beam with a person or particle walking** `7/10`. Find the extreme position by letting a reaction $\to0$: gives the greatest distance before tilting.
- **Equal and opposite moment checks** `6/10`: clockwise moments $=$ anticlockwise moments. If you get a negative reaction you've chosen the wrong direction or the beam has tilted.
- **Centre of mass of two masses** `6/10`: $\bar x=\dfrac{m_1x_1+m_2x_2}{m_1+m_2}$ — equivalent to moments about the origin.

## 5. Modelling vocabulary (worth marks)

- **"Light"** `6/10` = mass negligible so tension is equal throughout. **"Smooth"** = no friction. **"Inextensible"** = same acceleration magnitude along the string. **"Particle"** = no size, no rotation. **"Uniform"** = mass evenly distributed so weight is at the midpoint. **"Rough"** = friction present.
- **Modelling assumptions answer:** one clear sentence. "Air resistance is negligible", "the rod is rigid", "the string doesn't stretch", "the pulley is smooth so the tension is the same on both sides", "$g$ is constant".

## 6. Fast checks

- **Dimensions:** every term in an equation has the same units (N, m, s).
- **Limiting cases:** set $\mu=0$, equal masses, $\theta=0^\circ$ or $90^\circ$ in your final formula and see if it matches intuition.
- **Sign check:** positive acceleration should point the way you chose; a negative answer means the opposite direction (not an error).
- **Energy sanity:** a falling body's speed from height $h$ is $\sqrt{2gh}$; a quick cross-check for $v^2=u^2+2as$ answers.
