## 0. Techniques that work across every topic

- **Ratio / proportionality method** `10/10`. Write the formula once, mark what is constant, and scale. If $F\propto\dfrac1{r^2}$ and $r$ triples, $F$ becomes $\dfrac19$. Saves whole calculations and removes rounding errors. Typical structures: $\propto x^2$ (energy of a spring, power $\propto I^2$, intensity $\propto A^2$), $\propto\dfrac1{x^2}$ (inverse square), $\propto\sqrt x$ ($v\propto\sqrt T$).
- **Dimensional / unit checking** `9/10`. Express every term in base units (kg, m, s, A, K, mol). Homogeneity check catches missing factors. Derive a unit by rearranging: $\mathrm{N}=\mathrm{kg\,m\,s^{-2}}$, $\mathrm{J}=\mathrm{kg\,m^2\,s^{-2}}$, $\mathrm{W}=\mathrm{kg\,m^2\,s^{-3}}$, $\mathrm{Pa}=\mathrm{kg\,m^{-1}\,s^{-2}}$, $\mathrm{V}=\mathrm{kg\,m^2\,s^{-3}\,A^{-1}}$, $\Omega=\mathrm{kg\,m^2\,s^{-3}\,A^{-2}}$, $\mathrm{C}=\mathrm{A\,s}$, $\mathrm{T}=\mathrm{kg\,s^{-2}\,A^{-1}}$, $\mathrm{Hz}=\mathrm{s^{-1}}$.
- **Uncertainty rules** `10/10`. **Add or subtract:** add the *absolute* uncertainties. **Multiply or divide:** add the *percentage* (fractional) uncertainties. **Power $n$:** multiply the percentage uncertainty by $|n|$. $\sqrt x$: halve it. Repeated reading: $\pm\tfrac12(\text{max}-\text{min})$. A single timing of many oscillations: divide the uncertainty by the number of oscillations.
- **Graph gradient uncertainty** `9/10`. Draw the best-fit line and the **steepest and shallowest acceptable lines** (through the error bars); uncertainty $=\tfrac12(m_{\max}-m_{\min})$. Use a large triangle, at least half the line length.
- **Making a straight line** `10/10`. $y=ax^n\Rightarrow\lg y=n\lg x+\lg a$ (gradient $n$, intercept $\lg a$). $y=ae^{kx}\Rightarrow\ln y=kx+\ln a$ (gradient $k$). Know the graph for $T^2$ vs $L$, $1/v$ vs $u$, $\sqrt{}$ forms.
- **Significant figures** `8/10`: final answer to the **fewest** sig figs of the data (usually 2 or 3); never round mid-calculation. Keep 4 s.f. in working.
- **Estimation anchors** `8/10`: person mass $\sim70\ \mathrm{kg}$, height $1.7\ \mathrm m$, walking speed $1.4\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, atmospheric pressure $1.0\times10^5\ \mathrm{Pa}$, density of water $1000\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$, AA cell $1.5\ \mathrm V$, mains $230\ \mathrm V$ (UK) / lab $\sim12\ \mathrm V$, wavelength of visible light $400$–$700\ \mathrm{nm}$, sound speed in air $340\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, $c=3.0\times10^8\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, speed of a car $30\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, mass of an apple $0.1\ \mathrm{kg}$, electron mass $9.11\times10^{-31}\ \mathrm{kg}$.
- **Vectors** `9/10`. Resultant by Pythagoras if perpendicular; components $F\cos\theta$ and $F\sin\theta$ (angle **to** the axis you are resolving along). **Closed triangle of forces** for equilibrium.
- **"Explain" mark patterns** `8/10`: state the principle ($\rightarrow$ cause $\rightarrow$ effect with the physical quantity named). E.g. "resistance increases, so current falls, so $V$ across the other component increases".

## 1. Kinematics, dynamics and forces (AS 2–4)

### Kinematics
- **Choose the suvat equation by the missing variable** `10/10`: see below, identical to the maths M1 table (no $s$: $v=u+at$; no $a$: $s=\tfrac12(u+v)t$; no $t$: $v^2=u^2+2as$; no $v$: $s=ut+\tfrac12at^2$).
- **Area under $v$–$t$ is displacement; gradient is acceleration** `10/10`. Area under $a$–$t$ is the change in velocity. Under a force–time graph, impulse (change in momentum).
- **Vertical throw symmetry** `9/10`: time up $=\dfrac ug$, max height $\dfrac{u^2}{2g}$, equal speeds at equal heights.
- **Projectiles** `10/10`. Horizontal: $s_x=u\cos\theta\,t$ (constant velocity). Vertical: suvat with $a=-g$. For a launch and landing at the same level: time of flight $T=\dfrac{2u\sin\theta}g$, range $R=\dfrac{u^2\sin2\theta}g$, max height $H=\dfrac{u^2\sin^2\theta}{2g}$. **Complementary angles ($\theta$ and $90^\circ-\theta$) give equal range; $45^\circ$ is the maximum.**
- **Horizontally launched from a height $h$** `8/10`: $t=\sqrt{2h/g}$; horizontal range $=u\sqrt{2h/g}$; **time of flight doesn't depend on horizontal speed**.
- **Terminal velocity** `8/10`: resultant force $0$, $a=0$; weight $=$ drag (+ upthrust). Approached by a decreasing-gradient $v$–$t$ curve.
- **Relative velocity** `8/10`: $\mathbf v_{AB}=\mathbf v_A-\mathbf v_B$; rain on a moving car; boat on a river (cross the river in the shortest time by aiming straight across; reach the point opposite by aiming upstream at $\sin\theta=\dfrac{v_{\text{river}}}{v_{\text{boat}}}$).
- **Average speed over two equal distances** `7/10`: harmonic mean $\dfrac{2v_1v_2}{v_1+v_2}$.

### Dynamics and momentum
- **$F=\dfrac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}$** `10/10`. Area under $F$–$t$ is impulse. Force on a surface by a stream (hose, hail, sand): $F=\dfrac{\Delta m}{\Delta t}\Delta v=\rho Av\times\Delta v$ — **flow rate times velocity change**.
- **Kinetic energy and momentum** `9/10`: $E_k=\dfrac{p^2}{2m}$; so for equal momenta the lighter object has more KE; for equal KE the heavier has more momentum ($p=\sqrt{2mE_k}$).
- **Collisions** `10/10`. Momentum is always conserved (vector). Elastic: KE conserved $\Leftrightarrow$ **relative speed of approach $=$ relative speed of separation**. **Equal masses, one at rest, elastic, head-on: velocities exchange.** Perfectly inelastic: $v=\dfrac{m_1u_1+m_2u_2}{m_1+m_2}$.
- **Explosions from rest** `7/10`: total momentum $0$, so $m_1v_1=-m_2v_2$, and the lighter mass gets the larger KE (energy $\propto\dfrac1m$).
- **Newton's third law pairs** `8/10`: same type of force, same magnitude, opposite direction, **on different bodies**. Weight and normal reaction on a book are **not** a pair.
- **Connected objects / lifts** `8/10`: treat the system first, then split. Lift $R=m(g\pm a)$. **Weight of a person in a lift** = reaction.
- **Moments** `10/10`. Take moments about a point where an unknown force acts to remove it. $\sum$ clockwise $=\sum$ anticlockwise and $\sum F=0$. **Couple** $=F\times$ perpendicular separation. Uniform body: weight at the midpoint.

### Density, pressure, upthrust
- **$p=h\rho g$ is the *extra* pressure** `10/10` from a liquid column. Total pressure $=p_{\text{atm}}+h\rho g$. The pressure at a given depth is independent of container shape. **Two connected columns at equal pressure:** $h_1\rho_1=h_2\rho_2$ (U-tube with two liquids).
- **Upthrust $=\rho_{\text{fluid}}Vg$ (weight of fluid displaced)** `10/10`. Floating: upthrust $=$ weight, so **fraction submerged $=\dfrac{\rho_{\text{object}}}{\rho_{\text{fluid}}}$**. Apparent weight $=W-\text{upthrust}$.
- **Hydraulic press** `7/10`: $\dfrac{F_1}{A_1}=\dfrac{F_2}{A_2}$; distances moved are in the **inverse** ratio (energy conserved).
- **Pressure from a force over an area** `8/10`: $p=\dfrac FA$; for a person on one foot vs two, area halves so pressure doubles.
- **Density of a mixture** `7/10`: $\rho=\dfrac{m_1+m_2}{V_1+V_2}$ (not the mean of densities).

## 2. Energy, work and power (AS 5)

- **Work $W=Fs\cos\theta$; area under $F$–$x$** `10/10`. Zero work if the force is perpendicular to the displacement. Net work $=\Delta E_k$.
- **Energy conservation first, kinematics second** `10/10`. $mgh=\tfrac12mv^2$ gives $v=\sqrt{2gh}$ for any smooth path; **mass cancels**. With losses: $E_{\text{in}}=E_{\text{out}}+E_{\text{lost}}$.
- **Power $P=Fv$** `10/10`. At **maximum speed** driving force $=$ resistive force, so $v_{\max}=\dfrac P{F_R}$. Uphill: $P=(F_R+mg\sin\theta)v$.
- **Efficiency** `8/10` $=\dfrac{\text{useful output}}{\text{total input}}$. For a chain of machines, multiply efficiencies.
- **Fluid flow: wind and water** `10/10`. Mass flow rate $\dfrac{\Delta m}{\Delta t}=\rho Av$, so power in the flow $P=\tfrac12\rho Av^3$ (**$P\propto v^3$**, $\propto r^2$). Hydro: $P=\rho gh\times\dfrac{\Delta V}{\Delta t}$ (volume rate). Doubling wind speed gives eight times the power.
- **Falling in an energy-conserving loop** `8/10`: use $mgh$ differences, not kinematics; the loop-the-loop minimum release height is $2.5R$ (just completing a vertical circle).
- **Gravitational PE change** `8/10`: $\Delta E_p=mg\Delta h$, only the vertical height matters.
- **Kinetic energy ratio** `8/10`: $E_k\propto v^2$; speed doubles, KE quadruples, braking distance quadruples ($d=\dfrac{v^2}{2a}$).
- **Energy per unit of time units** `6/10`: $1\ \mathrm{kWh}=3.6\times10^6\ \mathrm J$.

## 3. Deformation of solids (AS 6)

- **Hooke's law, $F=kx$; energy $E=\tfrac12Fx=\tfrac12kx^2$** `10/10`. **Doubling the extension quadruples the energy.** Energy $=$ area under the $F$–$x$ graph (also for non-linear curves, count squares).
- **Springs combined** `9/10`. **Series:** $\dfrac1k=\dfrac1{k_1}+\dfrac1{k_2}$ (each carries the full load; extensions add). **Parallel:** $k=k_1+k_2$ (load shared, extension equal). Same logic as capacitors, opposite to resistors.
- **Stress, strain, Young modulus** `10/10`. $\sigma=\dfrac FA$, $\varepsilon=\dfrac xL$, $E=\dfrac\sigma\varepsilon=\dfrac{FL}{Ax}$. The gradient of stress–strain is $E$; **$k=\dfrac{EA}L$**. Strain energy per unit volume $=\tfrac12\sigma\varepsilon$ ($=\dfrac{\sigma^2}{2E}$).
- **Wire diameter: area $\propto d^2$** `9/10`. Halving diameter quarters area; doubling the length doubles extension for the same force. Uncertainty in $A$ is **twice** the percentage uncertainty in $d$.
- **Force–extension vs stress–strain** `8/10`: the first depends on the specimen; the second is a material property.
- **Limit of proportionality / elastic limit / yield** `7/10`: beyond the elastic limit the wire does not return to its original length (permanent extension). Ductile, brittle, polymeric (rubber hysteresis; the area between the loading and unloading curves is the energy dissipated as thermal energy).
- **Energy conversion in a spring launcher** `8/10`: $\tfrac12kx^2=\tfrac12mv^2+mgh$ in one line.

## 4. Waves and superposition (AS 7–8)

- **Wave equation** `10/10`: $v=f\lambda$; $T=\dfrac1f$. When the medium changes, $f$ stays constant and $\lambda\propto v$.
- **Intensity** `10/10`: $I=\dfrac PA$; $I\propto A_{\text{amp}}^2$ (amplitude squared); for a point source $I\propto\dfrac1{r^2}$. Doubling the distance quarters the intensity; doubling the amplitude gives four times the intensity.
- **Phase difference** `9/10`: $\Delta\phi=\dfrac{2\pi\Delta x}\lambda=360^\circ\dfrac{\Delta x}\lambda$ (path difference), or $360^\circ\dfrac{\Delta t}T$ (time difference). From a displacement–position graph, count the fraction of a wavelength between the two points.
- **Doppler (source moving)** `9/10`: $f_o=\dfrac{f_sv}{v\pm v_s}$ ($-$ approaching, $+$ receding). **Higher pitch approaching**.
- **Malus's law** `9/10`: $I=I_0\cos^2\theta$ through a second polariser at angle $\theta$; unpolarised light halves on the first polariser. Crossed polarisers block all light.
- **Wave speed from a graph** `7/10`: speed $=\dfrac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}}$ or $f\lambda$; a **displacement–position** graph gives $\lambda$; **displacement–time** gives $T$.
- **EM spectrum order (long to short $\lambda$)** `8/10`: radio, microwaves, infrared, visible (red to violet), ultraviolet, X-rays, gamma. All travel at $c$ in vacuum. **Mnemonic:** *R*abbits *M*ate *I*n *V*arious *U*nusual *X*-rated *G*ardens.
- **Double slit** `10/10`: $\lambda=\dfrac{ax}D$ ($a$ slit separation, $x$ fringe spacing, $D$ screen distance). **Measure across many fringes** to cut the uncertainty. Wider spacing $x$ for longer $\lambda$ or larger $D$, smaller $a$.
- **Diffraction grating** `10/10`: $d\sin\theta=n\lambda$ with $d=\dfrac1{\text{lines per metre}}$. **Maximum order** $n_{\max}=\left\lfloor\dfrac d\lambda\right\rfloor$ (as $\sin\theta\le1$). **Total number of maxima** $=2n_{\max}+1$. Same $\lambda$, more lines per mm give larger angles and fewer orders.
- **Path difference conditions** `9/10`: constructive: $n\lambda$ (in phase sources); destructive: $(n+\tfrac12)\lambda$. For sources in antiphase, swap them.
- **Stationary waves on a string (fixed both ends)** `10/10`: $f_n=\dfrac{nv}{2L}$, **harmonics $1,2,3,\dots$**; $v=\sqrt{T/\mu}$ (not on data booklet) so $f\propto\sqrt T$. Node-to-node spacing $=\dfrac\lambda2$.
- **Closed pipe (one end closed)** `10/10`: **odd harmonics only**, $f=\dfrac{(2n-1)v}{4L}$; fundamental $\dfrac v{4L}$; node at closed end, antinode at open. **Open pipe:** all harmonics, $f=\dfrac{nv}{2L}$. A closed pipe's fundamental is **half** that of an open one of the same length.
- **Find $v$ from resonance in a tube** `9/10`: the difference in two consecutive resonance lengths $=\dfrac\lambda2$, so $v=2f(L_2-L_1)$ and the **end correction cancels**.
- **Stationary vs progressive** `8/10`: stationary waves have nodes, constant phase within a loop (adjacent loops $180^\circ$ out of phase), amplitude varying with position and no energy transfer.
- **Rayleigh criterion (A Level)** `5/10`: $\theta\approx\dfrac\lambda b$ for a slit of width $b$ (for a circular aperture $1.22\lambda/b$).
- **Single-slit diffraction** `7/10`: first minimum at $b\sin\theta=\lambda$; central maximum is twice as wide as the others.

## 5. Electricity and DC circuits (AS 9–10)

- **Charge and current** `10/10`: $Q=It$; $V=\dfrac WQ$ (energy per unit charge); $E=VIt$. $1\ \mathrm{kWh}=3.6\ \mathrm{MJ}$.
- **Drift velocity** `9/10`: $I=nAvq$. **Same current, thinner wire gives higher drift speed** ($v\propto\dfrac1A$); a semiconductor has far fewer carriers (small $n$) so a much larger $v$.
- **Resistance** `10/10`: $R=\dfrac{\rho L}A$; **doubling the length doubles $R$; doubling the diameter divides $R$ by $4$**; stretching a wire at constant volume (new length $kL$) multiplies $R$ by $k^2$.
- **Power: the three forms** `10/10`: $P=VI=I^2R=\dfrac{V^2}R$. **Same current: $P\propto R$ (series). Same voltage: $P\propto\dfrac1R$ (parallel/mains).** Doubling the current quadruples the heat.
- **Parallel resistors** `10/10`: two: $R=\dfrac{R_1R_2}{R_1+R_2}$ (product over sum); $n$ equal: $\dfrac Rn$. **A parallel combination is always smaller than the smallest branch.** Parallel of unknown value $R_x$ giving $R_T$: $R_x=\dfrac{R_TR_1}{R_1-R_T}$.
- **Potential divider** `10/10`: $V_{\text{out}}=V_{\text{in}}\dfrac{R_2}{R_1+R_2}$. **Voltage splits in proportion to resistance.** With a thermistor/LDR: resistance falls when hot/bright, so the share across it falls.
- **Loaded potential divider** `8/10`: a load in parallel with $R_2$ lowers $R_2$'s effective value and so $V_{\text{out}}$ falls. A **sliding contact** varies $V_{\text{out}}$ from $0$ to $V_{\text{in}}$.
- **EMF and internal resistance** `10/10`: $E=I(R+r)=V+Ir$. Plot $V$ against $I$: **gradient $=-r$, intercept $=E$** (at $I=0$). **Terminal voltage falls when current rises.** Short-circuit current $=\dfrac Er$.
- **Maximum power transfer (A Level favourite)** `7/10`: load power is greatest when $R=r$, with $P_{\max}=\dfrac{E^2}{4r}$ and efficiency $50\%$.
- **Kirchhoff** `9/10`: **first law** (current in $=$ current out at a junction, conservation of charge); **second law** ($\sum E=\sum IR$ around a loop; conservation of energy). Write a loop equation for each independent loop.
- **Series cells** `7/10`: emf adds, internal resistances add. **Identical cells in parallel:** emf stays $E$, internal resistance $\dfrac rn$.
- **Ammeter and voltmeter** `7/10`: ammeter in series (low resistance); voltmeter in parallel (high resistance). Resistance of a lamp changes with temperature, so it does not obey Ohm's law (curved $I$–$V$).
- **I–V graphs** `8/10`: resistance at a point is $V/I$ (**not** the gradient unless the graph is a straight line through the origin).
- **Potentiometer (A Level)** `6/10`: balance length gives $\dfrac{E_1}{E_2}=\dfrac{l_1}{l_2}$ at no current (so it measures a true emf).
- **Kilowatt-hour cost** `7/10`: energy $=P(\mathrm{kW})\times t(\mathrm h)$; cost $=$ energy $\times$ rate.
- **Fuse choice** `6/10`: $I=\dfrac PV$; choose the next rating above it.

## 6. Particle physics (AS 11)

- **Quarks** `10/10`: up $+\tfrac23e$, down $-\tfrac13e$, strange $-\tfrac13e$. **Proton uud** ($+1$), **neutron udd** ($0$). Antiquarks have the opposite charge. **Baryon** $=qqq$ (baryon number $+1$), **meson** $=q\bar q$ (baryon number $0$).
- **Beta decays** `10/10`: $\beta^-$: $n\to p+e^-+\bar\nu_e$ (**d turns into u**); $\beta^+$: $p\to n+e^++\nu_e$ (**u turns into d**). **Always check conservation of charge, baryon number and lepton number** (electron $+1$, positron $-1$, neutrino $+1$, antineutrino $-1$).
- **Conservation checklist** `9/10`: charge, baryon number, lepton number; **momentum and energy**. Strangeness is conserved only in the strong interaction (beyond spec).
- **Nuclide balancing** `10/10`: top (nucleon) numbers sum equal; bottom (proton) numbers sum equal. $\alpha$: $-4$ in $A$, $-2$ in $Z$. $\beta^-$: $Z\to Z+1$. $\beta^+$: $Z\to Z-1$. $\gamma$: no change.
- **Energy conversions** `9/10`: $1\ \mathrm{eV}=1.60\times10^{-19}\ \mathrm J$; $1\ \mathrm u=1.66\times10^{-27}\ \mathrm{kg}\equiv931.5\ \mathrm{MeV}/c^2$. $E=mc^2$: mass in u $\times931.5$ gives MeV directly.
- **Leptons and hadrons** `7/10`: leptons (electron, neutrino) are fundamental and don't feel the strong force; hadrons are made of quarks.
- **Fundamental forces' ranges (A Level reading)** `5/10`: strong (short, $\sim10^{-15}$ m), weak (very short), EM and gravity (infinite).

## 7. A Level: circular motion and gravitation (12–13)

- **Circular motion equations** `10/10`: $v=r\omega$, $\omega=\dfrac{2\pi}T=2\pi f$, $a=\dfrac{v^2}r=\omega^2r$, $F=\dfrac{mv^2}r=m\omega^2r$. **The centripetal force is the resultant of real forces towards the centre, never an extra force.**
- **Conical pendulum / banked track / car on a bend** `8/10`: $\tan\theta=\dfrac{v^2}{rg}$ (banked, no friction). Maximum speed on a flat bend: $v=\sqrt{\mu gr}$.
- **Vertical circle** `8/10`: at the **top** need $T+mg=\dfrac{mv^2}r$ so the minimum speed is $v=\sqrt{gr}$ (string just taut); at the **bottom** minimum speed to complete the circle is $\sqrt{5gr}$ (by energy conservation); tension at the bottom exceeds at the top by $6mg$.
- **Gravitational force and field** `10/10`: $F=\dfrac{GMm}{r^2}$, $g=\dfrac{GM}{r^2}$ (inverse square). **At altitude $h$:** $g'=g\left(\dfrac R{R+h}\right)^2$. Surface: $g=\tfrac43\pi G\rho R$ (uniform sphere).
- **Orbits: the key identity** `10/10`: gravity $=$ centripetal, so $v=\sqrt{\dfrac{GM}r}$ and $T^2=\dfrac{4\pi^2}{GM}r^3$ (**Kepler: $T^2\propto r^3$**). **Mass of the orbiting body cancels.** Use the ratio method: $\dfrac{T_1^2}{T_2^2}=\dfrac{r_1^3}{r_2^3}$ (Moon $27.3$ d at $3.84\times10^8$ m).
- **Geostationary** `9/10`: $T=24\ \mathrm h$, equatorial, same direction as Earth's rotation, $r\approx4.22\times10^7\ \mathrm m$ (height $\approx3.6\times10^7\ \mathrm m$).
- **Potential and orbital energy** `9/10`: $\phi=-\dfrac{GM}r$; potential energy $-\dfrac{GMm}r$. In a circular orbit: **$E_k=\dfrac{GMm}{2r}$, $E_p=-\dfrac{GMm}r$, $E_{\text{total}}=-\dfrac{GMm}{2r}$**. So $E_p=-2E_k$ and $E_{\text{total}}=-E_k$. A lower orbit has **more** KE, lower (more negative) total energy.
- **Escape speed** `9/10`: $v_{\text{esc}}=\sqrt{\dfrac{2GM}R}=\sqrt{2gR}$, which is $\sqrt2\times$ the circular-orbit speed at the surface. Earth: $11.2\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$.
- **Work done in moving between two points** `7/10`: $W=m\Delta\phi$. Area under a $g$–$r$ graph is $-\Delta\phi$.
- **Gravitational vs electric analogy** `7/10`: $\dfrac{GMm}{r^2}\leftrightarrow\dfrac{Qq}{4\pi\varepsilon_0r^2}$; gravity is always attractive.
- **Field between two bodies** `6/10`: $g=0$ at the neutral point where $\dfrac{M_1}{r_1^2}=\dfrac{M_2}{r_2^2}$, i.e. $\dfrac{r_1}{r_2}=\sqrt{\dfrac{M_1}{M_2}}$.

## 8. A Level: gases and thermal physics (14–15)

- **Ideal gas** `10/10`: $pV=nRT=NkT$ with $R=8.31$, $k=1.38\times10^{-23}$. **Ratio form** for a fixed quantity: $\dfrac{p_1V_1}{T_1}=\dfrac{p_2V_2}{T_2}$ (**always in kelvin**). Density: $\rho=\dfrac{pM}{RT}$.
- **Kinetic theory** `10/10`: $pV=\tfrac13Nm\langle c^2\rangle$, $p=\tfrac13\rho\langle c^2\rangle$. **Average KE per molecule** $=\tfrac32kT$ (**depends only on $T$**); $c_{\text{rms}}=\sqrt{\dfrac{3kT}m}=\sqrt{\dfrac{3RT}M}$. Lighter gas molecules move faster at the same $T$ ($v\propto\dfrac1{\sqrt m}$).
- **Number of molecules** `8/10`: $N=\dfrac{pV}{kT}=nN_A$. Internal energy of an ideal monatomic gas $U=\tfrac32NkT=\tfrac32nRT$.
- **First law** `9/10`: $\Delta U=q+w$ (**$w$ is work done *on* the gas**). Work done by an expanding gas $=p\Delta V$ (area under $pV$). Isothermal: $\Delta U=0$ so $q=-w$. Constant volume: $w=0$. Adiabatic: $q=0$.
- **Temperature scales** `8/10`: $T/\mathrm K=\theta/^\circ\mathrm C+273.15$. A change of $1\ \mathrm K$ equals $1^\circ\mathrm C$.
- **Heating** `10/10`: $Q=mc\Delta T$, $Q=mL$; **electrical heater: $Pt=mc\Delta T$**. For specific latent heat with losses, take the **difference of two runs** to cancel the constant loss.
- **Mixing/thermal equilibrium** `8/10`: heat lost $=$ heat gained. Mixing two amounts of water: $T_f=\dfrac{m_1T_1+m_2T_2}{m_1+m_2}$ (same $c$).
- **Specific heat vs latent heat graph** `7/10`: flat sections are phase changes; **steeper line means smaller $c$** (for constant power).
- **Moles and atoms** `7/10`: $N=nN_A$, mass $=nM$, $1\ \mathrm{mol}=6.02\times10^{23}$.
- **Brownian motion (qualitative)** `5/10`: random molecular collisions on smoke particles.

## 9. A Level: oscillations (16)

- **SHM defining equation** `10/10`: $a=-\omega^2x$. $\omega=2\pi f=\dfrac{2\pi}T$. **Period independent of amplitude.**
- **Displacement, velocity, acceleration** `10/10`: from the **equilibrium** start $x=x_0\sin\omega t$, $v=v_0\cos\omega t$, $v_0=\omega x_0$, $a_{\max}=\omega^2x_0$. **From an extreme position** $x=x_0\cos\omega t$. **Velocity at any displacement:** $v=\pm\omega\sqrt{x_0^2-x^2}$ — no time needed.
- **Energy in SHM** `10/10`: total $E=\tfrac12m\omega^2x_0^2$; $E_k=\tfrac12m\omega^2(x_0^2-x^2)$; $E_p=\tfrac12m\omega^2x^2$. **$E_k=E_p$ at $x=\dfrac{x_0}{\sqrt2}$.** Total energy $\propto x_0^2$ and $\propto f^2$.
- **Periods** `10/10`: simple pendulum $T=2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac Lg}$ (small angles); mass–spring $T=2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac mk}$. **Ratio method:** quadrupling $m$ doubles $T$. Spring in series/parallel: use effective $k$.
- **Graph shapes** `8/10`: $a$ against $x$ is a straight line of gradient $-\omega^2$ through the origin (use to find $T$). $v$ against $x$ is an ellipse. $E_k$ and $E_p$ against $x$ are parabolas (opposite). Against $t$, $E_k$ and $E_p$ oscillate at **twice** the SHM frequency.
- **Damping and resonance** `8/10`: light damping $\rightarrow$ amplitude falls slowly; **critical damping** gives the fastest return without oscillating; heavy damping slows the return. At resonance (driving $f=$ natural $f_0$) the amplitude peaks; **more damping lowers and broadens the peak** and moves it to slightly lower frequency.
- **Phase** `7/10`: $v$ leads $x$ by $\dfrac\pi2$; $a$ is in antiphase with $x$.
- **Finding $\omega$ from two data points** `8/10`: $v^2=\omega^2(x_0^2-x^2)$ for two pairs $\rightarrow$ solve for $\omega^2$ and $x_0^2$ (subtract).

## 10. A Level: electric fields and capacitance (17–18)

- **Coulomb and field** `10/10`: $F=\dfrac{Qq}{4\pi\varepsilon_0r^2}$, $E=\dfrac Q{4\pi\varepsilon_0r^2}$ (point charge), $E=\dfrac Vd$ (uniform field between plates), $F=qE$. **Same inverse-square ratio method as gravity.**
- **Electric potential** `9/10`: $V=\dfrac Q{4\pi\varepsilon_0r}$ (scalar; sign matters); work $=q\Delta V$; $E=-\dfrac{dV}{dx}$ (gradient of the $V$–$r$ graph, sign), and area under $E$–$r$ gives $-\Delta V$. $E_k$ gained by a charge through $V$: $\tfrac12mv^2=qV$.
- **Charged particle in a uniform field** `8/10`: parabolic path like a projectile with "$g$" replaced by $\dfrac{qE}m$; electrons: $a=\dfrac{eV}{md}$.
- **Capacitors** `10/10`: $C=\dfrac QV$; **energy** $W=\tfrac12QV=\tfrac12CV^2=\dfrac{Q^2}{2C}$ (area under $V$–$Q$). **Parallel plates:** $C=\dfrac{\varepsilon_0A}d$ (so $C\propto A$, $C\propto\dfrac1d$).
- **Combining capacitors** `10/10`: **parallel add** $C=C_1+C_2$; **series** $\dfrac1C=\dfrac1{C_1}+\dfrac1{C_2}$ — the **opposite of resistors** (series capacitors share the same charge; voltages divide inversely to $C$).
- **RC discharge** `10/10`: $Q=Q_0e^{-t/RC}$, same form for $I$ and $V$; **time constant $\tau=RC$**; after one $\tau$ the quantity falls to $37\%$ ($e^{-1}$); **half-life $t_{1/2}=RC\ln2\approx0.693\,RC$**. **Charging:** $V=V_0(1-e^{-t/RC})$ reaches $63\%$ after $\tau$. Gradient of $Q$–$t$ at $t=0$: $I_0=\dfrac{V_0}R$.
- **Using logs** `8/10`: $\ln V=\ln V_0-\dfrac t{RC}$; the gradient of $\ln V$ against $t$ is $-\dfrac1{RC}$.
- **Energy lost in charging through a resistor** `7/10`: half of the supply's work $QV$ goes into the capacitor, half is dissipated as heat.
- **Capacitor smoothing** `7/10`: a larger $C$ or $R$ means a slower discharge (smoother output of a rectifier).

## 11. A Level: magnetism, induction, AC (19–21)

- **Force on a conductor / charge** `10/10`: $F=BIL\sin\theta$, $F=Bqv\sin\theta$. **Fleming's left-hand rule** for the direction of the force. **Circular motion in a magnetic field:** $Bqv=\dfrac{mv^2}r\Rightarrow r=\dfrac{mv}{Bq}=\dfrac p{Bq}$, and period $T=\dfrac{2\pi m}{Bq}$ **independent of $v$**.
- **Velocity selector** `8/10`: undeflected when $qE=Bqv$, i.e. $v=\dfrac EB$.
- **Hall effect** `7/10`: $V_H=\dfrac{BI}{ntq}$ ($t$ thickness); used to find $n$ or $B$ (a Hall probe). A thinner semiconductor strip with small $n$ gives a bigger voltage.
- **Field formulae** `8/10`: long straight wire $B=\dfrac{\mu_0I}{2\pi r}$; flat coil/solenoid $B=\mu_0nI$ ($n$ turns per metre; $B$ doubles if $I$ doubles). Force between parallel wires $\dfrac FL=\dfrac{\mu_0I_1I_2}{2\pi d}$ (attract if currents in the same direction).
- **Flux and Faraday** `10/10`: $\Phi=BA\cos\theta$, flux linkage $N\Phi$, $|\mathcal E|=N\dfrac{d\Phi}{dt}$. **Lenz:** the induced current opposes the change. EMF of a straight rod moving across a field: $\mathcal E=Blv$ (**sweeps area at rate $Blv$**). EMF of a rotating coil: $\mathcal E=BAN\omega\sin\omega t$, peak $BAN\omega$.
- **Induced EMF from a graph** `9/10`: **gradient of the flux–time graph** (not the height).
- **Transformer** `10/10`: $\dfrac{V_s}{V_p}=\dfrac{N_s}{N_p}$; for an ideal one $V_pI_p=V_sI_s$ (so step-up voltage means step-down current). Transmission losses $P=I^2R$ — **step the voltage up to cut $I$**. Real transformer losses: resistance of windings, eddy currents (laminated core), hysteresis, flux leakage.
- **AC** `10/10`: $V_{\text{rms}}=\dfrac{V_0}{\sqrt2}$, $I_{\text{rms}}=\dfrac{I_0}{\sqrt2}$, mean power $=\tfrac12I_0V_0=\dfrac{V_0^2}{2R}$ (half the peak power). **Reading a CRO:** $V_{\text{peak}}=$ divisions $\times$ volts per div; period $=$ divisions $\times$ time-base; $f=\dfrac1T$.
- **Rectification** `7/10`: a diode gives half-wave; a bridge gives full-wave; a smoothing capacitor reduces the ripple.
- **Lenz-law direction trick** `8/10`: the induced current always creates a field that **opposes the flux change**: moving a magnet towards a coil, the near face is the same pole as the magnet.

## 12. A Level: quantum, nuclear, medical, astronomy (22–25)

### Quantum
- **Photon energy** `10/10`: $E=hf=\dfrac{hc}\lambda$. **Quick conversion** (not on data booklet): $\lambda(\mathrm{nm})\times E(\mathrm{eV})\approx1240$. A $2.0\ \mathrm{eV}$ photon is $620\ \mathrm{nm}$.
- **Photoelectric effect** `10/10`: $hf=\Phi+E_{k,\max}$ where $\Phi=hf_0$. $eV_s=E_{k,\max}$ (stopping potential). **Intensity** changes the **number** of photoelectrons (current), **not** their max energy. $E_k$ against $f$: gradient $h$, $x$-intercept $f_0$, $y$-intercept $-\Phi$.
- **de Broglie** `10/10`: $\lambda=\dfrac hp=\dfrac h{mv}$. For an electron accelerated through $V$: $\lambda=\dfrac h{\sqrt{2meV}}\approx\dfrac{1.23}{\sqrt{V}}\ \mathrm{nm}$ (V in volts). Doubling the accelerating voltage divides $\lambda$ by $\sqrt2$.
- **Energy levels** `9/10`: $hf=E_2-E_1$ for emission/absorption; **number of possible spectral lines** from level $n$: $\dfrac{n(n-1)}2$. Line spectra evidence for discrete levels; continuous spectrum from hot solids.
- **X-rays** `7/10`: minimum wavelength $\lambda_{\min}=\dfrac{hc}{eV}$.
- **Wave–particle evidence** `7/10`: electron diffraction (wave), photoelectric effect (particle).

### Nuclear
- **Decay** `10/10`: $N=N_0e^{-\lambda t}$ (also $A$, count rate), $A=\lambda N$, $\lambda=\dfrac{\ln2}{t_{1/2}}$. **After $n$ half-lives the fraction is $\left(\tfrac12\right)^n$**; use it before exponentials. Remaining fraction after time $t$: $2^{-t/t_{1/2}}$.
- **Activity ratios** `8/10`: activity of a source $\propto N$; for two sources, the one with the shorter half-life has the higher activity for the same number of nuclei.
- **Background correction** `8/10`: corrected rate $=$ measured $-$ background. **Measure the background first, over a long time**.
- **Binding energy and mass defect** `10/10`: $\Delta m=Zm_p+(A-Z)m_n-m_{\text{nucleus}}$; $E_B=\Delta mc^2$, with $\Delta m$ in u $\times931.5=$ MeV. **Binding energy per nucleon** peaks at iron (Fe-56): fusion of light nuclei and fission of heavy ones both release energy.
- **Energy released in a reaction** `9/10`: $Q=\left(\sum m_{\text{reactants}}-\sum m_{\text{products}}\right)c^2$; use atomic or nuclear masses consistently. In MeV: $\Delta m(\mathrm u)\times931.5$.
- **Rutherford and the nucleus** `6/10`: most $\alpha$ pass undeflected (atom mostly empty), a few deflect through large angles (small dense positive nucleus); radius $R=r_0A^{1/3}$ so **density is the same for all nuclei** ($\sim10^{17}\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$).
- **Nuclear radius** `7/10`: $R=r_0A^{1/3}$ with $r_0\approx1.2\ \mathrm{fm}$.
- **Safety** `5/10`: inverse-square law for distance; shielding; $\alpha$ stopped by paper, $\beta$ by aluminium, $\gamma$ by thick lead (intensity falls exponentially).

### Medical and imaging
- **Attenuation** `10/10`: $I=I_0e^{-\mu x}$; **half-value thickness** $x_{1/2}=\dfrac{\ln2}\mu$. Same structure as radioactive decay.
- **Acoustic impedance** `9/10`: $Z=\rho c$; **intensity reflection coefficient** $\alpha=\dfrac{(Z_2-Z_1)^2}{(Z_2+Z_1)^2}$; **gel** removes the air gap (large mismatch) so more is transmitted.
- **Ultrasound imaging** `7/10`: depth $=\dfrac{vt}{2}$ (echo travels there and back).
- **X-ray CT, MRI, PET** `7/10`: CT combines many projections; MRI uses RF resonance of protons in a field gradient; PET uses positron annihilation to give two $0.511\ \mathrm{MeV}$ photons in opposite directions.

### Astronomy and cosmology
- **Luminosity–flux** `10/10`: $F=\dfrac L{4\pi d^2}$ (inverse square). Standard candles $\rightarrow$ distance by comparing $F$.
- **Stefan–Boltzmann** `10/10`: $L=4\pi r^2\sigma T^4$. **Ratio method:** doubling radius $\times4$ in $L$; doubling $T$ $\times16$. For two stars $\dfrac{L_1}{L_2}=\left(\dfrac{r_1}{r_2}\right)^2\left(\dfrac{T_1}{T_2}\right)^4$.
- **Wien** `9/10`: $\lambda_{\max}T=2.898\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{m\,K}$ — hotter means bluer. Estimate a star's temperature from its peak wavelength.
- **Redshift and Hubble** `10/10`: $\dfrac{\Delta\lambda}\lambda\approx\dfrac vc$ (for $v\ll c$); $v=H_0d$; **age of the universe $\sim\dfrac1{H_0}$**. Convert units: $1\ \mathrm{pc}=3.09\times10^{16}\ \mathrm m$, $1\ \mathrm{ly}=9.46\times10^{15}\ \mathrm m$, $1\ \mathrm{AU}=1.50\times10^{11}\ \mathrm m$.
- **Parallax** `8/10`: $d(\mathrm{pc})=\dfrac1{p(\mathrm{arcsec})}$.
- **Evolution of stars** `6/10`: low mass: main sequence $\rightarrow$ red giant $\rightarrow$ planetary nebula $\rightarrow$ white dwarf; high mass: red supergiant $\rightarrow$ supernova $\rightarrow$ neutron star / black hole.
- **Cosmic microwave background** `7/10`: $\sim2.7\ \mathrm K$, redshifted relic of the hot early universe; evidence for the Big Bang along with Hubble's law and the abundance of light elements.

## 13. Quick-check list

- **Sign and magnitude** checks: energy and power positive, speed $\ge0$; force direction with a sketch.
- **Orders of magnitude:** if your answer for a person's speed is $300\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, recheck.
- **Limiting cases:** let a quantity $\to0$ or $\infty$ and see if the formula behaves.
- **Units in the final line.** Put them with a conversion if needed ($\mathrm{cm}\to\mathrm m$, $\mathrm{eV}\to\mathrm J$, $\mathrm{u}\to\mathrm{kg}$).
- **Read the data booklet first:** many constants and equations (like $\rho Av$ flow, $c=f\lambda$, $F=\Delta p/\Delta t$) are on it.
