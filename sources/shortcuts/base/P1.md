## 1. Algebra

### Quadratics
- **Vertex without calculus.** `9/10` $y=ax^2+bx+c$ has its turning point at $x=-\dfrac b{2a}$, value $c-\dfrac{b^2}{4a}$. Same as completing the square: $a\left(x+\dfrac b{2a}\right)^2+c-\dfrac{b^2}{4a}$.
- **Discriminant is a decision tool.** `10/10` $b^2-4ac>0$ two real roots, $=0$ repeated (tangent), $<0$ none. For "line meets curve" put them equal, make a quadratic, read the discriminant: **tangent $\Leftrightarrow\ \Delta=0$**.
- **Sum and product of roots** `8/10` $\alpha+\beta=-\dfrac ba$, $\alpha\beta=\dfrac ca$ — find a missing coefficient from a known root: if one root is $r$ then the other is $\dfrac ca\cdot\dfrac1r$.
- **Roots difference** `5/10` $|\alpha-\beta|=\dfrac{\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{|a|}$.
- **AC (cross) method to factorise $ax^2+bx+c$:** `7/10` find two numbers with product $ac$ and sum $b$, split the middle term, factor by grouping. Check by expanding the factors' constant terms.
- **Perfect-square test:** `5/10` $\Delta=0$ means the quadratic is $a(x+\tfrac b{2a})^2$.
- **Quadratic in disguise.** `9/10` $x^4-5x^2+4$, $2^{2x}-3\cdot2^x+2$, $x-5\sqrt x+4$, $\dfrac1{x^2}+\dfrac3x-4$: substitute $u=x^2$, $2^x$, $\sqrt x$, $\dfrac1x$.
- **Symmetric trick for "$ax^2+bx+c$ is always positive".** `7/10` Needs $a>0$ and $\Delta<0$. Always positive $\Leftrightarrow$ both.
- **Position of roots relative to a number $k$:** `5/10` check the sign of $f(k)$ and the vertex side.
- **Maximise/minimise a quadratic expression** `8/10` (areas, profits): it is a vertex question, so use $-\dfrac b{2a}$ not differentiation.

### Surds and indices
- **Rationalise** `9/10` $\dfrac1{a+\sqrt b}=\dfrac{a-\sqrt b}{a^2-b}$. The denominator becomes $a^2-b$ on sight.
- **Simplify $\sqrt{n}$** `8/10` by pulling out the largest square factor: $\sqrt{72}=6\sqrt2$. Squares to know: $4,9,16,25,36,49,64,81,100$.
- **Square of a surd sum:** `5/10` $(\sqrt a+\sqrt b)^2=a+b+2\sqrt{ab}$. Reverse it to un-nest: $\sqrt{7+2\sqrt{10}}=\sqrt5+\sqrt2$ (find two numbers with sum 7 and product 10).
- **Index laws collapse a product of different bases:** `8/10` write everything in the same prime base first, then add the exponents. $\dfrac{8^x}{2^{x+1}}=2^{3x-x-1}=2^{2x-1}$.
- **Negative and fractional powers:** `7/10` $x^{-n}=\dfrac1{x^n}$, $x^{m/n}=\sqrt[n]{x^m}$. $(a/b)^{-n}=(b/a)^n$ (flip then power).

### Inequalities
- **Quadratic inequalities by sketch:** `9/10` solve $=0$, sketch the $\cup$ or $\cap$, read the region. Above axis for $>0$.
- **Don't multiply an inequality by something that can be negative.** `9/10` Move everything to one side, factorise, sign-diagram (or sketch). For rational inequalities multiply by the **square** of the denominator ($>0$).
- **Combining:** `5/10` write each solution as intervals and intersect (and) / unite (or).

### Simultaneous equations (one linear, one quadratic)
Substitute the linear into the quadratic, then:
- two solutions $\Leftrightarrow\Delta>0$; tangent $\Leftrightarrow\Delta=0$; miss $\Leftrightarrow\Delta<0$.
- **Midpoint of the intersection chord** `7/10` has $x$-coordinate $\dfrac{x_1+x_2}2=-\dfrac b{2a}$ of the substituted quadratic; plug into the line for $y$. No need for individual roots.
- **Chord length** `5/10` $=\sqrt{1+m^2}\;|x_1-x_2|=\sqrt{1+m^2}\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt{\Delta}}{|a|}$.

### Polynomials
- **Factor theorem:** `9/10` $(x-a)$ is a factor $\Leftrightarrow f(a)=0$. For $(px-q)$ test $f(q/p)$. Try $\pm1,\pm2$ and factors of the constant term (divided by factors of the leading coefficient).
- **Remainder theorem:** `8/10` remainder on division by $(x-a)$ is $f(a)$; by $(px-q)$ is $f(q/p)$.
- **Synthetic division** `8/10` (a quick, error-free long division): for root $a$, bring down the leading coefficient, multiply by $a$, add to the next, repeat. The last number is the remainder.
- **Factorise a cubic fast:** `8/10` find one root $a$ by trial, divide, then factorise the quadratic.
- **Sum of coefficients** `7/10` $f(1)$; **alternating sum** $f(-1)$; **constant term** $f(0)$: free information.
- **Cubic root relations (beyond spec):** `4/10` $\alpha+\beta+\gamma=-\dfrac ba$, $\alpha\beta+\beta\gamma+\gamma\alpha=\dfrac ca$, $\alpha\beta\gamma=-\dfrac da$.

### Graphs and transformations
- $y=f(x+a)$: shift **left** $a$; $y=f(x)+a$: up $a$; $y=f(ax)$: stretch $x$ by $1/a$; $y=af(x)$: stretch $y$ by $a$; $y=-f(x)$: reflect in $x$-axis; $y=f(-x)$: reflect in $y$-axis.
- **Move the point, not the curve:** `6/10` transform a few key points (intercepts, turning points, asymptotes) and re-sketch.
- **Sketching a cubic:** `7/10` roots and sign pattern. A **repeated factor** touches the axis; an **odd power** crosses; a **triple root** flattens as it crosses. Leading-coefficient sign gives the ends.
- **Reciprocal graphs** `5/10` $y=\dfrac ax$: asymptotes at the axes; after transformation, asymptotes move with the shift.

## 2. Coordinate geometry

### Lines
- **Straight line forms:** `6/10` $y=mx+c$; $y-y_1=m(x-x_1)$; two points $\dfrac{y-y_1}{y_2-y_1}=\dfrac{x-x_1}{x_2-x_1}$; $ax+by+c=0$ with gradient $-\dfrac ab$.
- **Distance** `6/10` $\sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2}$; **midpoint** $\left(\dfrac{x_1+x_2}2,\dfrac{y_1+y_2}2\right)$.
- **Perpendicular bisector:** `8/10` midpoint + gradient $-\dfrac1m$. Every point on it is equidistant from the two ends: set $PA^2=PB^2$ for the locus equation straight away; the squares cancel to a linear equation.
- **Distance from a point to a line** `7/10` (beyond spec, very handy): $\dfrac{|ax_0+by_0+c|}{\sqrt{a^2+b^2}}$.
- **Line through the intersection of two lines:** `6/10` $L_1+\lambda L_2=0$ is **every** line through that point; choose $\lambda$ to satisfy one more condition, with no need to find the intersection.
- **Parallel line through a point:** `7/10` same $a,b$; only $c$ changes: $ax+by=ax_1+by_1$. **Perpendicular line:** swap and negate: $bx-ay=bx_1-ay_1$.
- **Collinearity:** `6/10` equal gradients, or zero area (shoelace below).
- **Intersection of two lines:** `7/10` substitute, or use Cramer: for $a_1x+b_1y=c_1$, $a_2x+b_2y=c_2$,
$x=\dfrac{c_1b_2-c_2b_1}{a_1b_2-a_2b_1}$, $y=\dfrac{a_1c_2-a_2c_1}{a_1b_2-a_2b_1}$.

### Areas
- **Triangle with a vertex at the origin:** `6/10` $\tfrac12|x_1y_2-x_2y_1|$.
- **Shoelace formula** `9/10` for any polygon, vertices in order:
$$A=\tfrac12\left|\sum(x_iy_{i+1}-x_{i+1}y_i)\right|.$$
List coordinates in a column, repeat the first at the end, cross-multiply downwards and upwards. A triangle takes 30 seconds and avoids dropping a perpendicular height.
- **Right-angle check:** `6/10` $PQ^2+QR^2=PR^2$, or gradients multiply to $-1$.
- **Rectangle/parallelogram from three vertices:** `6/10` $D=A+C-B$ (opposite vertices have the same midpoint).
- **Kite and rhombus:** `5/10` diagonals bisect at right angles; area $=\tfrac12d_1d_2$.

### Circles
- **Equation:** `7/10` $(x-a)^2+(y-b)^2=r^2$. **General form** $x^2+y^2+2gx+2fy+c=0$ has centre $(-g,-f)$, radius $\sqrt{g^2+f^2-c}$. Read, don't complete the square.
- **Circle through three points:** `7/10` substitute each point into $x^2+y^2+2gx+2fy+c=0$: three linear equations.
- **Diameter with ends $A,B$:** `7/10` $(x-x_A)(x-x_B)+(y-y_A)(y-y_B)=0$. No centre or radius needed.
- **Angle in a semicircle is $90^\circ$:** `8/10` if a triangle has a right angle at $P$, the hypotenuse is a diameter and the centre is its midpoint.
- **Perpendicular from the centre bisects a chord.** `7/10` Two chords' perpendicular bisectors meet at the centre.
- **Tangent at $(x_1,y_1)$ on $(x-a)^2+(y-b)^2=r^2$:** `9/10` $(x-a)(x_1-a)+(y-b)(y_1-b)=r^2$. Or gradient $=-\dfrac{x_1-a}{y_1-b}$ (perpendicular to the radius).
- **Length of tangent from external point $P$:** `7/10` $\sqrt{(\text{distance to centre})^2-r^2}=\sqrt{x_P^2+y_P^2+2gx_P+2fy_P+c}$ (substitute the point into the general form).
- **Line meets circle:** `9/10` substitute, discriminant. **Circle touches an axis:** radius $=|$centre coordinate$|$. **Touches both axes:** centre $(r,r)$ etc.
- **Two circles touch:** `6/10` distance between centres $=r_1+r_2$ (externally) or $|r_1-r_2|$ (internally).
- **Point inside/outside:** `6/10` compare distance to the centre with $r$, or substitute into $x^2+y^2+2gx+2fy+c$: $<0$ inside.

## 3. Trigonometry

### Values and identities
- **Exact values to know cold:** `9/10`

| $\theta$ | $0$ | $30^\circ$ | $45^\circ$ | $60^\circ$ | $90^\circ$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\sin$ | $0$ | $\tfrac12$ | $\tfrac{\sqrt2}2$ | $\tfrac{\sqrt3}2$ | $1$ |
| $\cos$ | $1$ | $\tfrac{\sqrt3}2$ | $\tfrac{\sqrt2}2$ | $\tfrac12$ | $0$ |
| $\tan$ | $0$ | $\tfrac1{\sqrt3}$ | $1$ | $\sqrt3$ | — |

  Memory aid: $\sin=\dfrac{\sqrt0,\sqrt1,\sqrt2,\sqrt3,\sqrt4}{2}$ in order, and cos is the reverse.
- **CAST via the graph, not the diagram:** `8/10` $\sin(180^\circ-x)=\sin x$, $\cos(180^\circ-x)=-\cos x$, $\sin(-x)=-\sin x$, $\cos(-x)=\cos x$, $\sin(90^\circ-x)=\cos x$, $\cos(90^\circ-x)=\sin x$, $\tan(180^\circ+x)=\tan x$.
- **Pythagorean:** `9/10` $\sin^2x+\cos^2x=1$ so $1+\tan^2x=\sec^2x$ and $1+\cot^2x=\operatorname{cosec}^2x$ (beyond spec).
- **Given $\sin x=\dfrac pq$, find the other ratios from a right triangle sketch.** `6/10` No calculator.
- **$\tan x=\dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x}$:** `8/10` an equation mixing $\sin,\cos,\tan$ → divide by $\cos$ to get $\tan$, or replace $\tan$ and multiply through.
- **Quadratic in $\sin$ or $\cos$:** `9/10` $2\sin^2x+\cos x-1=0$: replace $\sin^2=1-\cos^2$, giving a quadratic in $\cos x$. Always replace the **squared** term so the equation has only one function.
- **Never divide by a trig function that can be zero:** `8/10` factorise instead (you'd lose roots).

### Solving equations
- **Principal value then symmetries:** `9/10` $\sin\theta=k$: $\theta_0,\;180^\circ-\theta_0$ (+360° repeats); $\cos\theta=k$: $\pm\theta_0$ (+360°); $\tan\theta=k$: $\theta_0+180^\circ n$.
- **Compound angle $\sin(ax+b)=k$:** `9/10` solve for the whole bracket, **then** list all values in the *transformed* range ($ax+b$ runs over a longer interval), then back-solve. Always write the range for $ax+b$ first.
- **General solutions (radians):** `6/10` $\sin\theta=\sin\alpha\Rightarrow\theta=\alpha+2\pi n$ or $\pi-\alpha+2\pi n$; $\cos\theta=\cos\alpha\Rightarrow\theta=\pm\alpha+2\pi n$; $\tan\theta=\tan\alpha\Rightarrow\theta=\alpha+\pi n$.
- **Check count:** `7/10` over one full circle, $\sin$/$\cos$ give 2 solutions per value in $(-1,1)$ and $\tan$ gives 2 solutions. Count them against the graph sketch.

### Triangles
- **Sine rule** `8/10` $\dfrac a{\sin A}=\dfrac b{\sin B}=\dfrac c{\sin C}$ (= $2R$, diameter of the circumcircle). **Ambiguous case:** given $a,b,A$ with $a<b$ there can be two triangles ($B$ and $180^\circ-B$).
- **Cosine rule** `8/10` $a^2=b^2+c^2-2bc\cos A$; **rearranged** $\cos A=\dfrac{b^2+c^2-a^2}{2bc}$. Quick test for the type of angle: if $a^2>b^2+c^2$ then $A$ is obtuse.
- **Area** `7/10` $\tfrac12ab\sin C$. **Heron (beyond spec):** $\sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}$, $s=\tfrac{a+b+c}2$.
- **Radians (when P1 reaches them):** `6/10` arc $s=r\theta$, sector $\tfrac12r^2\theta$, **segment** $\tfrac12r^2(\theta-\sin\theta)$, chord $2r\sin\tfrac\theta2$.
- **Small-angle approximations (beyond spec):** `5/10` $\sin\theta\approx\theta$, $\tan\theta\approx\theta$, $\cos\theta\approx1-\tfrac{\theta^2}2$.

## 4. Differentiation

- **Power rule $\dfrac d{dx}x^n=nx^{n-1}$ after rewriting.** `9/10` Rewrite roots, reciprocals and quotients with a single-term denominator first: $\dfrac{x^2+3}{\sqrt x}=x^{3/2}+3x^{-1/2}$.
- **Expand before differentiating** `7/10` unless it is a power of a bracket. For brackets raised to powers use the **chain rule**: $\dfrac d{dx}(ax+b)^n=na(ax+b)^{n-1}$ (beyond spec) — differentiate the bracket's inside last as a constant factor.
- **Tangent and normal.** `9/10` Tangent gradient $m=f'(x_1)$, normal $-\dfrac1m$; **equation $y-y_1=m(x-x_1)$.** For a normal passing through a named point, substitute straight in.
- **Stationary points:** `8/10` $f'(x)=0$. **Nature by $f''$:** $>0$ min, $<0$ max, $=0$ inconclusive (use sign change). For a quadratic, use the vertex (§1).
- **Increasing/decreasing:** `6/10` a sign diagram of $f'(x)$. For "increasing for all $x$" a quadratic $f'$ needs $a>0$, $\Delta<0$.
- **Optimisation recipe.** `9/10` (1) one variable by using the constraint; (2) formula for the quantity; (3) $\dfrac{dA}{dx}=0$; (4) test; (5) answer in context with units. **Look for the constraint that removes a variable first.**
- **Fixed perimeter rectangle ⇒ square maximises area; fixed area ⇒ square minimises perimeter.** `6/10`
- **Closed cylinder** `6/10` volume $V$ fixed, minimise surface area: optimum at $h=2r$ (height = diameter). Open top: $h=r$.
- **AM–GM (beyond spec):** `7/10` for $a,b>0$, $\dfrac{a+b}2\ge\sqrt{ab}$ with equality at $a=b$. So $x+\dfrac kx\ge2\sqrt k$ with minimum at $x=\sqrt k$ — no calculus.
- **Gradient of a chord and the derivative.** `5/10` $\dfrac{f(x+h)-f(x)}h\to f'(x)$ (first principles): for $x^n$ expand by the binomial theorem and keep the $h^1$ term.
- **Rates:** `6/10` $\dfrac{dy}{dt}=\dfrac{dy}{dx}\times\dfrac{dx}{dt}$.

## 5. Integration

- **Power rule** `9/10` $\int x^n\,dx=\dfrac{x^{n+1}}{n+1}+c$ ($n\ne-1$); **always rewrite first** and **never forget $+c$** for indefinite integrals.
- **Linear bracket (beyond spec):** `6/10` $\int(ax+b)^n\,dx=\dfrac{(ax+b)^{n+1}}{a(n+1)}+c$.
- **Definite integral workflow:** `7/10` integrate, substitute top, substitute bottom, subtract. Keep brackets round negative values.
- **Area between curve and $x$-axis:** `7/10` split at roots; areas below the axis count positive (take the absolute value of that part).
- **Area between two curves:** `8/10` $\int(\text{upper}-\text{lower})\,dx$ between intersection $x$-values. Sketch first: curves can swap order.
- **The one-sixth formula (parabola and chord).** `10/10` The area between $y=ax^2+bx+c$ and a straight line meeting it at $x=\alpha$ and $x=\beta$ is
$$A=\frac{|a|\,(\beta-\alpha)^3}{6}.$$
Two parabolas with leading coefficients $a_1,a_2$ use $a=a_1-a_2$. Check: $y=x^2$ and $y=x$: $\alpha=0,\beta=1$, $A=\tfrac16$, and $\int_0^1(x-x^2)dx=\tfrac16$ ✓. This works with **no integration** and applies to "area bounded by a parabola and a line".
- **Cubic and its tangent.** `5/10` If the tangent to $y=a(x-\alpha)^2(x-\beta)+(\text{line})$ at $x=\alpha$ meets the cubic again at $x=\beta$, the enclosed area is $\dfrac{|a|(\beta-\alpha)^4}{12}$ (the gap between cubic and tangent is $a(x-\alpha)^2(x-\beta)$).
- **Trapezium rule** `7/10` $\tfrac h2\left[y_0+y_n+2(y_1+\dots+y_{n-1})\right]$. A curve bending upwards ($f''>0$) ⇒ trapezia **overestimate**; bending downwards ($f''<0$) ⇒ underestimate.
- **Area under $y=x^2$ from $0$ to $a$** `5/10` is $\tfrac{a^3}3$: area of **a triangle-like ratio** — the area under $x^n$ from 0 to $a$ is $\dfrac{a^{n+1}}{n+1}=\dfrac1{n+1}\times$ the bounding rectangle. So the region between the curve and the $y$-axis (to the same height) is $\dfrac n{n+1}$ of the rectangle.
- **Finding the constant:** `7/10` substitute the given point into the integrated equation immediately.
- **Even/odd symmetry:** `6/10` $\int_{-a}^a f=2\int_0^af$ for even $f$, $=0$ for odd $f$.

## 6. Fast checks

- **Algebra:** `6/10` substitute a simple number ($x=1,2$) into your final and starting expressions; they must agree.
- **Trig solutions:** `6/10` sub one back in; count against the number of intersections on a sketch.
- **Differentiation:** `6/10` differentiate numerically at one point with a tiny step $h$ if unsure.
- **Integration:** `6/10` differentiate your answer; it must give the integrand.
- **Geometry:** `6/10` check distances and gradients with a rough scale sketch.
