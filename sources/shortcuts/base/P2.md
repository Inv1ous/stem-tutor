## 1. Proof

- **Disproof by counter-example** `6/10` is one line: find one $n$ that breaks the claim. Try $n=0,1,2$, a negative, a prime, a large number.
- **Even/odd bookkeeping:** `6/10` write even $=2k$, odd $=2k+1$. A square: odd$^2=4k(k+1)+1$, so **an odd square is always $1$ more than a multiple of $8$** (since $k(k+1)$ is even).
- **Consecutive integers:** `6/10` $n(n+1)$ is even; $n(n+1)(n+2)$ is a multiple of $6$; $(n-1)n(n+1)$ likewise.
- **Difference of squares** `6/10` $a^2-b^2=(a-b)(a+b)$ for proofs about differences of squares of consecutive numbers: $(n+1)^2-n^2=2n+1$.
- **Squares are never $\equiv2$ or $3\pmod 4$** `5/10`; they are $\equiv0$ or $1\pmod 4$. They are $0,1,4\pmod 8$ and $0,1,4,7\pmod9$ (beyond spec: modular arithmetic).
- **Proof by contradiction template (irrationality):** `7/10` assume $\sqrt2=\dfrac pq$ in lowest terms, so $p^2=2q^2$, $p$ even, $p=2m$, $q^2=2m^2$, $q$ even — contradiction. Also works for "there are infinitely many primes".
- **Proof by exhaustion:** `5/10` list the finite cases (e.g. $n=1,2,3,4$) and check each.
- **To show a quadratic is always positive:** `6/10` complete the square or use $\Delta<0$.

## 2. Algebra and functions

### Partial fractions
- **Cover-up rule (linear distinct factors).** `10/10` For $\dfrac{f(x)}{(x-a)(x-b)}=\dfrac A{x-a}+\dfrac B{x-b}$: $A=\dfrac{f(a)}{a-b}$. Cover $(x-a)$, substitute $x=a$ into what is left. $B$ likewise. **Gives answers in seconds and avoids simultaneous equations.**
- **Repeated factor** `8/10` $\dfrac{f(x)}{(x-a)^2(x-b)}=\dfrac A{x-a}+\dfrac B{(x-a)^2}+\dfrac C{x-b}$: cover-up gives $B$ and $C$; $A$ from substituting any other value, or from the leading terms (compare $x^2$ coefficients).
- **Improper fraction (degree top $\ge$ bottom):** `8/10` divide first; the quotient is a polynomial and the remainder is the proper fraction.
- **Quick check:** `6/10` substitute $x=0$ (or $x=1$) into both sides.
- **Telescoping** `6/10` $\dfrac1{r(r+1)}=\dfrac1r-\dfrac1{r+1}$ and the general $\dfrac1{(x-a)(x-b)}=\dfrac1{a-b}\left(\dfrac1{x-b}-\dfrac1{x-a}\right)$.

### Algebraic division and graphs
- **Synthetic division** `7/10` for $(x-a)$ (see P1). **Equate coefficients** for $(x^2+bx+c)$ style division: set quotient $=px+q$ and match.
- **Modulus graphs:** `8/10` reflect the part below the axis. $|f(x)|=k$: solve $f=k$ and $f=-k$. $|f(x)|<k\Leftrightarrow -k<f(x)<k$. $|x-a|<b\Leftrightarrow a-b<x<a+b$ ("distance from $a$ is less than $b$").
- **Two-modulus equations** `7/10` $|f|=|g|$: square both sides, or solve $f=\pm g$.
- **Solving $|ax+b|=cx+d$:** `8/10` sketch first, then solve the two linear cases and **check for extraneous roots** (need $cx+d\ge0$).
- **Composite functions:** `7/10` $fg(x)=f(g(x))$, apply $g$ first. **Domain of $fg$** is the inputs of $g$ whose outputs lie in the domain of $f$.
- **Inverse:** `7/10` swap $x\leftrightarrow y$, solve. Graph is the reflection in $y=x$. **Domain of $f^{-1}$ = range of $f$.** Self-inverse functions satisfy $f(f(x))=x$ — e.g. $\dfrac{ax+b}{cx-a}$ and $\dfrac kx$.
- **Fixed points of the inverse:** `7/10` $f(x)=f^{-1}(x)$ where $f$ is increasing $\Leftrightarrow f(x)=x$ (quicker than finding $f^{-1}$).
- **Transformation $y=f(ax+b)$:** `7/10` translate $y=f(x)$ **left by $b$**, then stretch parallel to the $x$-axis by factor $\dfrac1a$ (or stretch by $\dfrac1a$ first, then translate left by $\dfrac ba$).

### Binomial expansion
- **Positive integer powers:** `7/10` $(1+x)^n=1+nx+\dfrac{n(n-1)}{2!}x^2+\dfrac{n(n-1)(n-2)}{3!}x^3+\dots$
- **$(a+bx)^n=a^n\left(1+\dfrac bax\right)^n$.** `9/10` Factor out $a^n$ **before** expanding; the validity condition is $|bx/a|<1$.
- **Fractional/negative $n$:** `9/10` valid only for $|x|<1$ (or $|bx/a|<1$). **Check this condition** — it is routinely examined.
- **Coefficient of one term only:** `8/10` use $\binom nr a^{n-r}b^r$ directly; the exponent of $x$ is $r$.
- **Pascal row** `6/10` $1\,4\,6\,4\,1$, $1\,5\,10\,10\,5\,1$, $1\,6\,15\,20\,15\,6\,1$.
- **Approximating $\sqrt[n]{\cdot}$ values:** `5/10` write the number as $a(1+\epsilon)$, e.g. $\sqrt{101}=10\sqrt{1.01}=10\left(1+\tfrac12(0.01)-\tfrac18(0.01)^2\dots\right)$.
- **Standard expansions:** `9/10` $\dfrac1{1-x}=1+x+x^2+\dots$; $\dfrac1{1+x}=1-x+x^2-\dots$; $(1+x)^{-2}=1-2x+3x^2-4x^3\dots$; $(1+x)^{1/2}=1+\tfrac x2-\tfrac{x^2}8+\tfrac{x^3}{16}\dots$; $(1+x)^{-1/2}=1-\tfrac x2+\tfrac{3x^2}8-\tfrac{5x^3}{16}\dots$.
- **Binomial with an unknown coefficient $k$:** `6/10` match the required $x^r$ coefficient and solve for $k$; write the equation in terms of $k$ first, don't substitute numbers early.
- **Sum of binomial coefficients** `5/10` $=2^n$; alternating sum $=0$; $\binom nr=\binom n{n-r}$.

## 3. Coordinate geometry and parametrics

- **Parametric to Cartesian:** `8/10` eliminate $t$. For $x=a\cos t$, $y=b\sin t$: $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}+\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}=1$ (ellipse). For $x=at^2$, $y=2at$: $y^2=4ax$.
- **Gradient:** `8/10` $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{dy/dt}{dx/dt}$. **Normal** gradient $-\dfrac{dx/dt}{dy/dt}$.
- **Circle parametrics:** `6/10` $x=a+r\cos t$, $y=b+r\sin t$.
- **Trig identities for elimination:** `7/10` $\cos^2+\sin^2=1$, $\sec^2=1+\tan^2$, $\cos2t=1-2\sin^2t$. If both $x$ and $y$ involve $\sin 2t$ and $\cos t$, use double-angle first.
- **Intersection of curves in parametric form:** `5/10` substitute the Cartesian equation of one into the parameter of the other.

## 4. Sequences and series

### Arithmetic
- $u_n=a+(n-1)d$; $S_n=\dfrac n2\left(2a+(n-1)d\right)=\dfrac n2(a+l)$.
- **Terms equidistant from the middle average to the middle term.** `6/10` For an odd number of terms, $S=n\times\text{middle term}$.
- **Common difference from two terms:** `7/10` $d=\dfrac{u_p-u_q}{p-q}$.
- **Sum of multiples of $k$ up to $N$:** `5/10` number of terms $=\lfloor N/k\rfloor$, sum $=k\cdot\dfrac{m(m+1)}2$ with $m=\lfloor N/k\rfloor$. Multiples of 3 or 5 (inclusion–exclusion): add multiples of 3 and of 5, subtract multiples of 15.
- **Three terms in AP:** `7/10` write them as $a-d,\,a,\,a+d$ (sum is $3a$ immediately). For four terms $a-3d,a-d,a+d,a+3d$.
- **$S_n=An^2+Bn$ form:** `6/10` $d=2A$, $a=A+B$; $u_n=S_n-S_{n-1}$.

### Geometric
- $u_n=ar^{n-1}$; $S_n=\dfrac{a(1-r^n)}{1-r}=\dfrac{a(r^n-1)}{r-1}$ (use whichever makes the numerator positive); $S_\infty=\dfrac a{1-r}$ for $|r|<1$.
- **Three terms in GP:** `8/10` write them $\dfrac ar,\,a,\,ar$ (product $=a^3$); the **middle term squared equals the product of the outer two**: $b^2=ac$.
- **Ratio from two terms:** `7/10` $r^{p-q}=\dfrac{u_p}{u_q}$.
- **Recurring decimals:** `6/10` $0.\dot a=\dfrac a9$, $0.\dot a\dot b=\dfrac{ab}{99}$, $0.\,x\dot a=\dfrac{xa-x}{90}$ (non-repeating digit then a repeating one: write as $x$ then the geometric tail).
- **Geometric sum of a block of terms:** `7/10` first term $\times\dfrac{1-r^{\text{number of terms}}}{1-r}$.
- **Convergence to a target:** `7/10` $|r|<1$. "Sum exceeds $N$" with $S_n=\dfrac{a(1-r^n)}{1-r}$: solve for $r^n$ then take logs (flip the inequality when dividing by $\ln r<0$).
- **Compound growth/decay and loans:** `5/10` the balance after $n$ years with yearly deposit $d$ at multiplier $m$ is a GP: $d\dfrac{m^n-1}{m-1}$ (first deposit gets $m^{n}$ or $m^{n-1}$ depending on timing — define it).

### Sigma and recurrences
- $\sum_{r=1}^n r=\dfrac{n(n+1)}2$; $\sum r^2=\dfrac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}6$ (beyond spec at this level unless given).
- **Limit of a convergent recurrence $u_{n+1}=f(u_n)$:** `6/10` set $L=f(L)$ and solve; reject the root outside the allowed range.
- **Periodic sequences:** `5/10` compute a few terms, find the period $p$, then $u_n=u_{n \bmod p}$ and $S_n=\lfloor n/p\rfloor S_p+S_{n\bmod p}$.

## 5. Exponentials and logarithms

- **Three laws.** `10/10` $\log_ab+\log_ac=\log_a bc$; $\log_ab-\log_ac=\log_a\dfrac bc$; $k\log_ab=\log_ab^k$. **Also** $\log_ab=\dfrac{\log_cb}{\log_ca}$ and $\log_ab=\dfrac1{\log_ba}$.
- **Change of base shortcut:** `8/10` $a^{\log_b c}=c^{\log_b a}$ (swap base and argument); $\log_{a^n}b^m=\dfrac mn\log_ab$.
- **$\log_a1=0$, $\log_aa=1$, $a^{\log_ax}=x$.** `7/10` Convert $\log_ax=y\Leftrightarrow x=a^y$ at once.
- **Solve $a^{f(x)}=b$** `9/10` by taking logs: $f(x)=\dfrac{\ln b}{\ln a}$ — write the exact form, then decimals at the end.
- **Equations that look like quadratics:** `8/10` $2^{2x}-5\cdot2^x+4=0$: $u=2^x$. $\ln$ equations: $(\ln x)^2-\ln x-2=0$: $u=\ln x$.
- **Mixed bases:** `6/10` put all logs in one base before collecting; $\log_2x+\log_4x=3$ gives $\log_2x+\tfrac12\log_2x=3$.
- **Solve $\log_ax+\log_a(x-k)=m$:** `9/10` combine, make the quadratic, **reject roots outside the domain** ($x>0$, $x-k>0$).
- **Reduce to linear to find constants:** `9/10` $y=ab^x\Rightarrow\log y=\log a+x\log b$ (straight line, gradient $\log b$, intercept $\log a$). $y=ax^n\Rightarrow\log y=\log a+n\log x$ (plot $\log y$ against $\log x$; gradient $n$).
- **Exponential growth/decay:** `7/10` $N=N_0e^{kt}$; doubling time $=\dfrac{\ln2}k$; half-life $=\dfrac{\ln2}{|k|}$. **Percentage change per time unit** $=e^k-1$.
- **Rule of 72 (approximation):** `3/10` doubling time $\approx\dfrac{72}{\text{percentage rate}}$ years.
- **Large powers without a calculator:** `4/10` $\log_{10}2\approx0.301$, $\log_{10}3\approx0.477$, $\log_{10}7\approx0.845$. Number of digits in $N$ is $\lfloor\log_{10}N\rfloor+1$. First digit from the fractional part of the log.
- **Derivatives:** `9/10` $\dfrac d{dx}e^{kx}=ke^{kx}$, $\dfrac d{dx}a^x=a^x\ln a$, $\dfrac d{dx}\ln x=\dfrac1x$, $\dfrac d{dx}\ln f(x)=\dfrac{f'}{f}$ (**logarithmic derivative**).

## 6. Trigonometry

### Compound and double angles
- **Addition formulae:** `10/10` $\sin(A\pm B)=\sin A\cos B\pm\cos A\sin B$; $\cos(A\pm B)=\cos A\cos B\mp\sin A\sin B$; $\tan(A\pm B)=\dfrac{\tan A\pm\tan B}{1\mp\tan A\tan B}$.
- **Double angles (three versions of $\cos2A$):** `10/10` $\sin2A=2\sin A\cos A$; $\cos2A=\cos^2A-\sin^2A=2\cos^2A-1=1-2\sin^2A$; $\tan2A=\dfrac{2\tan A}{1-\tan^2A}$.
- **Rearrange for powers:** `9/10` $\cos^2A=\dfrac{1+\cos2A}2$, $\sin^2A=\dfrac{1-\cos2A}2$ — gold for integrating $\cos^2$, $\sin^2$.
- **Half-angle tangent:** `6/10` $t=\tan\dfrac\theta2$: $\sin\theta=\dfrac{2t}{1+t^2}$, $\cos\theta=\dfrac{1-t^2}{1+t^2}$, $\tan\theta=\dfrac{2t}{1-t^2}$ (beyond spec; turns $a\sin\theta+b\cos\theta=c$ into a quadratic).
- **Triple angle:** `5/10` $\sin3A=3\sin A-4\sin^3A$; $\cos3A=4\cos^3A-3\cos A$ (beyond spec but frequently proven in exams).
- **Product → sum and sum → product (beyond spec):** `5/10` $2\sin A\cos B=\sin(A+B)+\sin(A-B)$; $2\cos A\cos B=\cos(A+B)+\cos(A-B)$; $2\sin A\sin B=\cos(A-B)-\cos(A+B)$; $\sin P+\sin Q=2\sin\dfrac{P+Q}2\cos\dfrac{P-Q}2$; $\cos P+\cos Q=2\cos\dfrac{P+Q}2\cos\dfrac{P-Q}2$.

### Harmonic form
- **$a\sin x+b\cos x=R\sin(x+\alpha)$** `10/10` with $R=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$, $\tan\alpha=\dfrac ba$ (**$\cos\alpha=\dfrac aR$, $\sin\alpha=\dfrac bR$: fix the quadrant**).
- **Maximum $R$, minimum $-R$.** `8/10` Max occurs when $x+\alpha=90^\circ$. Equation $a\sin x+b\cos x=c$ has solutions iff $|c|\le R$.
- **Alternatives:** `6/10` $R\cos(x-\beta)$ with $\tan\beta=\dfrac ab$. Choose the form that matches what the question wants.
- **Minimum/maximum of $\dfrac1{k+a\sin x+b\cos x}$:** `6/10` from the harmonic form's extremes.

### Equations and exact values
- **Exact values to know:** `7/10` $\sin15^\circ=\dfrac{\sqrt6-\sqrt2}4$, $\cos15^\circ=\dfrac{\sqrt6+\sqrt2}4$, $\tan15^\circ=2-\sqrt3$, $\tan75^\circ=2+\sqrt3$.
- **$\sin18^\circ=\dfrac{\sqrt5-1}4$, $\cos36^\circ=\dfrac{1+\sqrt5}4$** `4/10` (pentagon values, beyond spec).
- **Equations with $\sin2x$ and $\sin x$:** `8/10` factorise $2\sin x(\cos x-\dots)$; don't divide by $\sin x$.
- **Reciprocals:** `6/10` $\sec=\dfrac1{\cos}$, $\operatorname{cosec}=\dfrac1{\sin}$, $\cot=\dfrac1{\tan}$; $\sec^2=1+\tan^2$, $\operatorname{cosec}^2=1+\cot^2$.
- **Inverse trig ranges:** `6/10` $\arcsin\in[-\tfrac\pi2,\tfrac\pi2]$, $\arccos\in[0,\pi]$, $\arctan\in(-\tfrac\pi2,\tfrac\pi2)$. $\arcsin x+\arccos x=\dfrac\pi2$.
- **Small angles:** `6/10` $\sin\theta\approx\theta$, $\cos\theta\approx1-\dfrac{\theta^2}2$, $\tan\theta\approx\theta$ (radians). Used to simplify limits and approximations.
- **Radians:** `7/10` arc $=r\theta$, sector $=\tfrac12r^2\theta$, segment $=\tfrac12r^2(\theta-\sin\theta)$. Convert: $\times\dfrac\pi{180}$.

## 7. Differentiation

### Rules (and how to avoid them)
- **Chain rule fast:** `10/10` differentiate the outside, keep the inside, multiply by the derivative of the inside. $\dfrac d{dx}f(g(x))=f'(g(x))\,g'(x)$. Linear inside: just multiply by its gradient.
- **Product rule:** `9/10` $(uv)'=u'v+uv'$. **Quotient rule:** $\left(\dfrac uv\right)'=\dfrac{u'v-uv'}{v^2}$.
- **Reciprocal of a function:** `7/10` $\dfrac k{f}=kf^{-1}$, so $\dfrac d{dx}\dfrac k{f}=-\dfrac{kf'}{f^2}$ with no quotient rule; $\dfrac1{(2x+1)^3}=(2x+1)^{-3}$ is a plain chain rule.
- **Logarithmic differentiation (products/powers):** `6/10` $y=x^x$, $y=\dfrac{(x+1)^3(x-2)^2}{x^5}$: take $\ln$ first: $\dfrac{y'}y=\dots$ (beyond spec at some levels).
- **Differentiate $a^x$:** `7/10` $a^x\ln a$.
- **Standard derivatives:** `10/10` $(\sin x)'=\cos x$, $(\cos x)'=-\sin x$, $(\tan x)'=\sec^2x$, $(\sec x)'=\sec x\tan x$, $(\operatorname{cosec}x)'=-\operatorname{cosec}x\cot x$, $(\cot x)'=-\operatorname{cosec}^2x$, $(\ln x)'=\dfrac1x$, $(e^{kx})'=ke^{kx}$.
- **Implicit differentiation:** `9/10` differentiate each term in $x$; every $y$-term gets $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ attached; collect, factor, divide. Example: $x^2+y^2=r^2\Rightarrow\dfrac{dy}{dx}=-\dfrac xy$. $xy$ → $y+x\dfrac{dy}{dx}$.
- **Parametric:** `8/10` $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{\dot y}{\dot x}$; second derivative $\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}=\dfrac{d}{dt}\left(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right)\Big/\dfrac{dx}{dt}$ (not $\ddot y/\ddot x$).
- **Inverse derivative:** `5/10` $\dfrac{dx}{dy}=\dfrac1{dy/dx}$.
- **Connected rates:** `8/10` $\dfrac{dA}{dt}=\dfrac{dA}{dr}\dfrac{dr}{dt}$. Quote the formula for the quantity first, differentiate with respect to the variable, then use the chain rule.

### Applications
- **Gradient of the inverse at a point:** `5/10` $(f^{-1})'(b)=\dfrac1{f'(a)}$ where $f(a)=b$.
- **Stationary point nature:** `7/10` $f''$; if $f''=0$ use the sign of $f'$ either side.
- **Points of inflection:** `5/10` $f''=0$ and $f''$ changes sign.
- **Newton–Raphson:** `6/10` $x_{n+1}=x_n-\dfrac{f(x_n)}{f'(x_n)}$; $\sqrt a$: $x_{n+1}=\tfrac12\left(x_n+\dfrac a{x_n}\right)$.
- **Fixed-point iteration:** `5/10` write $f(x)=0$ as $x=g(x)$; converges when $|g'(x^*)|<1$: a **staircase** diagram when $0<g'<1$, a **cobweb** when $-1<g'<0$.
- **Small changes:** `6/10` $\delta y\approx\dfrac{dy}{dx}\,\delta x$ — error propagation, percentage error: $\dfrac{\delta y}y\approx n\dfrac{\delta x}x$ for $y\propto x^n$.

## 8. Integration

### Rules of thumb
- **Reverse chain rule (inside is linear):** `9/10` $\int f(ax+b)\,dx=\dfrac1aF(ax+b)+c$. $\int e^{kx}=\dfrac{e^{kx}}k$; $\int\dfrac1{ax+b}=\dfrac1a\ln|ax+b|$; $\int\sin(kx)=-\dfrac{\cos kx}k$; $\int\cos(kx)=\dfrac{\sin kx}k$; $\int\sec^2(kx)=\dfrac{\tan kx}k$.
- **"The derivative is on top" recognition:** `9/10` $\int\dfrac{f'(x)}{f(x)}dx=\ln|f(x)|+c$; $\int f'(x)\,[f(x)]^n\,dx=\dfrac{[f(x)]^{n+1}}{n+1}+c$; $\int f'(x)e^{f(x)}dx=e^{f(x)}+c$. Spot these before reaching for substitution.
- **Trig powers:** `8/10` $\sin^2$ and $\cos^2$ via double angle; $\sin^3x=\sin x(1-\cos^2x)$ then substitute $u=\cos x$ (**odd power: peel off one factor**); $\tan^2=\sec^2-1$; $\sin x\cos x=\tfrac12\sin2x$.
- **Products of sines/cosines:** `6/10` product-to-sum formulas (see §6).

### Substitution
- Choose $u$ as the **inner function** whose derivative appears (up to a constant) elsewhere in the integrand. **Change limits** with $u$ and never go back to $x$ in a definite integral.
- **Typical substitutions:** `8/10` $\sqrt{ax+b}\to u=\sqrt{ax+b}$ (or $u^2=ax+b$); $\sqrt{1-x^2}\to x=\sin\theta$; $\dfrac1{1+x^2}\to x=\tan\theta$ so $\int=\arctan x$; $\dfrac1{\sqrt{1-x^2}}=\arcsin x$ (beyond spec at some levels).
- **Even simpler for linear-over-root:** `6/10` $\int\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{x+1}}dx$: set $u=x+1$ so $x=u-1$.

### By parts
- $\int u\,dv=uv-\int v\,du$. **LIATE** order for choosing $u$: **L**og, **I**nverse trig, **A**lgebraic, **T**rig, **E**xponential (first letter wins $u$).
- **Log alone:** `7/10` $\int\ln x\,dx=x\ln x-x+c$ ($u=\ln x$, $dv=dx$).
- **Cyclic by parts** `7/10` ($e^x\sin x$): apply twice; the original reappears; solve for it: $\int e^{ax}\sin bx\,dx=\dfrac{e^{ax}(a\sin bx-b\cos bx)}{a^2+b^2}$ and $\int e^{ax}\cos bx\,dx=\dfrac{e^{ax}(a\cos bx+b\sin bx)}{a^2+b^2}$.
- **Tabular (DI) method** `8/10` for $\int x^n e^{kx}$, $\int x^n\sin kx$: differentiate $x^n$ down to zero, integrate the other factor repeatedly, alternate signs $+,-,+,\dots$ and multiply diagonally. $\int x^2e^{x}dx=e^{x}(x^2-2x+2)+c$.
- **Check:** `5/10` differentiate your result.

### Areas, volumes, differential equations
- **Area between curves** `8/10` $\int(\text{top}-\text{bottom})\,dx$; the parabola/chord one-sixth shortcut $\dfrac{|a|(\beta-\alpha)^3}6$ (see P1).
- **Volume of revolution about the $x$-axis:** `8/10` $\pi\int y^2\,dx$; about the $y$-axis $\pi\int x^2\,dy$. **Washer:** $\pi\int(R^2-r^2)\,dx$.
- **Standard volumes:** `5/10` sphere $\tfrac43\pi r^3$, cone $\tfrac13\pi r^2h$, frustum from the two-cone difference; the volume of a cap of height $h$ on a sphere radius $r$: $\pi h^2\left(r-\dfrac h3\right)$.
- **Separable differential equations:** `8/10` $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=f(x)g(y)\Rightarrow\int\dfrac1{g(y)}dy=\int f(x)\,dx$. **Add $+c$ on one side only; use the initial condition at once.** $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=ky\Rightarrow y=Ae^{kx}$.
- **Trapezium rule** `7/10` $\tfrac h2[y_0+y_n+2\sum y_i]$ (over/under: curve bending up ⇒ over). **Simpson's rule (beyond spec):** $\dfrac h3\left[y_0+y_n+4(\text{odd})+2(\text{even})\right]$ with an **even number of strips**; exact for cubics.
- **Integral of a modulus:** `6/10` split at the zero of the inside function.
- **Symmetric limits:** `6/10` odd function over $[-a,a]$ gives $0$; even gives twice the half.
- **King's property (beyond spec):** `5/10` $\int_a^bf(x)\,dx=\int_a^bf(a+b-x)\,dx$ — often turns $\int_0^{\pi/2}\dfrac{\sin x}{\sin x+\cos x}dx$ into $\tfrac\pi4$ by adding the two forms.
- **Famous results to know:** `6/10` $\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^2x\,dx=\int_0^{\pi/2}\cos^2x\,dx=\dfrac\pi4$; $\int_0^1x^n\,dx=\dfrac1{n+1}$; $\int_0^\infty e^{-x}dx=1$.
