## 1. Data: summary and representation

- **Use the calculator for $\bar x$, $\sigma$, $\sum x$, $\sum x^2$** `10/10`. Enter data (with frequency column on). Read $\bar x$, $\sigma_x$ (population, the one S1 uses), $\sum x$, $\sum x^2$. Switch to 2-VAR for $S_{xy}$ values and the regression line directly.
- **Variance and standard deviation** `10/10`. $\sigma^2=\dfrac{\sum x^2}n-\bar x^2$ (the "mean of squares minus square of mean"). With frequencies, $\sum fx^2/\sum f-\bar x^2$. $S_{xx}=\sum x^2-\dfrac{(\sum x)^2}n=n\sigma^2$.
- **Coding** `10/10`. If $y=\dfrac{x-a}b$ then $\bar x=a+b\bar y$ and $\sigma_x=|b|\,\sigma_y$. Adding a constant changes the mean but **not** the standard deviation; multiplying scales both.
- **Combining two groups** `9/10`. Combined mean $=\dfrac{n_1\bar x_1+n_2\bar x_2}{n_1+n_2}$. For combined variance get each group's $\sum x^2=n(\sigma^2+\bar x^2)$, add them, divide by total $n$ and subtract the new mean squared.
- **Missing observation** `8/10`. Use the sum: new $\sum x=n\bar x-\text{removed}+\text{added}$; same for $\sum x^2$. Never re-enter the whole data set.
- **Grouped data** `9/10`. Use class midpoints in the calculator with frequencies. Means and variances are **estimates**. **Median by interpolation:** $Q=L+\dfrac{\text{position}-\text{cumulative frequency before}}{f}\times\text{width}$ with position $=\dfrac n2$ (median), $\dfrac n4$ ($Q_1$), $\dfrac{3n}4$ ($Q_3$). Use the class boundaries (not the labels) for $L$ and width.
- **Histograms** `10/10`. Frequency density $=\dfrac{\text{frequency}}{\text{class width}}$ and **area $\propto$ frequency**. So when one bar is known (area $=$ frequency), every other frequency is area$\times$(that scale). Bar heights from a given frequency: divide by the width.
- **Outliers** `9/10`. $x<Q_1-1.5\,\mathrm{IQR}$ or $x>Q_3+1.5\,\mathrm{IQR}$. Alternative given rule: beyond $\bar x\pm k\sigma$ ($k$ stated). Say which rule you used, and **show the numerical limits**.
- **Skewness from quartiles** `8/10`. $(Q_3-Q_2)-(Q_2-Q_1)>0$ positive skew, $<0$ negative; $0$ roughly symmetric. Mean $>$ median for positive skew. Quote the numbers.
- **Box plot** `7/10`. Five values: min, $Q_1$, $Q_2$, $Q_3$, max, with outliers marked separately (whiskers stop at the last non-outlier). To compare two distributions say something about **a measure of location and a measure of spread**, in context.
- **Stem-and-leaf** `6/10`. Always give a key ($2\,|\,3$ means $2.3$). The shape also gives the median by counting.
- **Percentiles and deciles** `6/10`: position $\dfrac{k}{100}n$; interpolate if grouped.
- **Interquartile range vs standard deviation** `6/10`: IQR resists outliers; $\sigma$ uses every value. Choose accordingly in "which measure is better" questions.
- **Mean from a frequency table with a missing value** `7/10`: set up $\bar x=\dfrac{\sum fx}{\sum f}$ with the unknown $f$ and solve the linear equation.
- **Range, mode, median checks** `5/10`. The median lies between the quartiles; the mean lies between min and max; $\sigma\le\dfrac{\text{range}}2$ (population SD $\le$ half the range).

## 2. Correlation and regression

- **Calculator 2-VAR does everything** `10/10`. Gives $a$, $b$ and $r$ directly. Still show the working lines your paper requires.
- **Formulae with $S$ notation** `10/10`:
$$S_{xx}=\sum x^2-\frac{(\sum x)^2}n,\quad S_{yy}=\sum y^2-\frac{(\sum y)^2}n,\quad S_{xy}=\sum xy-\frac{\sum x\sum y}n,$$
$$b=\frac{S_{xy}}{S_{xx}},\qquad a=\bar y-b\bar x,\qquad r=\frac{S_{xy}}{\sqrt{S_{xx}S_{yy}}}.$$
- **The line passes through $(\bar x,\bar y)$** `9/10`. So given the equation and $\bar x$, you get $\bar y$ (or the reverse) with no data. Two-point lines (e.g., the quartile points) can be found by the same logic.
- **Coding and $r$** `9/10`. Adding constants or multiplying **both** variables by positive constants **does not change $r$**. Multiplying exactly one variable by a negative constant changes the **sign** of $r$. Regression gradient scales: if $y'=cy$, $x'=dx$ then $b'=\dfrac cd\,b$.
- **Interpreting $b$ and $a$** `9/10`. "For each extra unit of $x$, $y$ increases by $b$ on average." The intercept $a$ is the predicted $y$ at $x=0$ — **only meaningful if $x=0$ is in context**.
- **Interpolation vs extrapolation** `9/10`. Predictions inside the data range are reliable (interpolation); outside are not. Always comment.
- **Correlation is not causation** `8/10`: a third factor may drive both. Quote context.
- **Which variable is $x$?** `7/10`. The **explanatory** (controlled/independent) variable is $x$. Regression of $y$ on $x$ minimises vertical distances; do not rearrange it to predict $x$ from $y$.
- **$r$ close to $\pm1$** `7/10`: strong linear; near $0$: **no linear** correlation (there may still be a curve). A scatter diagram helps.
- **Hypothesis-type comparison with critical value** `6/10` (if given tables): $|r|>$ critical value means significant.
- **Residuals** `6/10`: residual $=y-\hat y$; positive means the point is above the line. Sum of residuals is $0$.
- **Spearman rank (beyond spec)** `5/10`: $r_s=1-\dfrac{6\sum d^2}{n(n^2-1)}$ for ranks without ties.
- **$r^2$ (beyond spec)** `5/10`: the proportion of the variation in $y$ explained by the line.

## 3. Probability

- **Fill a Venn diagram from the intersection outward** `10/10`. Start with $A\cap B$, then $A$-only $=P(A)-P(A\cap B)$, $B$-only likewise, and outside $=1-P(A\cup B)$. Every probability in the question becomes a number in a region.
- **Core formulae** `10/10`:
$$P(A\cup B)=P(A)+P(B)-P(A\cap B),\quad P(A\mid B)=\frac{P(A\cap B)}{P(B)},\quad P(A\cap B)=P(A)P(B\mid A).$$
- **Independent** `10/10` $\Leftrightarrow P(A\cap B)=P(A)P(B)\Leftrightarrow P(A\mid B)=P(A)$. **Mutually exclusive** $\Leftrightarrow P(A\cap B)=0$. **A common error: mutually exclusive events with nonzero probabilities are never independent.**
- **"At least one" $=1-$ "none"** `10/10`. $P(\ge1)=1-P(\text{none})$; for independent attempts $1-(1-p)^n$.
- **Tree diagrams** `10/10`. **Multiply along branches, add across routes.** Without replacement, change the denominators on the second set of branches.
- **De Morgan** `8/10`. $P(A'\cap B')=1-P(A\cup B)$; $P(A'\cup B')=1-P(A\cap B)$; $P(A\cap B')=P(A)-P(A\cap B)$.
- **Exactly one of $A,B$** `8/10`: $P(A)+P(B)-2P(A\cap B)$.
- **Conditional probability by restricting the sample space** `9/10`: "given $B$" means zoom into $B$'s region of the Venn diagram or tree and divide by its total.
- **Bayes in one line** `8/10`: $P(A\mid B)=\dfrac{P(A)P(B\mid A)}{P(B)}$ with $P(B)$ from the tree (add the routes that give $B$).
- **Sample-space grid for two dice or two spinners** `8/10`. Count favourable cells over $36$; sums: $7$ has $6$ ways, $2$ and $12$ one way.
- **Combinations for "choose without replacement"** `8/10` (beyond spec in places): drawing $k$ of colour 1 from $a$ and $n-k$ of colour 2 from $b$: $\dfrac{\binom ak\binom b{n-k}}{\binom{a+b}n}$. Faster than a long tree.
- **Probabilities that sum to 1** `7/10`: use this to find an unknown $x$ in a table of probabilities; **each must be between 0 and 1**, so reject roots outside.
- **Finding unknowns from two conditions** `8/10`: e.g. independence gives a product equation, and the union gives a sum equation; solve the pair (quadratic).
- **Geometric-type "first success on trial $n$"** `6/10` (beyond spec): $q^{n-1}p$.
- **Expected number of successes (beyond spec)** `5/10`: $np$.
- **Binomial (beyond spec)** `7/10`: $P(X=r)=\binom nrp^rq^{n-r}$; calculator "BinomialPD/CD".
- **Probability always in $[0,1]$, so check every result** `6/10`.

## 4. Discrete random variables

- **Distribution table checks** `10/10`: $\sum p=1$; **use this to find $k$** first.
- **$E(X)=\sum xp$; $E(X^2)=\sum x^2p$; $\mathrm{Var}(X)=E(X^2)-[E(X)]^2$** `10/10`. The variance is **never negative**: if it is, you subtracted the wrong way.
- **Transformations** `10/10`: $E(aX+b)=aE(X)+b$; $\mathrm{Var}(aX+b)=a^2\mathrm{Var}(X)$; sd scales by $|a|$. The **$+b$ drops out of the variance**.
- **$E[g(X)]=\sum g(x)p(x)$** `9/10`: apply the function to each value, not to $E(X)$. **$E(X^2)\ne[E(X)]^2$.**
- **Sums of variables** `8/10`: $E(X+Y)=E(X)+E(Y)$ always; $\mathrm{Var}(X\pm Y)=\mathrm{Var}(X)+\mathrm{Var}(Y)$ **only for independent**. Both **plus**, even for $X-Y$.
- **Unknown $a,b$ in a table** `9/10`: set up simultaneous equations from $\sum p=1$ and the given $E(X)$ (or a given probability).
- **Cumulative distribution $F(x)=P(X\le x)$** `8/10`: $P(X=x)=F(x)-F(x^-)$ (difference with the previous value). $P(X>x)=1-F(x)$.
- **Symmetric distribution** `8/10`: mean is the centre, so no need to calculate $E(X)$ at all. Variance uses the squared distance from the centre.
- **Discrete uniform on $1,\dots,n$** `8/10`: $E(X)=\dfrac{n+1}2$, $\mathrm{Var}(X)=\dfrac{n^2-1}{12}$. For a fair die $E=3.5$, $\mathrm{Var}=\dfrac{35}{12}$.
- **A fair game** `7/10`: expected gain $=0$. Expected profit per play $=E(\text{winnings})-\text{stake}$.
- **Mode vs mean vs median** `6/10`: mode has the greatest probability; median is where cumulative probability crosses $0.5$.
- **Standard deviation of $aX+b$** `7/10`: $|a|\sigma$. Check units.
- **Linear function trick** `7/10`: if $Y=aX+b$ and you know $E(Y)$ and $\mathrm{Var}(Y)$, invert to get $E(X)=\dfrac{E(Y)-b}a$, $\mathrm{Var}(X)=\dfrac{\mathrm{Var}(Y)}{a^2}$.
- **Expectation of a count** `6/10`: if each trial succeeds with probability $p$ the expected number is $np$ (beyond spec).

## 5. The Normal distribution

- **Standardise** `10/10`: $Z=\dfrac{X-\mu}\sigma\sim N(0,1)$. **Sketch the bell, shade the area**, then read the table or use calculator "Normal CD".
- **Symmetry** `10/10`: $\Phi(-z)=1-\Phi(z)$; $P(Z>z)=1-\Phi(z)$; $P(|Z|<z)=2\Phi(z)-1$; $P(a<X<b)=\Phi(z_b)-\Phi(z_a)$.
- **Inverse (given probability)** `10/10`. If $P(X<a)=p$ with $p>0.5$ then $z>0$; with $p<0.5$ then $z<0$ (use $-z_{1-p}$). Memorise the **percentage-point table**:

| Upper tail | $z$ |
|---|---|
| $10\%$ | $1.2816$ |
| $5\%$ | $1.6449$ |
| $2.5\%$ | $1.9600$ |
| $1\%$ | $2.3263$ |
| $0.5\%$ | $2.5758$ |
| $25\%$ | $0.6745$ |

- **Two unknowns $\mu,\sigma$** `10/10`. Standardise both conditions to get two **linear** equations: $\dfrac{x_1-\mu}\sigma=z_1$, $\dfrac{x_2-\mu}\sigma=z_2$. Subtract: $\sigma=\dfrac{x_2-x_1}{z_2-z_1}$, then back-substitute for $\mu$. Mind the **signs of $z$**.
- **Quartiles of a normal** `8/10`: $Q_1=\mu-0.6745\sigma$, $Q_3=\mu+0.6745\sigma$, so $\mathrm{IQR}=1.349\sigma$. Handy when a question gives quartiles and asks for $\sigma$.
- **68–95–99.7 rule** `8/10`: about $68\%$ within $\mu\pm\sigma$, $95\%$ within $\pm2\sigma$, $99.7\%$ within $\pm3\sigma$ — for sanity checks and quick estimates.
- **Given $P(X>a)$ and $P(X<b)$ to find $\mu$ only** `8/10`: use the single standardised equation.
- **"Same mean, different standard deviation"** `6/10`: sketch both curves; the larger-$\sigma$ curve is wider and lower. A smaller $\sigma$ puts more probability near the mean.
- **Normal approximation to binomial (beyond spec)** `7/10`: $X\sim B(n,p)\approx N(np,\,npq)$ when $np>5$ and $nq>5$; **continuity correction**: $P(X\le k)\to P(Y<k+0.5)$, $P(X\ge k)\to P(Y>k-0.5)$.
- **Sums of independent normals (beyond spec)** `7/10`: $N(\mu_1,\sigma_1^2)\pm N(\mu_2,\sigma_2^2)$: means add or subtract, **variances always add**.
- **Probability of an exact value is $0$** `6/10`: $P(X=a)=0$, so $<$ and $\le$ are equivalent.
- **Working with proportions** `7/10`: a percentage of a population above $a$ is $P(X>a)\times100$. Number expected in a sample of $n$: $n\,P(\cdot)$.
- **Fitting a normal to data** `6/10`: take $\mu=\bar x$ and $\sigma=s$, then compare predicted vs observed.
- **Rounding** `7/10`: carry at least 4 significant figures of $z$ and only round the final answer.

## 6. Mathematical models

- **Advantages and limitations of a model** `6/10`. Typical phrases: "simplifies a complex situation", "can make predictions", "may be refined"; limitations: "assumes independence", "assumes constant probability", "ignores outliers", "works only in the range of data".
- **Assumptions for a Normal model** `6/10`: continuous, symmetric, bell-shaped, unimodal. Check by comparing mean $\approx$ median, or skewness.
- **Independence assumption (and why it may fail)** `6/10`. State **in context**.

## 7. Fast checks

- **Probability tables:** do all entries sum to 1 and each lie in $[0,1]$?
- **Variance:** never negative; units are squared.
- **Regression:** the line passes through $(\bar x,\bar y)$; sign of $b$ matches sign of $r$.
- **Normal:** a shaded-area sketch tells you whether the probability should be above or below $0.5$.
- **Combined data:** the combined mean lies between the group means, weighted by sizes.
