## 0. Habits that win marks everywhere

- **Always write units and state symbols** `9/10` in equations. Conditions (temperature, catalyst, solvent) are a **mark** in organic equations: write them over the arrow.
- **Mole bridge** `10/10`. Every calculation is: *given $\to$ moles $\to$ ratio from the balanced equation $\to$ moles of wanted $\to$ answer.* Write the mole ratio line explicitly.
- **Number of significant figures** `8/10`: match the data (usually 3). Don't round intermediate steps.
- **Command words** `8/10`: *State* — one fact; *Explain* — reason with chemistry; *Suggest* — apply knowledge to something new; *Deduce* — reasoned conclusion from information given; *Calculate* — show working with units.
- **Colours and observations** `9/10`: give the **initial** and **final** colour ("orange to green") and the **state** (white precipitate, effervescence). "Clear" is not the same as "colourless".

## 1. Moles, formulae and equations (topic 2)

- **Core formulae** `10/10`: $n=\dfrac mM$; $n=cV$ with $V$ in $\mathrm{dm}^3$ ($\mathrm{cm}^3\div1000$); gas at r.t.p. $n=\dfrac V{24.0\ \mathrm{dm}^3}$ (r.t.p. $20^\circ\mathrm C$, $1$ atm); $pV=nRT$ in SI units (**Pa, $\mathrm m^3$, K**; $\mathrm{dm}^3\to\mathrm m^3$ is $\times10^{-3}$).
- **Titration** `10/10`: $\dfrac{c_1V_1}{n_1}=\dfrac{c_2V_2}{n_2}$ with $n_1,n_2$ the **stoichiometric coefficients** — skips moles altogether. Use **concordant** titres (within $0.10\ \mathrm{cm^3}$) and average them. For a scaled-down aliquot ($25\ \mathrm{cm^3}$ from $250\ \mathrm{cm^3}$) **multiply by 10 at the end**.
- **Empirical formula** `10/10`: percentage $\to$ divide by $A_r$ $\to$ divide by the smallest. If a ratio is $x.5$ multiply all by 2; $x.33$ or $x.67$ multiply by 3; $x.25$ or $x.75$ multiply by 4.
- **Molecular formula** `9/10`: $\dfrac{M_r}{\text{empirical mass}}=k$, multiply the empirical formula by $k$ (should be a whole number).
- **Limiting reagent** `9/10`: divide each reagent's moles by its coefficient; the **smaller** value is limiting. Everything else is in excess.
- **Percentage yield and atom economy** `9/10`: yield $=\dfrac{\text{actual}}{\text{theoretical}}\times100$; atom economy $=\dfrac{M_r\text{ of desired product}}{\sum M_r\text{ of all products}}\times100$ (using the balanced equation).
- **Dilution** `8/10`: $c_1V_1=c_2V_2$. Serial dilution: multiply dilution factors.
- **Concentration conversion** `8/10`: $\mathrm{g\,dm^{-3}}=\mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}}\times M_r$. Percent by mass, ppm ($\times10^{-6}$).
- **Gas volumes, same $T$ and $p$** `9/10`: **volume ratio $=$ mole ratio** (Avogadro). Combustion of a hydrocarbon $\mathrm C_xH_y$: $V_{\mathrm{CO_2}}:V_{\mathrm{O_2\,used}}$ gives $x$ and $y$ directly: $x=\dfrac{V_{\mathrm{CO_2}}}{V_{\text{hydrocarbon}}}$, water (liquid) volume change gives $y$.
- **$M_r$ from a gas** `8/10`: $M=\dfrac{mRT}{pV}$ (SI units). Density relation $\rho=\dfrac{pM}{RT}$.
- **Combustion analysis** `8/10`: $\mathrm{CO_2}\to$ moles of C, $\mathrm{H_2O}\to$ **twice** the moles of H, oxygen by difference.
- **Average $A_r$** `9/10`: $\dfrac{\sum(\text{abundance}\times\text{mass})}{\sum\text{abundance}}$; if the abundance is given as relative peak heights, same formula.
- **Number of atoms/ions** `7/10`: $N=n\times6.02\times10^{23}$; count ions in the **formula unit** ($\mathrm{Al_2(SO_4)_3}$ has 5 ions).
- **Oxidation numbers** `10/10`: free element $0$; group 1 $+1$; group 2 $+2$; F $-1$; H $+1$ (hydrides $-1$); O $-2$ (peroxides $-1$, OF$_2$ $+2$); halogens $-1$ in halides; **sum equals the overall charge**. Redox: the species that gains electrons (oxidation number falls) is reduced and is the **oxidising agent**.
- **Balancing redox quickly** `9/10`: change in oxidation number $\times$ number of atoms $=$ electrons; equalise electrons, then O with $\mathrm{H_2O}$, H with $\mathrm H^+$, charge with $e^-$ (acid conditions).
- **Ionic equations** `9/10`: write the full equation, split the aqueous ionic compounds, cancel **spectators**.
- **Mass spectrometry** `9/10`: **M+1 peak** gives the number of carbons: $\dfrac{\text{M+1 height}}{\text{M height}}\times\dfrac{100}{1.1}=n_C$. Chlorine: $\mathrm{M:M{+}2}=3:1$; bromine $1:1$; $\mathrm{Cl_2}$: $9:6:1$; $\mathrm{Br_2}$: $1:2:1$.

## 2. Atomic structure and bonding (topics 1, 3)

- **Electron configuration** `10/10`: fill $1s,2s,2p,3s,3p,4s,3d,4p,5s,4d\dots$; **$4s$ fills first and empties first** when forming ions. Exceptions: **Cr** $[\mathrm{Ar}]3d^54s^1$ and **Cu** $[\mathrm{Ar}]3d^{10}4s^1$ (half-filled and filled $d$ are stable). Transition-metal ions lose $4s$ then $3d$.
- **Successive ionisation energies identify the group** `10/10`: a **big jump** shows the electrons being removed from an inner shell, so the number of electrons before the jump $=$ group number.
- **Ionisation energy trends** `10/10`: across a period increases (nuclear charge up, same shielding); down a group decreases (more shells, more shielding). **Dips at the start of $p$ (e.g. Al below Mg, S below P)**: for Al, the electron is in a higher-energy $3p$ subshell; for S, **paired** $3p$ electrons repel. The same logic gives dips at B/O.
- **Isoelectronic species** `7/10`: same electrons; the more protons, the smaller the radius ($\mathrm{O^{2-}}>\mathrm{F^-}>\mathrm{Na^+}>\mathrm{Mg^{2+}}>\mathrm{Al^{3+}}$).
- **Shape in seconds (VSEPR)** `10/10`. Count the **electron domains** on the central atom: $\dfrac{\text{valence electrons}+\text{atoms bonded (monovalent)}-\text{charge}}{2}$. Then:

| Domains | Lone pairs | Shape | Bond angle |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 0 | linear | $180^\circ$ |
| 3 | 0 / 1 | trigonal planar / bent | $120^\circ$ / $<120^\circ$ |
| 4 | 0 / 1 / 2 | tetrahedral / pyramidal / bent | $109.5^\circ$ / $107^\circ$ / $104.5^\circ$ |
| 5 | 0 | trigonal bipyramidal | $90^\circ,120^\circ$ |
| 6 | 0 / 2 | octahedral / square planar | $90^\circ$ |

  **Each lone pair squeezes the angle by about $2.5^\circ$** (lone pairs repel more than bonding pairs). Double bonds count as one domain. Common ones: $\mathrm{BF_3}$ trigonal planar, $\mathrm{CO_2}$ linear, $\mathrm{SO_2}$ bent, $\mathrm{NH_4^+}$ tetrahedral, $\mathrm{SF_6}$ octahedral, $\mathrm{PCl_5}$ trigonal bipyramidal, $\mathrm{XeF_4}$ square planar.
- **Polarity** `9/10`: a molecule with polar bonds is non-polar overall if its **shape is symmetric** ($\mathrm{CO_2}$, $\mathrm{CCl_4}$, $\mathrm{BF_3}$, $\mathrm{SF_6}$); polar if not ($\mathrm{H_2O}$, $\mathrm{NH_3}$, $\mathrm{CHCl_3}$).
- **Sigma and pi counting** `9/10`: single bond $=1\sigma$; double $=1\sigma+1\pi$; triple $=1\sigma+2\pi$. Ethene has $5\sigma+1\pi$; benzene has $12\sigma$ and a delocalised $\pi$ system.
- **Intermolecular force ranking** `10/10` (boiling point): hydrogen bonding (**needs H on N, O or F and a lone pair on N, O or F**) $>$ permanent dipole $>$ instantaneous dipole (London) — but **London forces grow with the number of electrons**, so a big non-polar molecule can beat a small polar one.
- **Boiling point reasoning template** `9/10`: type of force $\to$ strength $\to$ energy needed to overcome. For ionic or giant covalent say **bonds**, for simple molecules say **intermolecular forces**, never "break covalent bonds" for simple molecular melting.
- **Hydrogen bond count** `7/10`: water forms about 2 per molecule; ethanol 1 on average; carboxylic acids dimerise.
- **Ionic radius and lattice energy** `8/10`: lattice energy magnitude $\propto\dfrac{|q_+\,q_-|}{r_++r_-}$. $\mathrm{MgO}\gg\mathrm{NaCl}$ because of the $2{+}/2{-}$ charges and small ions.
- **Metallic bonding** `7/10`: attraction between cations and delocalised electrons; strength increases with ionic charge and decreasing radius (Mg $>$ Na).
- **Bond energy and length** `7/10`: higher order means shorter and stronger. $\mathrm{C{-}C}\,347$, $\mathrm{C{=}C}\,612$, $\mathrm{C{\equiv}C}\,838\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$.
- **Allotropes** `6/10`: graphite conducts (delocalised electrons between layers), diamond doesn't; graphene is a single layer.

## 3. States of matter

- **Ideal gas** `10/10`: $pV=nRT$. **Ratio form** for fixed $n$: $\dfrac{p_1V_1}{T_1}=\dfrac{p_2V_2}{T_2}$ (K). Real gases deviate at **high pressure** (molecular volume matters) and **low temperature** (intermolecular forces matter). The closest to ideal: small, non-polar ($\mathrm{He}$, $\mathrm{H_2}$).
- **Dalton's law** `9/10`: $p_i=x_iP_{\text{total}}$ where $x_i$ is the mole fraction. Total pressure is the sum of partial pressures.
- **Conversions** `8/10`: $1\ \mathrm{atm}=101\ 325\ \mathrm{Pa}$; $1\ \mathrm{dm^3}=10^{-3}\ \mathrm{m^3}$; $T/\mathrm K=\theta/^\circ\mathrm C+273$.
- **Liquid and solid properties** `6/10`: giant ionic/covalent/metallic structures — high melting points; simple molecular — low.
- **Lattice types** `6/10`: simple cubic, body-centred, face-centred; **ionic conduction needs mobile ions** (molten or aqueous).
- **Sublimation** `5/10`: $\mathrm{I_2}$, dry ice, graphite.

## 4. Energetics (topics 5 and A Level 23)

- **Bond enthalpy method** `10/10`: $\Delta H=\sum(\text{bonds broken})-\sum(\text{bonds formed})$. Count **every** bond of every molecule, including the unchanged bonds only if they change (you can cancel them).
- **Hess's law from formation data** `10/10`: $\Delta H_r=\sum\Delta H_f(\text{products})-\sum\Delta H_f(\text{reactants})$ — **elements in standard states have $\Delta H_f=0$**; multiply by the coefficients.
- **Hess's law from combustion data** `10/10`: $\Delta H_r=\sum\Delta H_c(\text{reactants})-\sum\Delta H_c(\text{products})$ (the **reverse** order to formation).
- **Cycle direction rule** `10/10` (energy-cycle triangle): $\Delta H$ along a route in the direction of an arrow is positive; against it, negative. If unsure, write $\Delta H_{\text{target}}=\Delta H_1+\Delta H_2$ along the alternative path and reverse the signs of any step taken backwards.
- **Calorimetry** `10/10`: $q=mc\Delta T$ with $m$ the mass of **water/solution** (not the solid) and $c=4.18\ \mathrm{J\,g^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$ (1 cm³ of dilute solution $\approx1\ \mathrm g$). $\Delta H=-\dfrac q{n}$ ($\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$; **negative for a temperature rise**). Divide by the **limiting** reagent's moles.
- **Calorimetry errors** `8/10`: heat loss to surroundings (give a lower $\Delta T$), incomplete combustion, evaporation of the fuel, heat absorbed by the calorimeter. **Use extrapolated cooling curves** for $\Delta T$ at the mixing time.
- **Enthalpy of neutralisation** `7/10`: about $-57\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$ for strong acid + strong base; weak acids give less (energy needed to ionise).
- **Enthalpy change of solution** `9/10`: Cambridge defines lattice energy as the **formation** of the lattice from gaseous ions (negative), so $\Delta H_{\text{sol}}=-\Delta H_{\text{latt}}+\sum\Delta H_{\text{hyd}}$. Draw the cycle (gaseous ions on top, solid and solution below) to check the signs.
- **Born–Haber cycle** `9/10`: atomisation, ionisation energy, electron affinity, lattice energy: write the cycle with arrows up for endothermic. $\Delta H_f=\Delta H_{at}(\mathrm M)+\mathrm{IE}+\tfrac12\Delta H_{at}(\mathrm X_2)+\mathrm{EA}+\Delta H_{\text{latt}}$. **Lattice energy is the unknown on the bottom line.**
- **Entropy** `9/10`: $\Delta S_{\text{sys}}$ is positive when the number of **gas** moles rises (gas $\gg$ liquid $>$ solid) or a solid dissolves. Standard entropy change $\Delta S^\circ=\sum S^\circ(\text{products})-\sum S^\circ(\text{reactants})$.
- **Gibbs** `10/10`: $\Delta G=\Delta H-T\Delta S$; **feasible if $\Delta G<0$**. Be careful with units: $\Delta H$ in kJ, $\Delta S$ in $\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}$ (divide by 1000). **Temperature at which a reaction becomes feasible:** $T=\dfrac{\Delta H}{\Delta S}$. Sign table: $(-\Delta H,+\Delta S)$ always feasible; $(+\Delta H,-\Delta S)$ never; the other two depend on $T$.
- **$\Delta G$ links** `8/10`: $\Delta G^\circ=-RT\ln K=-nFE^\circ_{\text{cell}}$ (not on the data booklet in that form). $E^\circ>0\Leftrightarrow K>1\Leftrightarrow\Delta G^\circ<0$.
- **Energy level diagrams** `7/10`: label reactants, products, $\Delta H$ (vertical arrow) and $E_a$ (from reactants to the top of the hump). Catalysts lower $E_a$ but not $\Delta H$.
- **Mean bond enthalpy limitations** `6/10`: gas-phase averages, so answers are approximate vs formation data.
- **Standard conditions** `7/10`: $298\ \mathrm K$, $100\ \mathrm{kPa}$ (or 1 bar), $1\ \mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}}$.

## 5. Electrochemistry (topic 6 and A Level 24)

- **Cell potential** `10/10`: $E^\circ_{\text{cell}}=E^\circ_{\text{(more positive)}}-E^\circ_{\text{(less positive)}}=E^\circ_{\text{reduction}}-E^\circ_{\text{oxidation}}$. The more positive electrode is the **cathode** (reduction); electrons flow from the more negative electrode through the wire.
- **Predicting reactions** `10/10`: **anticlockwise rule** — the more negative $E^\circ$ half-equation goes backwards (oxidation); the reaction is feasible if $E^\circ_{\text{cell}}>0$. Feasible does **not** mean fast (kinetics).
- **Electrolysis amounts** `10/10`: $Q=It$, moles of electrons $=\dfrac Q F$ ($F=96\ 500\ \mathrm{C\,mol^{-1}}$), then use the half-equation ratio. Mass $=\dfrac{ItM}{zF}$. **Metal ion charge $z$ equals electrons per atom.** Same current in series: moles of product proportional to $\dfrac1z$.
- **Electrolysis products** `9/10`: at the **cathode** the less reactive species gets reduced ($\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}$ before $\mathrm{H^+}$; $\mathrm H_2$ over $\mathrm{Na^+}$); at the **anode**, halide before water unless dilute (concentrated $\mathrm{NaCl}$ gives $\mathrm{Cl_2}$; dilute gives $\mathrm{O_2}$). Mnemonic: **AN OX, RED CAT**.
- **Nernst (A Level)** `9/10`: $E=E^\circ+\dfrac{0.059}{z}\lg\dfrac{[\mathrm{ox}]}{[\mathrm{red}]}$ at $298\ \mathrm K$. **A tenfold increase in the oxidised ion changes $E$ by $0.059/z\ \mathrm V$** (less for larger $z$).
- **Standard hydrogen electrode** `7/10`: $1\ \mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}}\ \mathrm H^+$, $100\ \mathrm{kPa}$ $\mathrm H_2$, $298\ \mathrm K$, platinum electrode coated in platinum black.
- **Salt bridge** `6/10`: completes the circuit by ion flow without mixing; must not react with the ions ($\mathrm{KNO_3}$ not $\mathrm{KCl}$ with $\mathrm{Ag^+}$).
- **Fuel cell, batteries** `6/10`: the hydrogen–oxygen cell: negative $\mathrm H_2+2\mathrm{OH^-}\to2\mathrm{H_2O}+2e^-$; positive $\mathrm O_2+2\mathrm{H_2O}+4e^-\to4\mathrm{OH^-}$ (alkaline).
- **Titration data and $E^\circ$** `7/10`: $\mathrm{MnO_4^-}$ is a strong oxidiser, so **no indicator** (self-indicating pink). Don't acidify with HCl (the chloride is oxidised) — use $\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$.

## 6. Equilibria (topic 7 and A Level 25)

- **$K_c$ and $K_p$** `10/10`: products over reactants raised to powers; **omit solids and pure liquids**. $K_p$ uses partial pressures $p_i=x_iP$. $K_p=K_c(RT)^{\Delta n}$ where $\Delta n=\sum\text{gas products}-\sum\text{gas reactants}$ (not on the data booklet).
- **Units of $K$** `9/10`: compute them, don't guess: $K_c$ for $\mathrm A+\mathrm B\rightleftharpoons\mathrm C$ has $\mathrm{mol^{-1}\,dm^3}$. $\Delta n=0\Rightarrow$ no units.
- **ICE tables** `10/10`: **I**nitial, **C**hange (use the stoichiometric ratio), **E**quilibrium. Fill in the known equilibrium amount first and work back.
- **Le Chatelier shortcut** `10/10`: the system shifts to oppose the change. Pressure up favours **fewer gas moles**; temperature up favours the **endothermic** direction; **only temperature changes the value of $K$**; a catalyst doesn't shift the equilibrium, only the speed.
- **Reaction quotient $Q$** `7/10`: $Q<K$ moves forward, $Q>K$ moves back.
- **pH of a strong acid** `10/10`: $\mathrm{pH}=-\lg[\mathrm H^+]$ ($[\mathrm H^+]=c$ for monoprotic, $2c$ for diprotic). **Strong base:** $[\mathrm H^+]=\dfrac{K_w}{[\mathrm{OH^-}]}$ with $K_w=1.0\times10^{-14}$; $\mathrm{pH}=14-\mathrm{pOH}$. **A tenfold dilution raises the pH of a strong acid by $1$.**
- **pH of a weak acid (the approximation)** `10/10`: $[\mathrm H^+]=\sqrt{K_ac}$, so $\mathrm{pH}=\tfrac12\left(\mathrm pK_a-\lg c\right)$. Valid when ionisation is small (check $[\mathrm H^+]\ll c$). **Weak base:** $[\mathrm{OH^-}]=\sqrt{K_bc}$. $\mathrm pK_a+\mathrm pK_b=14$ for a conjugate pair.
- **Buffers (Henderson–Hasselbalch)** `10/10`: $\mathrm{pH}=\mathrm pK_a+\lg\dfrac{[\mathrm A^-]}{[\mathrm{HA}]}$ (not on the data booklet). **At half-neutralisation $[\mathrm A^-]=[\mathrm{HA}]$ so $\mathrm{pH}=\mathrm pK_a$.** Making a buffer: weak acid + its salt, or **excess** weak acid + some strong base (moles after reaction, not initial).
- **How a buffer works** `9/10`: added $\mathrm H^+$ is removed by $\mathrm A^-$ ($\mathrm A^-+\mathrm H^+\to\mathrm{HA}$); added $\mathrm{OH^-}$ by $\mathrm{HA}$ ($\mathrm{HA}+\mathrm{OH^-}\to\mathrm A^-+\mathrm{H_2O}$). **Both** components must be present in large amounts. Capacity is greatest when $[\mathrm A^-]=[\mathrm{HA}]$.
- **Indicator choice for a titration** `9/10`: pick one whose range lies in the **vertical section**. Strong acid/strong base: any; strong acid/weak base: methyl orange; weak acid/strong base: phenolphthalein; weak/weak: none.
- **Titration curves** `8/10`: the equivalence pH is $7$ (SA/SB), $<7$ (SA/WB), $>7$ (WA/SB). **Half-equivalence point $=\mathrm pK_a$.**
- **$K_{sp}$ and solubility $s$** `10/10`: $\mathrm{AB}$: $K_{sp}=s^2$; $\mathrm{AB_2}$ (or $\mathrm A_2\mathrm B$): $K_{sp}=4s^3$; $\mathrm{AB_3}$: $27s^4$; $\mathrm A_2\mathrm B_3$: $108s^5$. **Precipitation** if the ionic product exceeds $K_{sp}$. **Common-ion effect:** the added ion fixes one concentration, so $s$ falls.
- **Solubility in complexing/acid** `6/10`: add $\mathrm{NH_3}$ to dissolve $\mathrm{AgCl}$ (complex ion removes $\mathrm{Ag^+}$); add acid to dissolve a hydroxide (neutralises $\mathrm{OH^-}$).
- **Distribution (partition) coefficient** `7/10`: $K_{pc}=\dfrac{[\mathrm X]_1}{[\mathrm X]_2}$ at a fixed temperature. **Several small extractions beat one large one.**
- **Ionic product of water** `7/10`: $K_w$ rises with temperature (pH of pure water is below 7 when hot but it stays neutral).
- **Acid strength vs concentration** `7/10`: a weak acid can be concentrated; a strong acid can be dilute. They are different properties.

## 7. Kinetics (topic 8 and A Level 26)

- **Order from initial rates** `10/10`. Compare two experiments that change **one** concentration: rate ratio $=(\text{concentration ratio})^{\text{order}}$. Double $[\mathrm A]$ and rate $\times2\Rightarrow$ order 1; $\times4\Rightarrow$ order 2; no change $\Rightarrow$ order 0.
- **Units of $k$** `10/10`: overall order $n$ gives $\mathrm{mol}^{1-n}\mathrm{dm}^{3(n-1)}\mathrm s^{-1}$. First order: $\mathrm s^{-1}$; second order: $\mathrm{mol^{-1}\,dm^3\,s^{-1}}$; zero order: $\mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}\,s^{-1}}$.
- **Half-life of a first-order reaction is constant** `10/10`: $t_{1/2}=\dfrac{\ln2}k$ (not on the data booklet) $\Rightarrow$ $k=\dfrac{0.693}{t_{1/2}}$. **If successive half-lives are equal, it is first order.** Check this on a concentration–time graph before calculating $k$.
- **Graph shapes** `10/10`: concentration vs time: straight line down (zero order), exponential decay (first order). Rate vs concentration: horizontal line (zero), straight line through the origin (first), curve (second). **The gradient of a concentration–time curve is the rate**; the initial rate is the gradient at $t=0$.
- **Rate-determining step** `10/10`: the rate equation shows the species in (or before) the slow step. **Order $=$ the number of molecules in the RDS**. A mechanism must add to the overall equation and the RDS must agree with the rate equation.
- **Arrhenius** `10/10`: $k=Ae^{-E_a/RT}$, $\ln k=\ln A-\dfrac{E_a}{RT}$. **Plot $\ln k$ against $\dfrac1T$**: gradient $=-\dfrac{E_a}R$, intercept $\ln A$. **Two temperatures:** $\ln\dfrac{k_2}{k_1}=\dfrac{E_a}R\left(\dfrac1{T_1}-\dfrac1{T_2}\right)$ — no graph needed.
- **Rule of thumb** `6/10`: for $E_a\approx50\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$ near room temperature, a $10\ \mathrm K$ rise about doubles the rate.
- **Maxwell–Boltzmann** `9/10`: the curve **starts at the origin**, the area under it stays the same; at higher $T$ the peak shifts right and **lowers** and more molecules exceed $E_a$. A catalyst lowers $E_a$ so a larger shaded fraction exceeds it (the curve doesn't change).
- **Catalysis** `8/10`: heterogeneous (surface adsorption), homogeneous (intermediate), **autocatalysis**. Explain: "lowers the activation energy by providing an alternative route".
- **Clock reactions** `8/10`: rate $\propto\dfrac1t$; plot $\dfrac1t$ against concentration to get the order (a straight line through the origin means first order).
- **Reaction profile for a two-step mechanism** `7/10`: two humps with an intermediate between; the **higher** hump is the RDS.
- **Rate vs equilibrium** `7/10`: rate laws come from experiments, not from the stoichiometry (except for an elementary step).

## 8. Periodicity, Group 2, Group 17, nitrogen and sulfur (topics 9–12)

- **Period 3 oxides** `10/10`: $\mathrm{Na_2O}$ (basic), $\mathrm{MgO}$ (basic), $\mathrm{Al_2O_3}$ (**amphoteric**), $\mathrm{SiO_2}$ (acidic, giant covalent), $\mathrm{P_4O_{10}}$ (acidic), $\mathrm{SO_2}/\mathrm{SO_3}$ (acidic). Melting points: giant structures high, simple molecular low.
- **Period 3 chlorides** `9/10`: $\mathrm{NaCl}$ (dissolves, neutral); $\mathrm{MgCl_2}$ (slightly acidic); $\mathrm{AlCl_3}$ (hydrolyses, acidic with fumes of $\mathrm{HCl}$); $\mathrm{SiCl_4}$, $\mathrm{PCl_5}$ fully hydrolysed to $\mathrm{HCl}$ and an oxo-acid (acidic, white fumes).
- **Group 2 trends down the group** `10/10`: reactivity **increases**; **hydroxide solubility increases**, **sulfate solubility decreases** (mnemonic: *"hydroxides get happy, sulfates sad"*); **thermal stability of carbonates and nitrates increases** (smaller polarising power of larger cations). **Thermal decomposition:** $\mathrm{MCO_3}\to\mathrm{MO}+\mathrm{CO_2}$; $2\mathrm{M(NO_3)_2}\to2\mathrm{MO}+4\mathrm{NO_2}+\mathrm{O_2}$ (group 1: nitrites, $2\mathrm{MNO_3}\to2\mathrm{MNO_2}+\mathrm{O_2}$, except lithium).
- **Group 2 reactions** `8/10`: with water $\mathrm M+2\mathrm{H_2O}\to\mathrm{M(OH)_2}+\mathrm{H_2}$ (Mg slow, Ba fast); oxides $+$ water $\to$ hydroxides; limewater turns milky ($\mathrm{CaCO_3}$), cloudy with $\mathrm{CO_2}$.
- **Group 17 trends down** `10/10`: **oxidising power decreases** ($\mathrm{Cl_2}>\mathrm{Br_2}>\mathrm{I_2}$); halide ion **reducing power increases** ($\mathrm{I^-}>\mathrm{Br^-}>\mathrm{Cl^-}$); boiling point rises (more electrons, stronger London forces). A halogen displaces a less reactive halide.
- **Halides + conc. $\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$** `10/10`: $\mathrm{NaCl}\to\mathrm{HCl}$ only (steamy fumes); $\mathrm{NaBr}\to\mathrm{HBr}$ then $\mathrm{Br_2}$ (brown) + $\mathrm{SO_2}$; $\mathrm{NaI}\to\mathrm{I_2}$ (purple vapour/black solid), $\mathrm{SO_2}$, $\mathrm S$, **$\mathrm{H_2S}$** (smells of bad eggs). It shows the increasing reducing power.
- **Silver halide tests** `10/10`: $\mathrm{AgNO_3}$ (after adding dilute $\mathrm{HNO_3}$ to remove carbonate): $\mathrm{AgCl}$ white (dissolves in **dilute** $\mathrm{NH_3}$), $\mathrm{AgBr}$ cream (dissolves in **concentrated** $\mathrm{NH_3}$), $\mathrm{AgI}$ yellow (insoluble).
- **Disproportionation** `9/10`: $\mathrm{Cl_2}+2\mathrm{NaOH}\to\mathrm{NaCl}+\mathrm{NaClO}+\mathrm{H_2O}$ (cold, dilute); hot: $3\mathrm{Cl_2}+6\mathrm{NaOH}\to5\mathrm{NaCl}+\mathrm{NaClO_3}+3\mathrm{H_2O}$. $\mathrm{Cl_2}+\mathrm{H_2O}\rightleftharpoons\mathrm{HCl}+\mathrm{HClO}$. **Same element oxidised and reduced.**
- **Hydrogen halides** `7/10`: thermal stability falls $\mathrm{HCl}>\mathrm{HBr}>\mathrm{HI}$ (longer, weaker bond).
- **Nitrogen** `8/10`: $\mathrm{N_2}$ is inert (triple bond $945\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$); $\mathrm{NH_3}$ base; $\mathrm{NH_4^+}+\mathrm{OH^-}\to\mathrm{NH_3}$ (gently warm: damp red litmus turns blue); **nitrate in soil/eutrophication**; **acid rain** from $\mathrm{SO_2}$ and $\mathrm{NO_x}$; catalytic converter $2\mathrm{CO}+2\mathrm{NO}\to2\mathrm{CO_2}+\mathrm{N_2}$.
- **Sulfur** `7/10`: contact process $\mathrm{V_2O_5}$, $450^\circ\mathrm C$, $1$–$2$ atm; **why**: compromise between yield and rate; $\mathrm{SO_3}$ is absorbed in $\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$ not water (fog).
- **Haber process** `8/10`: $\mathrm{Fe}$, $450^\circ\mathrm C$, $200$ atm; $\mathrm N_2+3\mathrm H_2\rightleftharpoons2\mathrm{NH_3}$ ($\Delta H<0$). **Compromise** between yield, rate and cost.

## 9. Organic chemistry (AS 13–15, A Level 16–22)

### Naming and isomerism
- **Count structural isomers systematically** `8/10`: vary the chain length first, then the branch position, then functional group position. For $\mathrm{C_5H_{12}}$ there are 3; for $\mathrm{C_6H_{14}}$ 5; for $\mathrm{C_4H_{10}O}$ alcohols 4.
- **Chiral centre** `10/10`: a carbon with **four different groups**; $n$ chiral centres $\to$ up to $2^n$ stereoisomers. Enantiomers rotate plane-polarised light in opposite directions; a **racemic mixture** has no net rotation (e.g. $\mathrm{S_N1}$ on a chiral centre, or the nucleophilic addition to a ketone).
- **E/Z (cis/trans)** `8/10`: requires a $\mathrm C{=}\mathrm C$ with **two different groups on each carbon**; use CIP priority on each carbon.
- **Degrees of unsaturation (not in syllabus, very useful)** `8/10`: $\mathrm{DBE}=\dfrac{2C+2+N-H-X}2$. $1$ means one ring or one $\mathrm{C{=}C}$ or one $\mathrm{C{=}O}$; $4$ or more suggests a benzene ring.

### Mechanisms and reactions
- **Free-radical substitution** `10/10`: **initiation** (UV breaks $\mathrm{Cl{-}Cl}$ into $\mathrm{Cl\cdot}$), **propagation** (two steps that regenerate the radical), **termination** (two radicals combine). Gives a mixture of products (further substitution), so poor yield.
- **Electrophilic addition** `10/10`: **Markovnikov** — the electrophile $\mathrm H^+$ goes to the carbon with **more** hydrogens; the more stable carbocation (tertiary $>$ secondary $>$ primary) forms the major product. $\mathrm{Br_2}$ in the dark decolourises: **bromine water test** for $\mathrm C{=}\mathrm C$.
- **Nucleophilic substitution** `10/10`: **primary** halogenoalkanes mostly $\mathrm S_N2$ (one-step, inversion, rate $=k[\mathrm{RX}][\mathrm{Nu}]$); **tertiary** mostly $\mathrm S_N1$ (carbocation intermediate, **racemic**, rate $=k[\mathrm{RX}]$); secondary: both. **Reactivity:** $\mathrm{R{-}I}>\mathrm{R{-}Br}>\mathrm{R{-}Cl}$ (weaker bond), not polarity. Test: warm with $\mathrm{AgNO_3(aq)}$ in ethanol — yellow ppt fastest for iodides.
- **Substitution vs elimination** `9/10`: **aqueous** $\mathrm{NaOH}$, warm $\to$ substitution (alcohol); **ethanolic** $\mathrm{KOH}$, heat $\to$ elimination (alkene). $\mathrm{KCN}$ in ethanol gives a nitrile (**adds a carbon**); $\mathrm{NH_3}$ in excess ethanol under pressure gives a primary amine.
- **Oxidation of alcohols** `10/10`: **primary** $\to$ aldehyde (**distil** as it forms) $\to$ carboxylic acid (**reflux** with excess oxidant); **secondary** $\to$ ketone; **tertiary** — no reaction. Acidified $\mathrm{K_2Cr_2O_7}$: orange $\to$ green. $\mathrm{[O]}$ shorthand is fine for the equation if allowed.
- **Reductions** `9/10`: $\mathrm{LiAlH_4}$ (dry ether) reduces aldehydes, ketones, carboxylic acids, esters, amides, nitriles; $\mathrm{NaBH_4}$ reduces **only aldehydes and ketones**. Catalytic hydrogenation ($\mathrm{H_2}$/Ni) reduces $\mathrm{C{=}C}$ and also aldehydes/ketones.
- **Carbonyl tests** `10/10`: **2,4-DNPH** — orange ppt for aldehydes and ketones (melting point of the ppt identifies the compound); **Tollens'** — silver mirror (aldehydes only); **Fehling's/Benedict's** — brick-red ppt (aldehydes only). **Iodoform** ($\mathrm{I_2}$/NaOH): yellow ppt for $\mathrm{CH_3C{=}O}$ or $\mathrm{CH_3CH(OH)}$ groups.
- **Nucleophilic addition to carbonyl** `9/10`: $\mathrm{HCN}$ + trace base gives a hydroxynitrile (adds a carbon; racemic from planar $\mathrm{C{=}O}$); aldehydes more reactive than ketones (less steric hindrance, more $\delta+$).
- **Carboxylic acids** `9/10`: **acid tests** — effervescence with $\mathrm{Na_2CO_3}$ ($\mathrm{CO_2}$); **esterification** acid + alcohol, conc. $\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$ catalyst, reflux (reversible); **acid chloride** with $\mathrm{SOCl_2}$/$\mathrm{PCl_5}$ — much more reactive route to esters/amides. **Hydrolysis of esters:** acid (reversible) or alkali (irreversible, gives carboxylate salt).
- **Amines and amides** `7/10`: amines are bases (lone pair on N): $\mathrm{R{-}NH_2}$ stronger base than ammonia (alkyl groups push electrons) and aniline weaker (lone pair delocalised). Amides: neutral. Amide hydrolysis needs heat.
- **Phenol** `9/10`: **more** acidic than alcohols (weaker than carboxylic acids): reacts with $\mathrm{NaOH}$ but **not** $\mathrm{Na_2CO_3}$; bromine water gives a **white precipitate** (2,4,6-tribromophenol) at room temperature without a catalyst; neutral $\mathrm{FeCl_3}$ gives **violet**.
- **Benzene** `10/10`: **electrophilic substitution** (not addition) because delocalised $\pi$ electrons are stable. Nitration: conc. $\mathrm{HNO_3}$/conc. $\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$, $<55^\circ\mathrm C$ (higher gives dinitro); halogenation: $\mathrm{Br_2}$ with $\mathrm{FeBr_3}$ or $\mathrm{AlCl_3}$; Friedel–Crafts alkylation: $\mathrm{RCl}$/$\mathrm{AlCl_3}$; acylation $\mathrm{RCOCl}$/$\mathrm{AlCl_3}$. **Directing effects:** $\mathrm{OH}$, $\mathrm{NH_2}$, alkyl are **2,4-directing and activating**; $\mathrm{NO_2}$, $\mathrm{COOH}$, $\mathrm{CHO}$ are **3-directing and deactivating**.
- **Benzene stability evidence** `7/10`: all $\mathrm{C{-}C}$ bonds are $0.140\ \mathrm{nm}$; hydrogenation enthalpy is less exothermic than three times that of cyclohexene (about $150\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$ extra stability).
- **Polymers** `9/10`: **addition** — remove the double bond, show **two bonds extending** from the repeat unit (e.g. $\left[-\mathrm{CH_2{-}CH(Cl)-}\right]_n$). **Condensation** — polyester (diol + diacid, loses $\mathrm{H_2O}$) or polyamide (diamine + diacid, or amino acid); **find the monomers by breaking the ester/amide link and adding back $\mathrm{OH}$/$\mathrm H$**. Condensation polymers are **hydrolysable**; addition polymers are not (non-biodegradable).
- **Amino acids and peptides** `8/10`: zwitterion $\mathrm{H_3N^+{-}CHR{-}COO^-}$ at the isoelectric point; in acid the $\mathrm{COO^-}$ is protonated (**cation**), in alkali the $\mathrm{NH_3^+}$ loses a proton (**anion**), so they migrate in electrophoresis. Hydrolysis of a peptide gives the amino acids.
- **Synthesis route planning** `10/10`: **work backwards** from the target; know the interconversions: alkane $\to$ halogenoalkane (radical) $\to$ alcohol/nitrile/amine ($\mathrm{S_N}$) $\to$ aldehyde/ketone/acid (oxidation) $\to$ ester/amide; alkene $\leftrightarrow$ alcohol, alkene $\to$ halogenoalkane. **Each step: reagent, conditions and name of the reaction.**
- **Safe and standard apparatus** `7/10`: reflux (prevents loss of volatile reagents), distillation (separates by boiling point), **drying with anhydrous $\mathrm{MgSO_4}$ or $\mathrm{CaCl_2}$**, separating funnel.
- **Chromatography** `6/10`: $R_f=\dfrac{\text{distance moved by spot}}{\text{distance moved by solvent front}}$; compare with standards.

## 10. Analysis (topic 22)

- **IR absorptions to know** `10/10` (wavenumber, $\mathrm{cm^{-1}}$): $\mathrm{O{-}H}$ alcohol $3200$–$3600$ (broad); $\mathrm{O{-}H}$ carboxylic acid $2500$–$3300$ (very broad, overlaps $\mathrm{C{-}H}$); $\mathrm{N{-}H}$ $3300$–$3500$; $\mathrm{C{-}H}$ $2850$–$3100$; $\mathrm{C{\equiv}N}$ $2200$–$2260$; **$\mathrm{C{=}O}$ $1680$–$1750$** (strong, sharp); $\mathrm{C{=}C}$ $1620$–$1680$; $\mathrm{C{-}O}$ $1000$–$1300$. Match the pattern of peaks, not a single one.
- **Mass spectrum fragments** `10/10`: **M** peak gives $M_r$; common fragments **$15$ ($\mathrm{CH_3}^+$), $29$ ($\mathrm{C_2H_5}^+$ or $\mathrm{CHO}^+$), $43$ ($\mathrm{C_3H_7}^+$ or $\mathrm{CH_3CO}^+$), $45$ ($\mathrm{COOH}^+$ or $\mathrm{OC_2H_5}^+$), $77$ ($\mathrm{C_6H_5}^+$)**. Loss of $18$ is water; loss of $17$ is $\mathrm{OH}$; loss of $15$ is $\mathrm{CH_3}$. The **base peak** is the tallest, not necessarily M.
- **$^{13}$C NMR** `10/10`: **number of peaks $=$ number of different carbon environments** (use symmetry). Shifts: alkyl $0$–$50$, $\mathrm{C{-}O}$ $50$–$90$, $\mathrm{C{=}C}$ / aromatic $100$–$150$, $\mathrm{C{=}O}$ $160$–$220$.
- **$^1$H NMR** `10/10`: **peaks $=$ environments; integration (area) $=$ ratio of H; splitting: $n+1$ rule** (a peak is split into $n+1$ lines by $n$ neighbouring H on adjacent carbons). **Shifts ($\delta$):** alkyl $0.9$–$1.7$; next to $\mathrm{C{=}O}$ $2.0$–$2.7$; $\mathrm{O{-}CH}$ $3.3$–$4.3$; $\mathrm{C{=}C{-}H}$ $4.5$–$6$; aromatic $6.5$–$8$; aldehyde $9.4$–$10$; $\mathrm{COOH}$ $10$–$12$; $\mathrm{OH}/\mathrm{NH}$ variable. **$\mathrm D_2\mathrm O$ shake removes the $\mathrm{OH}$ and $\mathrm{NH}$ peaks.** Reference: **TMS** at $0$ ppm.
- **Solve an unknown structure** `10/10`: (1) $M_r$ and formula (or DBE) $\to$ (2) IR for functional groups $\to$ (3) $^{13}$C count (symmetry) $\to$ (4) $^1$H integration and splitting $\to$ (5) fit fragments in the mass spectrum. Draw it and check each piece.
- **Combined integration ratios** `8/10`: the integration gives the **simplest ratio**; multiply to match the molecular formula's total H.
- **Gas–liquid chromatography** `6/10`: retention time identifies; peak area gives amount.

## 11. Inorganic tests and transition elements

- **Cation tests with $\mathrm{NaOH}$ (and excess)** `10/10`: $\mathrm{Mg^{2+}}$ white ppt, **insoluble in excess**; $\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}$ white ppt (slight); $\mathrm{Al^{3+}}$ white ppt **soluble in excess** (amphoteric); $\mathrm{Zn^{2+}}$ white ppt soluble in excess (also in ammonia); $\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}$ **pale blue** ppt, insoluble in excess $\mathrm{NaOH}$ but gives a **deep blue solution in excess $\mathrm{NH_3}$**; $\mathrm{Fe^{2+}}$ **green** ppt (turns brown on standing); $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$ **red-brown** ppt; $\mathrm{Cr^{3+}}$ **green** ppt, soluble in excess (green solution); $\mathrm{Mn^{2+}}$ off-white ppt, browning in air.
- **Anion tests** `9/10`: $\mathrm{CO_3^{2-}}$: dilute acid $\to$ $\mathrm{CO_2}$ (limewater milky); $\mathrm{SO_4^{2-}}$: $\mathrm{Ba^{2+}}$ (after acid) $\to$ white ppt; $\mathrm{Cl^-},\mathrm{Br^-},\mathrm{I^-}$: see above; $\mathrm{NO_3^-}$: warm with $\mathrm{NaOH}$ and aluminium foil $\to$ $\mathrm{NH_3}$; $\mathrm{NH_4^+}$: warm with $\mathrm{NaOH}$ $\to$ $\mathrm{NH_3}$ (red litmus blue).
- **Gas tests** `9/10`: $\mathrm{H_2}$ — squeaky pop; $\mathrm{O_2}$ — relights a glowing splint; $\mathrm{CO_2}$ — limewater milky; $\mathrm{NH_3}$ — damp red litmus blue; $\mathrm{Cl_2}$ — damp blue litmus red then bleaches; $\mathrm{SO_2}$ — acidified dichromate orange to green; $\mathrm{HCl}$ — white fumes with $\mathrm{NH_3}$.
- **Transition elements** `9/10`: **variable oxidation states**, **coloured compounds** (d–d transitions, colour depends on the ligand and oxidation state), **catalysts**, **complex ions**. Colours: $\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}$ blue, $\mathrm{Fe^{2+}}$ pale green, $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$ yellow-brown, $\mathrm{Mn^{2+}}$ pale pink, $\mathrm{Cr^{3+}}$ green, $\mathrm{Co^{2+}}$ pink, $\mathrm{Cr_2O_7^{2-}}$ orange, $\mathrm{MnO_4^-}$ purple.
- **Ligand exchange** `8/10`: $\mathrm{[Cu(H_2O)_6]^{2+}}\to[\mathrm{Cu(NH_3)_4(H_2O)_2}]^{2+}$ (deep blue); $\to[\mathrm{CuCl_4}]^{2-}$ (yellow-green) with conc. $\mathrm{HCl}$. **Co-ordination number:** 6 octahedral, 4 tetrahedral or square planar, 2 linear ($\mathrm{[Ag(NH_3)_2]^+}$). Charge of the complex $=$ metal charge $+$ ligand charges.
- **Colour from $\Delta E$** `7/10`: the complex absorbs one colour and shows the complement; $\Delta E=hf$.
- **Redox titrations** `10/10`: $\mathrm{MnO_4^-}:\mathrm{Fe^{2+}}=1:5$; $\mathrm{MnO_4^-}:\mathrm{C_2O_4^{2-}}=2:5$; $\mathrm{MnO_4^-}:\mathrm{H_2O_2}=2:5$; $\mathrm{Cr_2O_7^{2-}}:\mathrm{Fe^{2+}}=1:6$; $\mathrm{I_2}:\mathrm{S_2O_3^{2-}}=1:2$. **Iodine–thiosulfate:** add starch near the end (blue-black $\to$ colourless). **Endpoint of permanganate:** first permanent faint pink.
- **Ligand substitution and stability** `6/10`: bidentate ligands (**edta**, en) give **more stable** complexes (entropy gain from releasing more small molecules).
- **Haemoglobin / Cisplatin facts** `5/10`: $\mathrm{CO}$ binds more strongly to $\mathrm{Fe^{2+}}$ than $\mathrm{O_2}$ (toxic); cisplatin binds to DNA (square planar $\mathrm{Pt(II)}$).

## 12. Practical-paper shortcuts

- **Reading a burette** `8/10`: to $\pm0.05\ \mathrm{cm^3}$, give to 2 d.p. (ending in 0 or 5). Percentage error $=\dfrac{\text{uncertainty}}{\text{reading}}\times100$; for a titre, **two readings** so double the burette uncertainty.
- **Reduce uncertainty** `8/10`: use a larger amount, a more precise apparatus, repeat and average.
- **Planning and evaluation** `8/10`: state the **independent**, **dependent** and **control** variables; give a safe method with quantities; **at least five values** in a range; repeat; draw the graph with a line of best fit.
- **Graph to constant** `8/10`: rearrange to $y=mx+c$ first, then read the gradient with a big triangle.
- **Common errors in "improvements"** `7/10`: avoid "be more careful" or "human error"; instead name a specific change (use a gas syringe instead of an inverted cylinder, insulate the calorimeter, use a pipette instead of a measuring cylinder).

## 13. Fast checks

- **Mole balance** — do atoms and charge match on both sides?
- **Orders of magnitude** — $K_a$ values, enthalpies in kJ, not J.
- **Sign** — exothermic means negative $\Delta H$; a spontaneous $\Delta G<0$.
- **Units** — $\mathrm{dm^3}$ vs $\mathrm{cm^3}$, kJ vs J, K vs $^\circ\mathrm C$.
- **Does the answer make chemical sense?** (A pH of $15$ or a negative concentration doesn't.)
