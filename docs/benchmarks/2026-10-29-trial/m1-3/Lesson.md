> [!quote] You
> Can you show me an example where a ball is thrown straight up and I need the time it takes to come back down?

> [!abstract] Worked example: Vertical motion under gravity
> **A ball is thrown vertically upwards at $14\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ from the ground. Find the greatest height and the time before it returns.**
>
> - Take upwards as positive: $u=14$, $a=-9.8$.
>
> - At the top $v=0$: $0=14^2-2\times9.8\,s$, so $s=10\,\mathrm{m}$.
>
> - Back at the ground $s=0$: $0=14t-4.9t^2$, so $t=\dfrac{14}{4.9}=2.86\,\mathrm{s}$ (3 s.f.).

> [!abstract] Helpful term
> **Acceleration due to gravity:** Constant downward acceleration of a freely moving particle; use $9.8$ unless told otherwise.
>
> Written: $g=9.8\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$
>
> Said: g equals nine point eight metres per second squared

> [!abstract] How it connects
> These equations and graphs describe the same motion: a constant velocity–time gradient is the acceleration, and the area under that line produces $s=ut+\tfrac12at^2$. If the line crosses the time axis, split the journey there to calculate distance.
